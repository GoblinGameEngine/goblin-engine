Construction Date : 1917
Architecture Style: Foursquare Box
Physical History : Real estate speculator Joseph J. Nagle platted a subdivi-
sion of Lot 2 of Randall's Subdivision in May 1914. This land on the lower west
side of Southern Avenue had previously been the site of Job Randall's sawmill.
In September 1917 John and Katherine Donahue purchased Lots 3, 4 and 5 from
Nagle (acquiring vacant Lot 6 in 1944) and immediately built this two-story
frame house. The Donahues were long-standing occupants of the house. After
John's death in January 1954, Katherine continued to reside here until the
mid-1960s. Over this extended occupation, the house has undergone only minor
exterior additions.

Significance:       Whether it was prefabricated and purchased for $150O-$2500
through a catalog or was built on-site by a local contractor using a standard-
ized design, the Donahue House looks like countless other modest patternbook
residences constructed across Iowa and the country during the 1910s and 1920s.
A foursquare box, even the small degree of ostentation presented by the con-
crete front porch cannot disguise this common workingman's house type.
