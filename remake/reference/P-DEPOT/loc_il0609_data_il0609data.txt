When the Chicago, Rock Island & Pacific Railroad reached Seneca
in 1853, a small timber-frame freight and passenger station was
built to serve the town. The present passenger station was
constructed in 1911-12 after a fire in 1911 destroyed the
original structure.   One of the few timber-frame passenger
depots in the canal corridor (most are of brick and stone
construction), this building has been abandoned for many years.
The Seneca depot is a one-story building measuring approximately
50' x 17'. The exterior is covered with asbestos shingles which
were probably placed over the original wood clapboard siding.
The large hipped roof also contains asphaltic shingles. The
windows and doors are boarded over.
