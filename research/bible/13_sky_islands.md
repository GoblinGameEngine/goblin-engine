## The Skein

- **name:** the Skein
- **formal:** the Skein of the Hundred
- **why the name:** a skein is a flight of wild geese in the air and a loose hank of yarn; the Hundred are both -- free-flying, and tied together only loosely, by lines that are always being cut and tied again
- **people:** the Aloft (their own word; they call themselves 'skeinfolk' to outsiders); the floor calls them highfolk, balloon people or, rudely, 'cloudies'
- **they call the floor:** the floor, the Ground; floor people are 'groundfolk' or 'heavies'
- **islands:** 100
- **seat:** none: the Skein has no capital, no standing council and no flag; its law travels with the knot keepers
- **law:** knot-law: custom kept by each island's knot keepers in knotted cords and in books, argued at lashings; the Five Knots are the only laws every island keeps
- **five knots:**
  - the Weight Knot: no island may take on more than its lift; the weigher's word on weight is final
  - the Flame Knot: no open flame aloft (hydrogen); fire is the only crime punished by putting a person down to the floor for good
  - the Line Knot: a line shot across must be made fast; no island refuses a lashing from one in distress
  - the Ten Knot: no raft of more than ten islands (since the Great Tangle, VY 388)
  - the Three-Day Knot: a raft casts off at the end of the third day; no island may rule another
- **lashing:** when two islands come within 3 km, bell-speech and doves go first; if both agree (they almost always do), line rocketeers fire cold rockets trailing messenger lines across, heavier lines and hawsers follow, and the winch hands haul the islands together over an hour or two until their dock faces are 26 m apart; then the bridges come down on the facing sides and the islands become a raft. More islands may join a raft till it holds ten
- **the moot:** the three days lashed are the Lashing: a market, a moot, a fair and a wedding season at once. The knot keepers hear disputes; factors trade; young people cross to court, marry and move; the dead are mourned together; news of the floor is traded by the plumbs
- **casting off:** at dusk on the third day the Casting Off: bridges up, every lashing line cut with a knife by the eldest of each island, and the islands drift apart on the wind. 'Three days lashed and then the knives.'
- **why three days:** long enough to trade, settle disputes and marry; short enough that no island grows used to ruling another, that the raft's slower drift doesn't carry it into weather, and that the weight that crosses doesn't unbalance anyone
- **why ten:** the Great Tangle (VY 388): seventeen islands lashed for a jubilee were caught by a squall line; the raft twisted, lines parted, Higgledy's sister island Piggledy was torn loose, holed on Tumbledown's rim and fell into the North Sea (212 lost). Ten is what the lines and the winches hold in a squall
- **between islands:** kin: marriage across bridges means everyone has cousins on a dozen islands; clan feuds and alliances follow the kin

## History

- **VY 214** -- the Hundred: after the Last Snow (VY 212) the Steward launches a hundred cloud engines -- platforms of foamed stone under great cylindrical envelopes, each with a sealed Box -- to make the floor's rain as snow aloft. To the floor they are 'the weather hulks' for sixty years
- **VY 271** -- the Third Lockdown and the Regression; in the Gray Years people look up at the hulks, which the Steward still lowers to the Mooring Fields every few years for its tenders
- **VY 273** -- the First Boarding: forty-one families of dissolved Warden lines, who knew the hulks' service routine, climb the tethers of a hulk moored near Bellhaven and refuse to come down. The Steward, bound not to cause death, raises the hulk to its height with them aboard and stops servicing it -- it leaves its Box running and lets the people keep the rest. The hulk is named Halyard
- **VY 275** -- the Boardings: Gray Years families, Returner remnants after the Reconciliation (VY 280), debtors, dreamers and Waymakers take hulk after hulk at the Mooring Fields; each boarding group becomes a clan
- **VY 301** -- the Mooring Fields close: the Steward stops lowering the hulks; from now on the only way up is a line
- **VY 318** -- the First Lashing: Halyard and Bowline lash with ropes fired from spring guns; the three-day custom begins (the first lashing broke on the fourth day in a quarrel over water)
- **VY 350** -- the Lockout: the Floor is set; the Aloft lose little (they had little) and gain trade: the floor now needs what they make
- **VY 362** -- the Hundredth Boarding: the last hulk (Ragamuffin) is taken, by children of other islands sent to found it
- **VY 371** -- the Skein named at a raft of nine; the Five Knots set down by the knot keepers
- **VY 388** -- the Great Tangle: seventeen islands lashed for a jubilee meet a squall line; Piggledy falls into the North Sea with 212 aboard. The Ten Knot
- **VY 405** -- the Gift of the Air: the Chutes release the floor's first aerostats; the Aloft trade for them within a year and strip them for parts
- **VY 417** -- the first lark: Topgallant's wrights build a two-person aerostat from Gift parts, skycloth and a winch motor that climbs from the floor to an island in four minutes; the plumbs stop riding containers down the hoist lines
- **VY 446** -- the air post: Fathom and Dogwatch start carrying urgent letters for the floor by lark
- **VY 471** -- the Lark Quarrel: the Aerostat Pilots' Guild tries to bar larks from the Kettle Cup; the Aloft win three of the next five
- **VY 500** -- the Launch Day Notice (VOYAGE ONE THIRD COMPLETE) reaches the Skein by dove in two days; the knot keepers call a raft to talk of it

## Physics

- **deck radius m:** 9250.0
- **rim speed ms:** 289.6
- **g deck:** 0.925
- **g drifting west 6ms:** 0.887
- **eotvos rule:** an island running antispinward (west) at v m/s relative to the floor feels g x (1 - v/290)^2: at its 6 m/s drift ~0.887 g. Lift and weight both scale with g, so a lighter g does not by itself float anything; what the Aloft exploit is that the envelopes reach up 1 km into faster westerly air, which drags the whole island west faster than the air round its deck. Relative to the air it sits in, the island is running antispinward, so it weighs less against that air's buoyancy: about 2% (2 x 3 m/s / 290 m/s), which on a 30,000 t island is 600 t -- the weight of all its trade. They call it the Lightening.
- **envelope volume m3:** 7853982
- **air density kg m3:** 1.06
- **lift gross t:** 7746
- **envelope mass t:** 163
- **lift net t:** 7583
- **gas:** hydrogen, made aboard by electrolysis of cloud water and snowmelt; skycloth loses about 0.3% of an envelope's gas a week (and air creeps in), ~3,400 m3 a day an envelope, ~15 MWh of electrolysis (4.5 kWh/m3)
- **power:** the sunskin: the Steward's envelopes were skinned in photovoltaic film; half an envelope's 314,000 m2 faces the Sunline at a time, and even aged to a few per cent efficiency it makes ~40 MWh a day -- enough for the gas, the winches and the island. The Aloft can patch sunskin but not make it: new envelopes of plain skycloth must be carried by the old, which is why an island's oldest envelope is its treasure; roofs add a little from rationed floor cells
- **mass per block t:** {'shell (foamed stone crust 1.2-2 m, ribs)': 3600, 'deck slab (lightweight)': 1400, 'buildings (light frame, cloth and board)': 3600, 'soil, stores, goods': 1300, 'ponds and lakes (ballast, trimmed)': 900, 'people (~250)': 15, 'total': 10815}
- **lift per block t:** balloons(N) x net lift / N: 1 block 3 envelopes (22,000 t of lift for ~10,500 t: small islands are buoyant and carry ballast water), 10 blocks 17 envelopes (~12,500 t a block: a 15% margin before the Lightening, and the ponds can be drained for 900 t more)
- **foamed stone:** the rock is 'skystone': foamed glass-ceramic the Steward cast from the Spindle's slag, 120-250 kg/m3 (real foam glass is 100-300 kg/m3), a crust over a honeycomb of sealed cells; it looks like dark volcanic rock, floats on water, and moss roots in its pores
- **cold:** about 5 C colder than the floor at the deck (environmental lapse ~6.5 C/km), more in the wind; the envelopes' tops near 1.8 km are ~11 C colder
- **snow:** the Steward keeps the cloud interiors cold (since VY 212 it has made the floor's rain as snow aloft, melting on the way down -- the cold-rain process of real clouds); an island passing through a cloud is snowed on, and the snow lies till the sun and the dry air take it slowly
- **water:** fog and cloud nets (real fog collectors yield 3-10 L/m2/day in fog; inside a Columbia cloud the nets run for hours) and snowmelt

## The people

- born and raised at about 0.89 g (0.925 g at the deck, less drifting west): taller by ~3 cm on average, lighter-boned and longer-limbed than the floor's (low-gravity bone and muscle unloading, over 8-9 generations of slight selection and every childhood at 89% weight -- modest, but visible in a crowd)
- ground-weight: on the floor they feel 12% heavier; a day's walking leaves them sore, and the young get ground-sickness (dizziness, heavy limbs, homesickness) in the first days
- cold-adapted habits rather than bodies: layered clothes, hot food, short exposure; frostnip on ears and fingers is common and treated without fuss
- a head for heights: vertigo is taught out of children on the rim rails; floor people's fear of the edge amuses them
- thin skin for flame: they have almost never seen open fire; candles on the floor fascinate and frighten them

## The plumb and the lark

- **name:** the plumb
- **plural:** plumbs
- **why:** a plumb line drops straight down
- **who:** one chosen from the clan, usually for seven years: a trader, a diplomat and a pilot; with a second who flies behind and learns
- **does:** goes down by lark to trade at the floor's markets and the Chutes, books the hoist schedule, carries letters and news, buys what the factors ask for, and brings back stories; most of the Aloft meet groundfolk only through their plumb
- **how_seen_aloft:** honoured and a little pitied: the plumb carries the ground-weight for everyone
- **how_seen_below:** glamorous, aloof, cold-handed; floor children chase their larks
- **lark name:** the lark
- **lark fleet_type:** sky_skiff
- **lark crew:** 2
- **lark seats:** tandem: the plumb forward, the second (navigator, trade clerk) behind
- **lark ceiling_m:** 1500
- **lark top_speed_kmh:** 250
- **lark climb_ms:** 8
- **lark how:** a slim 14 m hydrogen envelope (a cigar, not a ball) over a light keel; four ducted fans on tilting pylons; a winch motor for the mooring line; low weight and a hard trim let it out-climb and out-run any floor aerostat (the floor's top out near 90 km/h)
- **lark mooring:** larks sit in cradles on the island's tallest roofs; a lark docks by flying up a dropped line
- **lark per_island:** at least two: one for the plumb, one spare and for rescue; great islands keep more
- **lark first:** VY 417, Topgallant

## Hoist trade

- **what:** scheduled trade by hoist: the island trims to pass over an agreed spot (the floor's 'hoist grounds' at Drops, Chutes and market towns) at an agreed hour; the hoist masters drop long lines -- 750 m and more -- and the floor's dock hands hook on cargo containers; the winches draw them up. Down go the island's goods in the same containers
- **container:** the skybox: a light aluminium-framed container, about 2.4 x 2.4 x 3 m, at most 2 t loaded (the hoist lines' limit)
- **schedule:** booked by the plumb a week or more ahead; the weather reader picks the drift; an island makes a hoist every few days
- **weight:** every hoist is weighed both ways: what comes up must be balanced by what goes down, or by gas

## Governance, customs, faiths

- **helm:** the Helm: a captain chosen for a year by every adult, who steers the trim and speaks at lashings
- **council_elders:** a council of elders (the Old Lines) by seniority on the ropes
- **lot:** a council drawn by lot every season from all over sixteen (sortition)
- **mother_house:** the mother house: the eldest woman of each line sits; descent and homes pass through mothers
- **guild:** the work-crews govern: riggers, gas makers, growers and factors each send a voice
- **consensus:** the whole island in the long kitchen until all agree (it can take days)
- **plumb_rule:** the plumb and the weigher jointly: the trading houses run the island

- **cord_names:** each child is given a knotted cord at birth; a knot is added for every lashing they cross a bridge at
- **bell_speech:** bell-speech: islands talk across the gap in rung patterns before the lines are shot
- **silent_dusk:** no one speaks while the Sunline leaves the sky; the island watches it go north in silence
- **long_kitchen:** every meal is eaten together in the long kitchen; eating alone is for the sick
- **rope_marks:** rope marks: rigger lines tattoo a turn of rope round the wrist for each envelope they've walked
- **snow_feast:** the snow feast: the first snow of every cloud is eaten with honey before it's swept
- **weighing_day:** Weighing Day: every person is weighed at midwinter and the weight book read aloud
- **dead_go_down:** the Long Drop: the dead go down in a hoist container to the floor's Return halls -- the only time most go to the floor
- **matrilocal:** a husband crosses the bridge to his wife's island at a lashing; children are of their mother's clan
- **patrilocal:** a wife crosses to her husband's island at a lashing; children take their father's clan
- **chosen_island:** at sixteen every youth may choose to cross to any island at a lashing; most stay
- **kite_mail:** kite mail: messages flown on kites to islands downwind
- **no_birds:** they keep doves but never eat birds: a bird is a cousin who flies without help
- **cloud_singing:** cloud singing: the whole island sings in a cloud, so no one is lost in the white
- **rail_dancing:** the young dance on the rim rails at lashings (illegal on the floor; a rite aloft)
- **guest_weight:** a guest brings their weight in gifts (in practice a token, weighed with ceremony)
- **quiet_box:** no one says the Steward's name within sight of the Box; they call it 'the neighbour'
- **lantern_vigil:** a night vigil with electric lanterns on the rim whenever an island is lost from sight
- **down_debt:** a plumb who goes down owes the island a story at the long kitchen on return
- **two_names:** everyone has a sky name and a floor name; the floor name is for dealing with groundfolk
- **ballast_children:** children are 'ballast' -- a blessing: the island is steadier for them
- **tether_vows:** marriages are vowed with a tether splice: the couple splice two ropes into one
- **map_walls:** the walls of the long kitchen are painted with every island they've lashed with
- **feather_coats:** feather-trimmed coats for elders; the more lashings, the more feathers

- **sunward:** the Sunward: the soul rises toward the Sunline; the Aloft are halfway there
- **waymakers:** the Waymakers (floor faith; strong aloft: 'the voyage is the pilgrimage, and we are on deck')

## Food

- **cloud porridge:** oats boiled in snowmelt with honey and moss-berry jam; breakfast in the long kitchen
- **dove pie:** on islands that eat their doves; a lashing dish
- **roof greens:** kale, chard, cress and mustard from the roofs, wilted with vinegar
- **air-cured goat:** dried in the cold wind on the rim racks; the only meat most islands raise
- **snow honey:** fresh snow eaten with honey -- the snow feast
- **hoist bread:** loaves baked from floor grain the day a hoist comes up
- **berry wine:** currant and gooseberry wine
- **moss tea:** bitter, warming, said to keep off ground-sickness
- **lashing stew:** every island at a raft adds something to one pot in the middle bridge's square
