# Bank Branch

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: All tiers — rural villages typically have one small bank (sometimes shared/branch of a county bank); small towns have 1-3; small cities have several plus a main office.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 7-18m wide street frontage x 12-19m deep (24-60ft x 40-63ft); larger examples up to 18m x 20m in small cities. Often a corner lot for prestige/visibility.
- **Typical story count**: 1-2 stories. Two-story examples put offices/safe-deposit or a lodge hall upstairs.
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Masonry construction (brick, stone, or classical-revival stone facade) signaling solidity — columns, pediments common even on small-town examples. Large arched or rectangular windows, fewer/smaller than retail storefronts. Prominent formal entrance, sometimes with steps.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Public lobby/banking room**: 6-10m x 8-12m (historic example ~22ft x 32ft banking room)
- **Teller line**: 1.5-2m deep counter run along one side/end of lobby, 4-8m long
- **Vault**: 2-2.5m x 4-4.5m (historic example ~7ft x 14ft interior)
- **Private offices (loan officer, manager)**: 3m x 3.5m each (1-3 rooms)
- **Safe-deposit box room (small towns/cities)**: 3m x 4m, vault-adjacent
- **Back office/bookkeeping**: 4m x 5m
- **Restroom**: 1.5m x 2m

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Lobby**: standing desks/writing counters (1-2), waiting chairs/bench (1 set), velvet rope stanchions (0-2), decorative clock (1), potted plants (0-2).
- **Teller line**: teller windows/cages (2-5), cash drawers, marble or wood counter, brass grillwork (historic variant).
- **Vault**: circular vault door (1, ~2m diameter), steel shelving/cash storage, safe-deposit boxes (wall of small doors, 50-300, small towns/cities).
- **Private offices**: desk, chairs (2-3), filing cabinet, small safe.
- **Back office**: bookkeeping desks (2-4), ledger/file cabinets (3-5), adding machine/typewriter.

## Placement Notes
- Vault is always placed at the rear or a windowless interior core (structural/security convention), never against an exterior wall accessible from an alley.
- Teller line separates the public lobby from the back-office/vault zone — a hard visual and access boundary.
- Corner-lot placement is preferred when the generator is choosing lots in the downtown zone; grants two street-facing facades.
- Rural-village tier: reduce to a single small room (lobby + 1-2 teller windows + small vault) — no private offices or safe-deposit room.
