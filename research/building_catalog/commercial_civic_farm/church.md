# Small Church

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: All tiers — even the smallest rural villages typically support 1-3 churches of different denominations; small towns/cities have proportionally more, plus larger "consolidated" congregations. Generate multiple churches per settlement (Methodist, Baptist, Lutheran, Catholic, non-denominational are common Midwestern denominational spreads) sharing this template with different exterior/interior bias.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: Small (village-tier, ~80-100 seat): 10-14m x 18-24m. Medium (town-tier, ~150-250 seat): 14-18m x 24-32m. Larger (city-tier, ~300+ seat): 18-24m x 32-45m. Rule of thumb used for sizing: ~2.8 sq m (30 sq ft) of total building area per seat.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story sanctuary volume (tall, 6-10m ceiling) with a basement fellowship hall very common in the Midwest (half-buried daylight basement), occasionally a small bell tower/steeple as a vertical landmark element.
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Wood-frame with clapboard siding (rural/village), or brick/stone (town/city, especially Catholic and Lutheran examples). Steeple or bell tower on the street-facing gable end. Pointed-arch (Gothic Revival) or round-arch windows common; stained glass in larger/older examples.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Narthex/entry vestibule**: 3-5m x 4-6m, street-facing buffer
- **Nave/sanctuary**: 8-14m x 14-22m, seats 80-400
- **Chancel/altar area**: 3-5m deep strip at the far end of the nave from the entrance
- **Sacristy/robing room**: 2.5m x 3m, chancel-adjacent (more prominent in Catholic/Episcopal/Lutheran variants)
- **Pastor's study/office**: 3m x 3.5m
- **Fellowship hall (often basement)**: matches or exceeds nave footprint, 8-14m x 12-20m
- **Kitchen (fellowship-hall adjacent)**: 4m x 5m
- **Sunday school/classrooms**: 3-4m x 4-5m each, 2-6 rooms depending on tier
- **Choir loft/area (optional)**: 3m x 5-8m, rear-elevated or chancel-side

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Narthex**: coat racks/hooks (1-2), bulletin/literature table (1), guest book stand (1), small bench (0-1).
- **Nave**: pews (10-30 rows, split by center aisle), altar rail (0-1, liturgical denominations), lectern/pulpit (1-2), baptismal font (0-1), hymnal racks (per pew), stained-glass windows (as wall features, count varies).
- **Chancel/altar**: altar or communion table (1), cross/crucifix (1), candlesticks (2-6), pulpit or ambo (1), organ or piano (1).
- **Sacristy**: vestment cabinet, small sink, storage shelving.
- **Pastor's study**: desk, bookshelf, small meeting chairs (2).
- **Fellowship hall**: long folding tables (6-16), stacking chairs (60-200), small stage/podium (0-1).
- **Kitchen**: institutional range/oven, large coffee urns, serving counter, cabinets, industrial dishwasher (small city tier).
- **Sunday school rooms**: small tables/chairs, bookshelf, chalkboard/whiteboard.

## Placement Notes
- Sanctuary is always oriented with the altar/chancel wall opposite the entrance; narthex is the street-facing entry buffer that keeps direct sightlines from the street into the nave broken.
- Fellowship hall and kitchen are typically in the basement level (Midwest convention) with a separate exterior entrance for use when the sanctuary is closed (funeral luncheons, community meetings, election-day polling).
- Denomination variation to apply during generation: Catholic/Lutheran/Episcopal instances get a more prominent chancel + sacristy + altar rail + higher pew count relative to footprint; Baptist/Methodist/non-denominational instances favor a larger fellowship hall and classroom count relative to sanctuary size, with a simpler platform instead of a raised chancel.
- Steeple/bell tower is a non-interior landmark element — flag it for exterior silhouette generation, not floor space.
