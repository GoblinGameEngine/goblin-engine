# Bar / Tavern

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: All tiers — rural village usually has exactly one; small towns/cities have several, sometimes with a distinct "supper club" variant in the upper Midwest.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 6-10m wide x 12-20m deep (20-35ft x 40-65ft).
- **Typical story count**: 1 story, occasionally 2 (upstairs = pool hall, lodge, or apartment).
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Brick storefront, often minimal/blacked-out windows (neon beer signs substitute for display), a solid door rather than glazed. Corner taverns common on residential-fringe corner lots as well as downtown.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Main bar room**: 6-9m x 10-16m
- **Back room (pool table/storage overflow)**: 4-5m x 5-6m (optional, small town/city)
- **Kitchen (small, bar-food only, optional)**: 3m x 4m
- **Restrooms (2, men's/women's)**: 1.5m x 2m each
- **Cellar/keg storage**: footprint of main bar room, half-height, or a small 3m x 3m room

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Main bar room**:
  - Bar counter with backbar/shelving (1, 4-8m long)
  - Bar stools (6-14)
  - Booths/tables (3-8)
  - Beer taps (2-6)
  - Neon signage (2-5)
  - Jukebox (0-1)
  - Dartboard/pool table (0-2)
  - TV over bar (0-1, more common in later-era instances)
- **Back room**: pool table (0-1), extra tables (2-4), storage shelving.
- **Kitchen**: fryer, griddle, small prep counter, reach-in cooler.
- **Cellar/keg storage**: keg racks (4-10 kegs), CO2 tanks, liquor case storage.

## Placement Notes
- Bar counter is always placed opposite or perpendicular to the entrance, giving the bartender sightline to the door.
- Windows are minimized or covered — this archetype should generate with less street glazing than retail neighbors.
- Cellar/keg storage connects to a rear or side delivery point (keg deliveries), matching alley access.
- Corner-lot placement is common for neighborhood-edge taverns near residential zones, not just downtown core.
