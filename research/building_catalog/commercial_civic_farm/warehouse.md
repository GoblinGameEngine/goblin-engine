# Warehouse

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: Small town and small city, generally near the rail line/industrial fringe alongside the grain elevator and any small factory. Rural villages typically lack a dedicated warehouse (goods storage folds into the general store/elevator).
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 15-25m wide x 30-60m deep — long, simple rectangular volume to maximize storage bays and rail/truck dock frontage.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story (occasionally 2 in older brick mill-style examples with freight elevator).
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Brick or corrugated-metal siding, minimal windows (high clerestory strip windows for daylight only), large loading docks along one long side, rail spur access common in small-city examples.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Main storage floor**: open volume, 12-20m x 25-50m
- **Loading dock bays**: 3-4m x 4m per bay, 2-6 bays along one wall
- **Office**: 4m x 5m, near the main entrance
- **Break room**: 3m x 4m
- **Restroom**: 1.5m x 2m
- **Freight elevator shaft (2-story examples)**: 2m x 2.5m

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Main storage floor**: pallet racks (10-30 runs), forklift(s) (1-2), stacked crates/pallets, overhead crane/hoist track (0-1, older industrial examples), loading dock levelers.
- **Office**: desk(s) (1-3), filing cabinets, inventory clipboard station, small counter for drivers checking in.
- **Break room**: table/chairs, vending machine (0-1, small city tier), lockers.

## Placement Notes
- Loading docks always line the rail-spur or alley-facing long wall; the opposite wall is typically solid/windowless.
- Office sits at the "front" corner nearest the public road, separate from the dock/truck-access side.
- Best sited in the light-industrial fringe zone alongside the grain elevator and factory/mill archetypes rather than downtown retail core.
