# Pole Building / Machine Shed

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: Farm zone, all settlement tiers — the most common modern farm outbuilding, often newer than the barn on a given farmstead and used for equipment rather than livestock.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 9x12m to 12x18m (30x40ft to 40x60ft) is the most common range; larger operations run up to 12x24m (40x80ft) or beyond.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story, tall open volume, eave height commonly ~4-5m (13-16ft) to clear modern implements.
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Pole-frame construction (widely spaced wood posts set in the ground, no continuous foundation), corrugated steel or metal panel siding and roofing, one or more large sliding or hinged doors on a gable end, minimal windows, simple gable roof (not gambrel — distinguishes it from the older dairy barn).

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Main equipment bay**: single open volume, 9-12m x 12-24m depending on tier
- **Workshop corner (optional partitioned area)**: 4m x 5m
- **Small parts/supply room (optional)**: 3m x 3m

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Main bay**: tractor(s) (1-2), combine or larger implement (0-1, small city-adjacent large farms only), attached implements (planter, disc, baler, mower — 2-6), fuel tank/stand (1), workbench (0-1 if no separate workshop), parts shelving along one wall.
- **Workshop corner**: workbench (1), tool wall/pegboard (1), welder (0-1), vise, tool chests (1-2).
- **Parts/supply room**: shelving (filters, belts, fluids, spare parts), fasteners bins.

## Placement Notes
- Large gable-end doors should be oriented toward the farmstead's main equipment yard/driveway for easy pull-through access — no interior partitions should block a straight drive-through path when a through-door layout is generated.
- This archetype is functionally the "modern equivalent" of dedicated equipment storage that older farms kept in barn lean-tos; when both a barn and a pole building are generated on the same farmstead, bias the barn's contents toward hay/livestock and the pole building toward equipment/machinery to avoid redundant prop pools.
- Site near the farmhouse and driveway, typically closer to the road than the barn.
