# Library

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: All tiers — rural village libraries are often a single-room Carnegie-style or converted-house building; small town/city libraries are larger dedicated buildings with distinct sections.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: Rural village: 8-12m x 10-15m. Small town: 14-20m x 18-25m. Small city: 20-30m x 25-40m.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story (small/Carnegie-era) up to 2 stories (small city, with a lower-level meeting room/children's area).
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Brick or stone, often a distinctive Carnegie-library classical facade (columns, pediment, prominent central entrance up a short flight of steps) for early-20th-century examples; mid-century examples are plainer brick boxes with large windows.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Main reading room/stacks**: 8-14m x 10-20m
- **Circulation desk area**: 2m deep counter, 3-5m long, near entrance
- **Children's section**: 5-7m x 6-9m
- **Reference/study area**: 4-6m x 5-8m
- **Meeting/community room (small town/city)**: 6-9m x 8-12m
- **Librarian's office/workroom**: 3m x 4m
- **Restrooms (2)**: 1.5m x 2m each

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Main reading room**: bookshelves/stacks (10-30 double-sided ranges), reading tables (3-8), armchairs (2-6), card catalog or computer terminal (1-3 depending on era), circulation desk (1).
- **Children's section**: low shelving (4-8), small tables/chairs, rug/reading nook, picture-book bins.
- **Reference area**: reference shelving (3-6), study carrels/tables (2-5), microfilm reader (older/small-city variant, 0-1).
- **Meeting room**: folding tables/chairs (stackable, 20-60 capacity), podium (0-1), projector screen (0-1).
- **Workroom/office**: desk, book-repair table, sorting cart, shelving for processing.

## Placement Notes
- Circulation desk is always positioned near the entrance with sightlines across the main reading room (supervision convention).
- Children's section is placed away from the quiet reference/reading zones, often near a secondary entrance or the meeting room for noise separation.
- Meeting room, when present, should have independent after-hours access from the main library floor (separate exterior door) so it can be used when the library itself is closed.
- Rural-village tier: single combined room with shelving around the perimeter, a few tables in the center, and the circulation desk doubling as the librarian's only workstation.
