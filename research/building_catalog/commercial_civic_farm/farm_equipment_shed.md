# Farm Equipment Shed

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: Farm zone, all settlement tiers — a simpler, often older/smaller cousin of the `pole_building_machine_shed.md` archetype. Use this template for a smaller, more open-sided, lower-cost equipment shelter (common on smaller/older farmsteads), and use the pole-building template for larger enclosed modern equipment buildings. Generate one or the other per farmstead (rarely both) based on farm size.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 6-9m x 9-15m (20-30ft x 30-50ft) — smaller than a full pole building.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story, open volume, eave height ~3.5-4.5m.
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Simple post-and-beam or pole construction, often open on one or more long sides (three-sided shelter is common rather than a fully enclosed building), corrugated metal or wood roof, sometimes no doors at all on the open bays — just a roof and back/side walls for weather protection.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Open equipment bay**: single open volume, 6-9m x 9-15m, 1-3 open-fronted bays if the shed is divided by posts

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Equipment bay**: parked implements (plow, disc, planter, mower, wagon — 2-5 pieces), small tractor (0-1), miscellaneous stacked lumber/fence posts along a closed back wall, coiled fence wire, tool hooks on support posts.

## Placement Notes
- Unlike the pole building/machine shed, this archetype is frequently open-sided (no doors) — bias the generator toward leaving 1+ sides open rather than always enclosing it, which also simplifies interior generation (much of it can be treated as a covered exterior space rather than a fully enclosed room).
- Site at the farmstead periphery, often between the barn and the field edge, since it mainly shelters implements moved in and out of the fields.
- Prefer this template over the pole building for rural-village-tier smaller farms; prefer the pole building for larger/small-city-adjacent farms.
