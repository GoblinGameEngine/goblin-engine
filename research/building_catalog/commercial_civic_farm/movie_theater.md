# Movie Theater (Single-Screen)

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: Small towns (small "picture show," ~300-700 seats) and small cities (larger "movie palace" style, up to ~1,000-1,500 seats). Rural villages typically lack a dedicated theater.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: Small-town: 10-14m wide x 25-35m deep. Small-city: 15-22m wide x 35-50m deep. Marquee projects over the sidewalk at the entrance.
- **Typical story count**: 1 tall story for the auditorium (6-10m ceiling) with a 2-story front-of-house (lobby below, projection booth + sometimes balcony above).
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Brick or masonry box with an ornamented facade (Art Deco/Moderne common 1920s-40s), illuminated marquee and vertical blade sign, box office kiosk projecting onto the sidewalk.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Box office (exterior kiosk or lobby alcove)**: 1.5m x 2m
- **Lobby/concession area**: 6-10m x 8-12m
- **Auditorium**: 10-18m x 18-30m, seating 300-700 (small town) up to 700-1,500 (small city)
- **Screen wall/stage apron**: 1-2m deep strip at front of auditorium
- **Projection booth**: 3m x 4m, rear-upper of auditorium
- **Balcony (small city, optional)**: matches ~1/3 of auditorium footprint, rear-upper
- **Restrooms (2)**: 3m x 4m each, lobby-adjacent
- **Manager's office/storage**: 2.5m x 3m

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Lobby**: concession stand (popcorn machine, candy case, soda fountain) (1), ticket-taker podium (1), poster display cases (2-6), carpet/decorative fixtures, bench seating (0-2).
- **Auditorium**: fixed theater seating in rows (300-1,500 seats), aisle carpet runners, proscenium arch/drapery (1), screen (1), wall sconces (6-20), exit signs.
- **Projection booth**: projector(s) (1-2), film reel racks, small work counter, porthole windows facing screen.
- **Restrooms**: multiple stalls/urinals, sinks, mirrors.
- **Manager's office**: desk, safe (ticket cash), film canister shelving.

## Placement Notes
- Box office is always at the sidewalk edge, under the marquee, ahead of the lobby doors.
- Auditorium's long axis runs perpendicular to the street, screen at the far end from the entrance — building depth is dominated by this single big room.
- Projection booth is always at the opposite end from the screen, elevated, with a direct sightline down the auditorium.
- Small-city variant adds a balcony reached by a separate stair from the lobby; small-town variant omits it (single raked floor).
