# Gas Station / Service Station

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: All tiers — rural village has one small single-bay station; small towns/cities have several, up to 2-3 service bays at busier locations.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 8-14m wide x 10-16m deep for the "oblong box" building; canopy over the pump islands extends the site footprint significantly further (10-20m wide forecourt) but is not enclosed interior space.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story.
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Simple rectangular "oblong box" massing (especially 1930s-50s Art Deco/Moderne examples), porcelain-enamel panel or brick cladding, prominent corner office with large windows for sightlines to both the pumps and the service bays, roll-up glazed service bay doors, freestanding or attached canopy over the pump islands.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Office/sales counter**: 3-4m x 4-5m, corner position facing the pump islands
- **Service bay(s)**: 4-5m x 6-8m each, 1-3 bays
- **Restroom(s)**: 1.5m x 2m each (1-2, sometimes exterior-facing doors per mid-century convention)
- **Small parts/storage room**: 2.5m x 3m, bay-adjacent
- **Pump island/canopy (exterior, not enclosed)**: forecourt feature, 2-4 pump islands

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Office**: counter with register (1), cigarette/snack rack (0-1), road map display (1), stool, small oil-can display shelf, wall clock/thermometer sign.
- **Service bay**: hydraulic lift (0-1 per bay), tool chest/rollaway (1-2), air compressor, tire rack, workbench, oil drain pit or lift (1).
- **Parts/storage room**: shelving (oil, filters, belts), tire stack.
- **Restroom**: basic fixtures, sometimes with exterior-only door (no interior office access) per historic convention.

## Placement Notes
- Office is always placed at the corner nearest the pump islands and street intersection, with large windows on two sides so the attendant can watch both bays and pumps — a documented period-design rule.
- Service bays open directly onto the forecourt/driveway via roll-up doors, never onto the street frontage directly.
- Rural-village tier: single bay, no separate parts room (shelving folds into the bay itself).
- Site the building at a corner lot or along the highway-frontage edge of downtown/commercial zoning for maximum forecourt visibility.
