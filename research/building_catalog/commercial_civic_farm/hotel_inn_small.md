# Small Hotel / Inn

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: Small towns and small cities (a "downtown hotel" was a near-universal Main Street anchor into the mid-20th century). Rural villages may have a small boarding-house variant instead of a true hotel.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 12-20m wide x 20-30m deep (40-65ft x 65-100ft), often the tallest building on Main Street.
- **Typical story count**: 2-4 stories (ground floor commercial/lobby, upper floors guest rooms).
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Brick masonry, taller and more vertically proportioned than neighboring retail, decorative cornice, prominent ground-floor entrance sometimes with a covered portico/canopy for vehicle drop-off.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Lobby**: 6-10m x 8-12m, ground floor
- **Front desk/office**: 2.5m x 4m, lobby-adjacent
- **Ground-floor cafe/dining room (optional)**: 6-9m x 8-12m
- **Guest rooms**: 3-4m x 4-5m each, 8-30 rooms depending on tier, repeated per upper floor
- **Shared bathroom (older/budget examples)**: 2m x 2.5m per floor, 1-2 per floor
- **En-suite bathroom (nicer examples)**: 1.5m x 2m per guest room
- **Stairwell/elevator core**: 3m x 4m
- **Linen/housekeeping closet**: 2m x 2.5m per floor
- **Basement mechanical/boiler room**: matches building footprint, half

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Lobby**: sofas/armchairs (2-4 groupings), front desk counter (1), mailbox/key cubby wall (1), potted plants, rug, fireplace (0-1, older/nicer examples).
- **Front desk**: desk, register/ledger, key rack, small safe.
- **Guest room**: bed(s) (1-2), nightstand, dresser, small desk/chair, closet or wardrobe, window.
- **Dining room (optional)**: tables/chairs (4-10 sets), sideboard, kitchen pass-through.
- **Housekeeping closet**: linen shelving, cart, cleaning supplies.

## Placement Notes
- Lobby and front desk always occupy the street-level entrance bay; guest rooms are stacked identically on upper floors around a central corridor and stair/elevator core.
- Corridor runs the depth of the building with rooms on both sides (double-loaded corridor) for taller/larger examples; single-loaded (rooms one side, windows other) for narrower small-town lots.
- Small-village boarding-house variant: shrink to a converted large house footprint (see residential template), 4-8 rooms, shared bath only, no separate lobby/front desk (a parlor doubles as one).
