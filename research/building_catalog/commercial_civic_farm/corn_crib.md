# Corn Crib

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: Farm zone, all settlement tiers — classic Midwestern farmstead structure for storing unshelled ear corn; less universal on farms established after the shift to shelled-corn storage/silos, so treat as slightly more common on older/smaller farmsteads (rural village tier) than on the largest small-city-adjacent operations.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: Double-crib with center driveway: each crib ~2.4m (8ft) wide, driveway 3-3.5m (11ft) wide between them, total width roughly 8-9m; length variable, commonly 8-12m (documented example ~32ft/9.8m long). Single crib (no driveway): a small standalone crib roughly 2.5-4m wide x 4-8m long.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story crib volume plus a loft/gable space above the center driveway (in double-crib examples) used for small-grain bins or equipment storage.
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Distinctive slatted wood siding (widely spaced horizontal slats) to allow airflow for corn drying, walls taper outward from a narrower base to a wider top (classic corn-crib profile) to shed rain, raised on posts/piers above the ground for rodent control and airflow, gable roof spanning both cribs and the driveway.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Crib bay(s)**: 2.4m x 8-12m each, 1-2 cribs (slatted, ear-corn storage — mostly non-walkable bulk-storage volume)
- **Center driveway (double-crib only)**: 3-3.5m x 8-12m, open-sided, wagon/truck pass-through
- **Loft (above driveway, double-crib only)**: matches driveway footprint, low headroom

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Crib bay**: ear corn (bulk fill, set dressing, variable fill level), occasionally a small grain-scoop or corn fork leaned against the slats near the loading door.
- **Driveway**: wagon or truck-loading space (kept clear), hanging tools (corn hooks/husking pegs) on a driveway-facing post, feed sacks stacked to one side.
- **Loft**: small-grain storage bins (0-2), miscellaneous stored equipment, ladder access from the driveway.

## Placement Notes
- Slatted crib walls are a purely exterior/structural detail but should inform generator visual variety (slat spacing/weathering) even though the crib interior is mostly non-enterable bulk storage.
- Center driveway must be a clear through-lane wide enough for a wagon, flanked symmetrically by the two crib bays — keep this symmetry rule for the double-crib variant.
- Site within the farmstead cluster near the barn and machine shed, typically along the route between the field access road and the barnyard.
