# School (One-Room Rural / Consolidated)

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: One-room schoolhouse variant — rural village tier (and historically scattered in farm country outside any settlement, but relevant here for village generation). Consolidated school variant — small town and small city tiers.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: One-room schoolhouse: 6-9m x 8-11m (20-30ft x 26-36ft), often with a matching-size basement. Consolidated school (small town): 30-50m x 40-70m, single or two-story wings around a central hall. Consolidated school (small city, K-12 or split elementary/middle/high): 60-120m x 60-150m as a multi-wing campus.
- **Typical story count**: One-room school: 1 story + basement. Consolidated: 1-3 stories.
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: One-room school: wood-frame or brick, simple gable roof, single door, belfry/bell (small cupola) common, tall double-hung windows along one side for daylight. Consolidated school: brick institutional architecture, symmetrical facade, flagpole, distinct gymnasium massing visible from exterior (taller roof section).

## Room/Space List (Template)
### One-room schoolhouse
- **Classroom**: 6-9m x 8-11m, seats ~20-40
- **Cloakroom/entry vestibule**: 1.5m x 3-6m (narrow strip along entry wall)
- **Basement (coal storage/furnace, sometimes overflow space)**: matches classroom footprint

### Consolidated school (small town / small city)
- **Classrooms**: 7-9m x 8-10m each, 6-30+ rooms depending on tier
- **Gymnasium/auditorium**: 15-24m x 24-36m
- **Cafeteria**: 10-15m x 15-22m
- **Kitchen**: 6-8m x 8-10m, cafeteria-adjacent
- **Library/media center**: 8-12m x 10-16m
- **Administrative offices**: 6-8m x 8-10m, near main entrance
- **Hallways/lockers**: corridor network, 2.5-3m wide
- **Restrooms**: 4m x 5m per cluster, multiple clusters
- **Gymnasium locker rooms (2)**: 6m x 8m each

## Per-Space Content Catalog
### One-room schoolhouse
- Student desks (bolted rows, 20-40)
- Teacher's desk (1, front)
- Chalkboard (1-2, front wall)
- Potbelly/wood stove (1, center or rear)
- Bookshelf/small library (1)
- Water pail/washbasin (1, pre-plumbing era)
- Coat hooks (1 per student, cloakroom)
- Bell (belfry, exterior)
- Outhouses (2, exterior, if no plumbing — flag as separate small structures)

### Consolidated school
- **Classroom**: student desks (20-30), teacher's desk (1), chalkboard/whiteboard (1-2), bookshelf (1-2), coat closet (1).
- **Gymnasium**: bleachers (retractable, both sides), basketball hoops (2), stage (0-1, doubles as auditorium).
- **Cafeteria**: long tables with attached benches (8-20), serving line.
- **Kitchen**: institutional range/oven, steam tables, walk-in cooler, dish-washing station.
- **Library**: shelving (8-16 units), reading tables (4-8), circulation desk (1).
- **Admin offices**: reception counter, principal's office, nurse's office (small city tier), filing cabinets.

## Placement Notes
- One-room schoolhouse: single door and cloakroom always on the leeward/entry gable end; windows concentrated on one long side only (traditionally south-facing) to keep the teacher's view of the door and even daylight without cross-glare.
- Consolidated school: gymnasium is the tallest-volume room and typically anchors one end or wing, visible as a distinct massing from the exterior; cafeteria/kitchen cluster near a service entrance for deliveries; classrooms line double-loaded corridors.
- Administrative offices are always adjacent to the main public entrance for visitor control.
- Small-city tier may generate 2-3 separate school buildings (elementary/middle/high) rather than one — treat as repeated instances of the consolidated template at different scales.
