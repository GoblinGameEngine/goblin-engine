# Small Grocery / Market

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: All tiers. Rural villages have one small grocery/general-store hybrid; small towns have a dedicated independent grocery; small cities support 2-4 independent or small-chain groceries plus the downtown corner store type.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 12-20m wide x 20-35m deep (40-65ft x 65-115ft) — larger than typical storefronts due to aisle and cooler space.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story, tall ceiling (4-5m) for shelving/signage.
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Brick or concrete-block commercial building, wide street frontage with multiple display windows, prominent signage. Rear/side has a dedicated delivery dock, larger than typical retail alley doors.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Sales floor (grocery aisles)**: 10-18m x 15-25m
- **Produce section**: 4-6m x 5-8m, usually front-of-store near entry
- **Meat counter/butcher area**: 4-6m x 5-7m, rear third
- **Dairy/frozen cooler wall**: runs along one long wall, 1.5m deep x 8-15m
- **Back stockroom/receiving**: 6-10m x 8-14m
- **Walk-in cooler + freezer**: 3m x 4m each
- **Office**: 2.5m x 3m
- **Restroom (employee)**: 1.5m x 2m
- **Bottle/can return or break room (small cities)**: 3m x 3m

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Sales floor**:
  - Shelving aisles (5-10 double-sided runs)
  - Checkout counters/registers (1-3)
  - End-cap displays (4-8)
  - Basket/cart corral (1, near entry)
- **Produce section**: bins/tables (4-8), misting rack (0-1), scale (1).
- **Meat counter**: glass display case, butcher block, meat saw, scale, hanging hooks (cooler-adjacent).
- **Cooler wall**: glass-door reach-in units (6-14 doors' worth).
- **Stockroom**: pallet racks (6-10), hand truck/pallet jack (1-2), bulk crates.
- **Walk-in cooler/freezer**: wire shelving, hanging meat rail.
- **Office**: desk, safe, security-camera monitor (small city tier).

## Placement Notes
- Meat counter and coolers are always rear/side-adjacent to the stockroom and delivery dock for cold-chain logistics.
- Produce placed near the entrance is a near-universal convention — apply it as a hard rule.
- Delivery dock should connect to the alley/service-lane network; grocery deliveries are larger than typical retail, so dock width should be generated at the wide end of the alley-access range.
- Rural-village tier: shrink to a single combined "grocery + sundries" room (8m x 12m) with no separate meat counter, folding butcher function into a small service case near checkout.
