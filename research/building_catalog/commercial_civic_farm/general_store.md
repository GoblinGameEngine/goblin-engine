# General Store

## Overview
- **Population tiers**: Rural village (500-5,000) — often the anchor retail business in the smallest settlements; also present as a legacy "five and dime"/variety store in small towns (6,000-10,000). Rare as a standalone type in small cities (30,000-50,000), where it's typically replaced by specialized retail, but a corner variant can persist in older neighborhoods.
- **Typical exterior footprint**: 9-15m wide x 15-25m deep (30-50ft x 50-80ft). Narrow street frontage, long lot depth — classic Main Street commercial lot proportions.
- **Typical story count**: 1 story with retail, occasionally 2 stories (upper floor = storage, lodge hall, or apartment).
- **Typical exterior materials/architectural notes**: Brick or wood-frame with a false front (flat parapet disguising a lower gable roof) in the smallest villages; brick masonry with cast-iron or pressed-metal storefront cornice in larger towns. Large plate-glass display windows flanking a recessed central entry, bulkhead panels below windows, awning or covered porch overhang common in rural variants.

## Room/Space List (Template)
- **Sales floor**: 8-13m x 12-20m — the whole front two-thirds of the building.
- **Back stockroom/storage**: 4-7m x 5-8m, rear of building.
- **Office/counting room**: 2.5-4m x 3-4m, often a partitioned corner near the front counter or stockroom.
- **Cellar/root cellar (optional, older buildings)**: footprint matches sales floor, half-height, accessed by stairs behind counter.
- **Restroom (single)**: 1.5m x 2m, rear.
- **Loading/delivery alcove**: 2-3m x 3m at rear door, alley-facing.

## Per-Space Content Catalog
- **Sales floor**:
  - Wood or glass-front display counters (2-4)
  - Shelving units, wall-mounted floor-to-ceiling (6-14)
  - Pickle barrel / cracker barrel / grain bins (1-3)
  - Dry goods tables (1-3)
  - Cash register + checkout counter (1, near entry)
  - Potbelly stove or later a space heater, often with chairs for loafers (1 stove + 2-4 chairs, rural variant)
  - Glass candy/tobacco display case (1)
  - Scale (produce/feed) (1)
  - Hanging goods rack (tools, lanterns, harness) (1-2)
  - Post office boxes/window (small rural stores sometimes doubled as post office; 0-1)
- **Stockroom**:
  - Bulk shelving/pallet racks (3-6)
  - Wood crates and barrels (5-15)
  - Hand truck/dolly (1)
  - Stepladder (1)
- **Office**:
  - Rolltop desk (1)
  - Ledger cabinet/safe (1)
  - Chair, wastebasket
- **Restroom**: toilet, sink, small mirror.

## Placement Notes
- Sales floor always fronts the street with the display windows and main entrance; recessed entry is typical.
- Stockroom and delivery alcove are rear/alley-adjacent, matching the alley/service-lane system already used in downtown zones.
- In the smallest villages (pop. <1,500) the general store may be the ONLY commercial building — treat it as capable of absorbing post-office and lunch-counter functions (add 1 small table set to sales floor).
- Second floor, when present, generates as either extra storage or a small fraternal-lodge hall / rental apartment (link to residential template).
