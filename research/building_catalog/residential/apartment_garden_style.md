# Garden-Style Apartment Complex

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1960-2000; the dominant multi-family form in postwar/late-20th-century American suburbs — a cluster of low-rise buildings around shared parking and green space rather than a single monolithic structure.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small cities (30,000-50,000) almost exclusively — requires enough population and commuter infrastructure (surface parking, arterial road access) to make sense; not expected in rural villages or most small towns.
- **Exterior footprint**: A complex of multiple 2-3 story buildings, each 18.3-30.5m x 10.7-13.7m (60-100 ft x 35-45 ft), arranged around internal driveways/parking lots and shared green courtyard space. Total complex footprint highly variable (0.4-2+ hectares / 1-5+ acres).
- **Story count**: 2-3 stories per building, no elevator, exterior stair/breezeway access (units open directly to an outdoor walkway rather than an interior corridor).
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Vinyl siding or brick-veneer/siding combo, low-pitch gable or hip roofs, private balcony or patio per unit, exterior stairwells serving 2-4 units per landing.

## 2. Unit type catalog (the template)

**One-bedroom unit** (~45-55% of units): living/dining combo 4.0-4.9m x 3.4-4.0m, kitchen (often open to living/dining) 2.7-3.4m x 2.4-3.0m, bedroom 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m, bathroom 2.1-2.4m x 1.8-2.1m, private balcony/patio 1.5-2.4m x 1.2-1.8m. Total ~55-75 sq m (600-800 sq ft).

**Two-bedroom unit** (~35-45% of units): living/dining combo 4.3-5.5m x 3.7-4.3m, kitchen 2.7-3.7m x 2.4-3.0m, primary bedroom 3.4-3.7m x 3.0-3.4m, secondary bedroom 3.0-3.4m x 2.7-3.0m, bathroom (hall, full) 2.1-2.4m x 1.8-2.1m, bathroom (primary ensuite, half or full) 1.8-2.1m x 1.5-1.8m, balcony/patio 1.8-2.7m x 1.2-1.8m. Total ~80-105 sq m (850-1,130 sq ft).

**Three-bedroom unit** (~5-15% of units, townhouse-style 2-floor units common): adds a secondary bedroom 2.7-3.0m x 2.4-2.7m, ~105-130 sq m (1,130-1,400 sq ft).

Shared complex amenities (site-level, not per-building):
- Leasing office/clubhouse (0-1 per complex): 9.1-15.2m x 7.3-10.7m
- Swimming pool + pool deck (common, especially larger complexes): 15-25m x 8-12m
- Fitness room (in clubhouse): 6.1-9.1m x 4.6-6.1m
- Mail kiosk/parcel area: small standalone structure or clubhouse alcove
- Surface parking lots: dominant site feature, typically 1.5-2 spaces per unit
- Dumpster/trash corral enclosures: distributed across the site

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Living/dining combo**: sofa (1), armchair (0-1), TV/entertainment unit (1), coffee table (1), dining table + chairs (1 set, 2-4 chairs, often placed at one end of the combined room), area rug (0-1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), cabinets, dishwasher (0-1, common by 1980s+), microwave (0-1, built-in over range in later units), small appliances (1-3), trash can (1)

**Bedrooms**: bed (queen primary, full/twin secondary), nightstand(s) (1-2), dresser (1), reach-in closet (1)

**Bathrooms**: toilet (1), sink/vanity (1), tub/shower combo (full) or shower/toilet/sink only (half), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

**Balcony/patio**: small outdoor table + chair(s) (0-1 set), grill (0-1), storage closet (0-1, common for in-unit-adjacent storage)

**Clubhouse/leasing office**: front desk (1), lounge seating (2-4 pieces), fitness equipment (in fitness room, 2-5 machines), vending machines (0-2)

## 4. Placement notes

- Site-first design: place the buildings as a cluster arranged around internal drive aisles and parking, with a clubhouse/pool near the complex entrance as the amenity anchor — the site plan matters as much as any individual building's interior.
- Each building is a self-contained stack of 6-12 units served by 1-3 exterior stairwells; there is no interior corridor — walkways/breezeways are open-air, a key visual difference from the small in-town walk-up archetype.
- Ground-floor units typically get a small patio; upper-floor units get a balcony — both types share the same interior room template with only this exterior-feature variant.
- Units within a building are usually all the same 1BR or 2BR floor plate repeated and mirrored, with different buildings in the complex offering different unit-mix ratios — a good procedural lever (this building = mostly 1BR, that one = mostly 2BR).
- In-unit washer/dryer (stackable, in a small utility closet) is common in later (1980s+) construction; earlier complexes rely on a shared on-site laundry room instead.
- Surface parking dominates the site's visual footprint — budget significant open, paved area per building when laying out the complex, more than any of the single-family archetypes require.
