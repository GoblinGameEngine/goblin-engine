# Gable-Front Vernacular Farmhouse

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1870-1930, with a strong Midwestern regional character (National Folk / "I-house" / gable-front-and-wing forms). The oldest surviving housing stock type in rural areas; many still occupied and incrementally added onto.
- **Population tier(s)**: Rural villages (500-5,000) and farm-zone acreages; occasionally found repurposed at town edges of small towns.
- **Exterior footprint**: Main block 7.6-10m x 6.1-7.6m (25-33 ft x 20-25 ft), frequently with a perpendicular rear wing/ell 4.6-6.1m x 4.6-9.1m (15-20 ft x 15-30 ft) added over time for kitchen/summer-kitchen/storage.
- **Story count**: 2 stories on the main block; the rear ell is often 1 story or 1.5 stories.
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Wood clapboard siding (later stock may have vinyl or aluminum re-siding), steep gable roof (main block gable faces the road), simple painted trim, small covered entry porch (front and/or side), standing-seam metal or asphalt shingle roofing.

## 2. Room list (the template)

Main block, ground floor:
- Front parlor/sitting room: 3.7-4.6m x 3.4-4.0m
- Family/living room (may be same room as parlor in smaller variants): 3.4-4.3m x 3.0-3.7m
- Dining room: 3.0-3.7m x 3.0-3.4m
- Downstairs bedroom (common in older/rural variant, for aging occupants): 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m

Rear ell/wing:
- Kitchen: 3.4-4.6m x 3.0-3.7m
- Mudroom/back porch (enclosed): 1.8-3.0m x 1.5-2.4m
- Pantry: 1.5-2.1m x 1.2-1.8m
- Half-bath or full bath (added later, plumbing retrofit): 2.1-2.7m x 1.5-2.1m

Upper floor (main block):
- Primary bedroom: 3.4-4.3m x 3.4-3.7m
- Secondary bedroom(s) (x1-3): 2.7-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Bathroom (single, retrofit-era): 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.1m
- Narrow hallway/landing: 0.9-1.2m wide

**Room count**: 6-9 total rooms; 2-4 bedrooms; 1-1.5 bathrooms (many pre-1930s examples originally had none - an outhouse - with a bath retrofit added mid-century). Overall footprint 90-160 sq m (1,000-1,700 sq ft).

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Parlor/sitting room**: sofa or settee (1), armchair(s) (1-2), small table (1), wood or coal stove (0-1, period), radio cabinet or piano (0-1), family photos/wall décor, rag rug (0-1)

**Family/living room**: sofa (1), armchair (1-2), TV + stand (modern) or radio hutch (period), coffee table (1), bookshelf (0-1)

**Dining room**: dining table + chairs (4-8, often mismatched), hutch/china cabinet (0-1), sideboard (0-1)

**Downstairs bedroom**: bed - full/queen (1), dresser (1), nightstand (0-1), small closet or wardrobe (1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1, wood cookstove in period variants), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), open shelving/cabinets, farm table + chairs (1 set, often the primary eating spot), canning/preserve shelving (0-1), trash can (1)

**Mudroom/back porch**: boot tray (1), coat hooks (1 run), chest freezer (0-1, common in rural households), washer/dryer (0-1, if not in basement)

**Pantry**: open shelving (built-in), canned goods, flour/grain bins (0-2), root vegetable baskets

**Bedrooms (upstairs)**: bed - twin/full/queen depending on room (1), dresser (1), nightstand (0-1), wardrobe (built-ins rare pre-1920s, so freestanding wardrobe common) (1), rag rug (0-1)

**Bathroom**: toilet (1), sink (1), tub (1, tub-only common; shower retrofit variable), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Gable-front orientation means the narrow gable end faces the road; front door is on the gable end, often with a small stoop rather than a grand porch.
- Kitchen and pantry are pushed into the rear ell, physically and thermally separated from the "good rooms" (parlor/dining) at the front - a legacy of keeping cooking heat away from formal spaces.
- Mudroom is the de facto main entrance for daily use (farm/rural households enter through the back), while the front door is reserved for guests/formal occasions.
- Bathroom, if present, is usually a later insertion carved out of a bedroom or closet space - place it adjacent to the kitchen or stacked above it to reuse a single plumbing stack.
- Downstairs bedroom is common for grandparents or when upstairs stairs are steep/narrow; place near the parlor.
- Additions (ell, porches) read as visually distinct massing from the original gable block - useful for procedural variation between "modest original" and "expanded over generations" instances.
