# Small Starter Home (Minimal Traditional / Postwar Cottage)

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1935-1955 (FHA/GI Bill era); mass-built, small, affordable single-family homes designed to be the "minimum house" for a young family - the smallest and plainest of the postwar single-family types, often built by the hundreds in a single subdivision phase.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small towns (6,000-10,000) and small cities (30,000-50,000), typically clustered in whole postwar subdivision blocks of near-identical homes with minor facade variation.
- **Exterior footprint**: Compact rectangle or slight L-shape, 7.3-9.1m x 7.6-9.8m (24-30 ft x 25-32 ft).
- **Story count**: 1 story (occasional shallow half-story attic, rarely finished).
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Wood clapboard or brick-veneer-and-siding combination, simple side-gable or front-gable roof with almost no ornamentation, small stoop or minimal porch at the front door, single small picture or double-hung windows.

## 2. Room list (the template)

- Living room: 3.4-4.3m x 3.4-3.7m
- Kitchen (with small dining space): 2.7-3.7m x 2.4-3.0m
- Primary bedroom: 3.0-3.4m x 2.7-3.0m
- Secondary bedroom: 2.7-3.0m x 2.4-2.7m
- Bathroom (single, full): 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-1.8m
- Small entry vestibule/coat closet: 1.2-1.5m x 0.9-1.2m

**Room count**: 4-6 total rooms; 2 bedrooms (occasionally 3 in slightly larger variants); 1 bathroom. Overall footprint 55-90 sq m (600-1,000 sq ft) - among the smallest archetypes in the catalog.

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Living room**: sofa (1), armchair (0-1), TV/entertainment unit (modern) or radio (period), coffee table (1), small area rug (0-1)

**Kitchen (with dining space)**: stove/range (1, compact), refrigerator (1, compact), sink + short counter run (1), a few cabinets, small kitchen table + chairs (1 set, 2-4 chairs), trash can (1)

**Bedrooms**: bed - full in primary, twin/full in secondary (1), nightstand (0-1), dresser (1), small closet (built-in, shallow) (1)

**Bathroom**: toilet (1), sink (1), tub/shower combo (1), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Absolute minimum viable house plan: no dining room, no hallway to speak of (rooms often open directly off one another or a very short 1-2m stub hallway), no garage in the original build (carport or driveway pad added later).
- Kitchen doubles as the dining space; place the small table near the kitchen's exterior window.
- Only one bathroom, positioned centrally to minimize plumbing run length - often shares a wall with the kitchen.
- Because these were built in large uniform tracts, use this archetype as the "high repetition, low variation" filler house for postwar residential blocks - light randomization of exterior color/trim is more important here than interior variety, since the base plan itself is nearly identical across a whole block.
- Small unfinished attic accessible by a pull-down ladder or hatch is common but not a real "room" - use as flavor/storage only, not a generated furnished space.
- A small detached garage or later carport addition at the rear of the lot (accessed by a shared alley or driveway) is common; do not assume an attached garage.
