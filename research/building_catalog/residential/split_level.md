# Split-Level (and Split-Foyer variant)

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1955-1980 (peak 1960s-1970s); a postwar/baby-boom suburban type, popular on sloped or varied-grade lots and as a way to add more square footage than a flat ranch on the same lot.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small towns (6,000-10,000) and small cities (30,000-50,000), typically in mid-century and later postwar subdivisions, often on lots with visible grade change.
- **Exterior footprint**: 11-15.2m x 9.1-11m (36-50 ft x 30-36 ft); attached garage often tucked under the upper level, extending the footprint.
- **Story count**: 3 short half-story levels offset by half-flights of stairs (true split-level), OR 2 full levels split by a foyer landing (split-foyer/raised-ranch variant).
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Brick veneer lower level, wood/vinyl siding upper level (common two-material combo); low-to-moderate pitch gable roof; the multi-level massing itself is the defining visual signature (a stepped silhouette).

## 2. Room list (the template)

Entry landing (mid-level, between floors): 1.5-2.1m x 1.5-1.8m

Main/upper level:
- Living room: 3.7-5.2m x 3.4-4.3m
- Dining room: 2.7-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Kitchen (often eat-in): 3.0-4.3m x 2.7-3.7m

Upper bedroom level (half-flight up from main):
- Primary bedroom: 3.4-4.3m x 3.4-3.7m
- Secondary bedroom(s) (x1-2): 3.0-3.4m x 2.7-3.0m
- Bathroom (full, hall): 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.1m
- Bathroom (half, ensuite, optional): 1.5-1.8m x 1.2-1.5m

Lower level (half-flight down from main, partly below grade):
- Family/rec room: 3.7-5.5m x 3.4-4.6m
- Bedroom (guest/optional 4th bedroom): 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.0m
- Laundry/utility room: 1.8-2.7m x 1.8-2.4m
- Attached garage: 3.0-6.1m x 5.5-6.1m

Basement level (true split-level, below the lower level, partly/fully below grade):
- Storage/mechanical/unfinished space: variable, 3.0-4.6m x 3.0-4.6m

**Room count**: 7-10 total rooms across 3-4 levels; 3-4 bedrooms; 1.5-2.5 bathrooms. Overall footprint 130-190 sq m (1,400-2,000 sq ft).

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Living room**: sofa (1), armchair(s) (1-2), TV/entertainment unit (1), coffee table (1), area rug (1)

**Dining room**: dining table + chairs (4-6), sideboard (0-1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), upper/lower cabinets, eat-in nook table + chairs (0-1 set), dishwasher (0-1), small appliances (1-3)

**Bedrooms (primary/secondary)**: bed (queen primary, twin/full secondary), nightstand(s) (1-2), dresser (1), built-in closet (1), desk (0-1)

**Family/rec room (lower level)**: sectional sofa or additional couch (1), TV/entertainment unit (1), games table (0-1, e.g. ping-pong or card table), bar cart or wet bar (0-1, period-common 1960s-70s feature), bookshelf (0-1)

**Lower-level bedroom**: bed - twin/full (1), dresser (1), small closet (1) — often used as guest room or teen bedroom for extra privacy from the main bedroom level

**Laundry/utility room**: washer (1), dryer (1), utility sink (0-1), shelving (1-2)

**Bathrooms**: toilet (1), sink/vanity (1), tub/shower combo (full bath) or shower-only/half fixtures (half bath), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

**Garage**: car(s) (1-2, implied), storage shelving (1-2), workbench (0-1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Defining rule: at least three distinct floor levels connected by half-flights of stairs (typically 6-8 steps each), all visible/reachable from a central stair core near the front entry - model this as a vertical "stack" rather than a flat floor graph.
- Entry door opens onto a small landing partway between levels, from which stairs go up to the main/bedroom levels and down to the lower level - this landing is the circulation hub.
- Bedrooms cluster on the uppermost level for maximum separation from the noisy lower-level family room.
- Family/rec room occupies the lower level (partially below grade) and is the informal, high-activity space - place TV, games, and bar features here rather than the formal upper living room.
- Garage is usually tucked beneath the bedroom level, at grade with the lower level, with direct interior access to the family room or a utility mudroom.
- Split-foyer variant simplifies this to just 2 main levels split by a half-flight from the entry foyer (no true third "in-between" level) - use as a smaller/cheaper procedural variant with the same room-content pools but 2 levels instead of 3-4.
