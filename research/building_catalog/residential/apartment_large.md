# Larger City Apartment Block (Mid-Rise)

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1910-1930 (older masonry mid-rise near a historic downtown) and c. 1990-present (modern mixed-use-adjacent mid-rise, sometimes with ground-floor retail). Represents the tallest/densest residential building type expected in the setting.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small cities (30,000-50,000) only, and even there typically limited to a handful of buildings near the true downtown core — this is the "biggest building in town" residential type, not a common one.
- **Exterior footprint**: 30.5-45.7m x 18.3-24.4m (100-150 ft x 60-80 ft), often filling most of a downtown block or a full block face.
- **Story count**: 4-6 stories (mid-rise; taller would require an elevator core and is treated as a single fixed instance per settlement rather than a repeatable template, per the population-tier ceiling of this setting).
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Older: masonry (brick or terra-cotta-trimmed) facade, flat roof, ornamented cornice line, single grand lobby entrance. Modern: brick-veneer/fiber-cement panel combo, flat or low-slope roof, ground-floor retail bays common (mixed-use variant), covered or podium parking.

## 2. Unit type catalog (the template)

**Studio unit** (~15-25% of units): combined living/sleeping/kitchenette 20-32 sq m (215-345 sq ft), bathroom 1.8-2.1m x 1.5-1.8m.

**One-bedroom unit** (~45-55% of units): living room 3.4-4.6m x 3.0-3.7m, kitchen (galley or small L-shape, sometimes open to living room) 2.4-3.4m x 2.1-2.7m, bedroom 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m, bathroom 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-2.1m. Total ~50-70 sq m (540-750 sq ft).

**Two-bedroom unit** (~20-30% of units): living/dining 3.7-4.9m x 3.4-4.0m, kitchen 2.7-3.7m x 2.4-3.0m, primary bedroom 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m, secondary bedroom 2.7-3.4m x 2.4-3.0m, bathroom (hall) 2.1-2.4m x 1.8-2.1m, bathroom (primary ensuite, optional) 1.8-2.1m x 1.5-1.8m. Total ~70-95 sq m (750-1,020 sq ft).

Shared building spaces:
- Ground-floor lobby (staffed or unstaffed): 4.6-7.3m x 3.7-5.5m
- Elevator core + stairwells (1 elevator + 1-2 egress stairs per building): 3.7-4.6m x 3.7-4.6m footprint, repeats every floor
- Double-loaded central corridor per floor: 1.5-1.8m wide
- Ground-floor retail bay(s) (modern mixed-use variant, optional): 6.1-12.2m x 9.1-15.2m each
- Shared laundry room (per floor or centralized): 3.7-5.5m x 3.0-4.0m
- Rooftop or courtyard common area (modern variant, optional): variable
- Basement/podium parking (modern variant, optional): structured, one level

**Unit count**: 24-60+ units per building, 5-12 units per floor across 4-6 floors.

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Studio**: sofa or sofa-bed (1), small TV/entertainment unit (1), compact table + 1-2 chairs (0-1 set), bed - full/queen (1), small dresser (1), kitchenette run along one wall (stove, fridge, sink, compact cabinets), small closet (1)

**Living room (1BR/2BR)**: sofa (1), armchair (0-1), TV/entertainment unit (1), coffee table (1), area rug (0-1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), cabinets, dishwasher (0-1, common in modern units), small appliances (1-3), trash can (1)

**Bedrooms**: bed (queen primary, full/twin secondary), nightstand (0-1), dresser (1), reach-in closet (1)

**Bathrooms**: toilet (1), sink/vanity (1), tub/shower combo or shower stall, mirror (1), towel rack (1)

**Lobby**: front desk or mailbox bank (1), seating (0-2 pieces), directory/buzzer panel (1)

**Retail bay (if present)**: left unfurnished/generic in the residential catalog — treat as a separate commercial-template slot woven into the ground floor of this archetype

## 4. Placement notes

- This is the tallest, most vertically-repetitive archetype in the residential catalog: generate one floor template with a double-loaded corridor and 5-12 units, then stack it 4-6 times with the elevator/stair core held in a fixed position on every floor.
- Corner and end-of-corridor units are larger/more desirable and are a good candidate for a "premium unit" footprint variant (extra windows on two exposures instead of one).
- Ground floor differs structurally from upper floors: lobby, mailroom, and (in the modern mixed-use variant) street-facing retail bays replace some of the residential unit slots — do not simply repeat the typical floor template on the ground level.
- Older masonry examples generally lack in-unit laundry (rely on a shared per-floor or basement laundry room); modern examples increasingly include a stacked in-unit washer/dryer closet in 1BR/2BR units.
- Because this type requires a genuine downtown/elevator-core commitment, cap the number of instances per settlement low (1-3 buildings) even at the top of the small-city population tier — it should read as a rare, prominent landmark type rather than a repeatable filler building.
