# Mobile / Manufactured Home

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1960-present (the "single-wide"/"double-wide" typology standardized after HUD code in 1976). Extremely common, low-cost rural and small-town housing, frequently sited individually on a rural lot or clustered in a mobile home park.
- **Population tier(s)**: Rural villages (500-5,000) most commonly, both as standalone farm-adjacent homes and in small trailer-park clusters; also found at the edges of small towns.
- **Exterior footprint**: Single-wide: 4.3-5.5m x 18-24m (14-18 ft x 60-90 ft). Double-wide: 7.3-9.8m x 17-24m (24-32 ft x 56-80 ft), assembled from two joined single-wide-like sections.
- **Story count**: 1 story, set on a pier/block foundation or slab, typically 0.5-1m off grade with wood or metal skirting concealing the underside.
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Vinyl or metal siding, shallow-pitched or nearly flat roof (older units) or a low-pitch shingled roof (newer "residential-style" units); a small attached wood deck/steps at the entry door(s).

## 2. Room list (the template)

Single-wide:
- Living room (front or end of home): 3.4-4.3m x 3.4-4.0m
- Kitchen (open to living/dining, galley-style): 2.4-3.7m x 2.1-2.7m
- Dining nook (often built-in bench or small table): 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.4m
- Primary bedroom (one end of the home): 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Secondary bedroom(s) (x1-2, other end or middle): 2.4-3.0m x 2.4-2.7m
- Bathroom(s) (1-2): 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-1.8m
- Central hallway (spine connecting the two ends): 0.8-1.0m wide

Double-wide (adds width, allowing separate rooms rather than open galley):
- Living room: 4.0-5.5m x 3.7-4.3m
- Kitchen (separate/defined): 3.4-4.3m x 2.7-3.4m
- Dining room/area: 2.7-3.7m x 2.4-3.0m
- Primary bedroom (with ensuite bath common): 3.7-4.6m x 3.4-3.7m
- Secondary bedroom(s) (x2-3): 3.0-3.4m x 2.7-3.0m
- Bathroom (hall, full): 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.1m
- Bathroom (primary ensuite): 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.1m
- Utility/laundry closet: 1.2-1.5m x 0.9-1.2m

**Room count**: single-wide: 4-6 rooms, 2-3 bedrooms, 1-2 bathrooms, 45-115 sq m (500-1,250 sq ft). Double-wide: 6-9 rooms, 3-4 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, 125-225 sq m (1,340-2,430 sq ft).

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Living room**: sofa (1), armchair (0-1), TV/entertainment unit (1), coffee table (1), area rug (0-1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1, often a compact apartment-size unit), refrigerator (1), sink + short counter run (1), overhead cabinets (compact run), microwave (1, common built-in-over-range), trash can (1)

**Dining nook/area**: built-in bench dinette (0-1, single-wide-common) or freestanding table + chairs (1 set, 4 chairs)

**Bedrooms (primary)**: bed - queen/full (1), nightstand(s) (1-2), dresser (1), small built-in closet (1)

**Bedrooms (secondary)**: bed - twin/full (1), nightstand (0-1), dresser (1), small built-in closet (1)

**Bathroom**: toilet (1), sink/vanity (compact, 1), shower stall or tub/shower combo (1), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

**Utility/laundry (double-wide)**: stacked or side-by-side washer/dryer (1), shelving (0-1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Strong linear/rectangular organization: rooms run in sequence along the length of the home off a single central hallway spine - closer in spirit to the shotgun house than to a branching floor plan.
- In single-wides, kitchen/dining/living are usually one continuous open zone at one end (often near the main entry) with bedrooms clustered at the other end for noise separation.
- Primary bedroom is typically placed at the far end from the living area for maximum privacy; in double-wides it often gets the only ensuite bathroom.
- Skirting conceals a crawlspace, not a basement - there is no below-grade level; utilities (water heater, furnace) are usually in a small closet, not a separate mechanical room.
- Deck/steps at entry doors are exterior add-ons, not part of the manufactured unit itself - useful as a small proc-gen exterior prop cluster.
- Double-wide's two halves are visually joined along a seam (the "marriage line") down the center - if modeling a double-wide as two combined single modules, this seam should run lengthwise through the middle of the home.
- Often sited with an accessory shed and/or a gravel or concrete parking pad rather than an attached garage.
