# Duplex (Two-Family House)

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1900-present, in two common forms: an older "stacked" up-down duplex (common in dense older small-city neighborhoods, often converted from a single-family Foursquare-style shell) and a modern "side-by-side" duplex (built as such, common in later infill and small-town rental development).
- **Population tier(s)**: Small cities (30,000-50,000) primarily, especially near downtown/older rental districts; occasionally a side-by-side variant in small towns as rental housing stock.
- **Exterior footprint**: Stacked variant: matches a Foursquare-scale footprint, 9.1-11m x 9.1-11m (30-36 ft x 30-36 ft). Side-by-side variant: wider, 12.2-15.2m x 8.5-10.4m (40-50 ft x 28-34 ft), essentially two narrow units mirrored across a shared center wall.
- **Story count**: Stacked: 2 stories, one unit per floor. Side-by-side: 1-2 stories, both units full height.
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Wood clapboard or brick; simple hip or gable roof; two separate front entries (side-by-side has two doors side by side on the facade; stacked has one shared entry vestibule leading to two interior unit doors, or a shared front stair plus a separate exterior stair to the upper unit).

## 2. Room list (the template — per unit)

- Living room: 3.4-4.3m x 3.0-3.7m
- Kitchen: 2.7-3.7m x 2.4-3.0m
- Dining area (often combined with kitchen or living room rather than a separate room): 2.4-3.0m x 2.1-2.7m
- Primary bedroom: 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Secondary bedroom (x0-2): 2.7-3.4m x 2.4-3.0m
- Bathroom: 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-2.1m
- Small entry vestibule/hall: 1.2-1.8m x 1.2-1.5m

**Room count (per unit)**: 4-6 rooms; 1-3 bedrooms per unit; 1 bathroom per unit. Overall footprint per unit 65-110 sq m (700-1,200 sq ft); two units per building.

## 3. Per-room content catalog (per unit)

**Living room**: sofa (1), armchair (0-1), TV/entertainment unit (1), coffee table (1), area rug (0-1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), a compact cabinet run, small appliances (1-2), trash can (1)

**Dining area**: small table + chairs (1 set, 2-4 chairs) — frequently placed in a kitchen alcove or a corner of the living room rather than a separate room

**Bedrooms**: bed - queen/full in primary, twin/full in secondary (1), nightstand (0-1), dresser (1), closet (1, often shallow built-in)

**Bathroom**: toilet (1), sink (1), tub/shower combo (1), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

**Shared/exterior**: mailbox cluster (1 per building), shared or split basement (laundry/storage, 0-1 per unit), shared or split backyard, exterior stair to upper unit (stacked variant only)

## 4. Placement notes

- Two full, independent housing units under one roof, each following a small-house room template (closer in scale to the starter home or Cape Cod archetypes than to a full single-family house) — generate each unit as a self-contained mini floor plan and mirror/stack them.
- Stacked variant: ground-floor unit typically gets access to the shared basement and a small backyard; upper-floor unit reaches its own entry via an exterior or enclosed side stair.
- Side-by-side variant: a shared demising wall runs down the center of the building; each unit is a mirror image of the other, each with its own full-height entry and often its own small stoop/porch.
- Kitchens are typically placed toward the rear of each unit, mirrored across the party wall so plumbing stacks/runs can share a common wet wall between units (cost-saving convention worth preserving procedurally).
- Basement and mechanical spaces may be either divided (one furnace/water heater per unit) or shared, depending on building age — older stacked conversions more often share a single basement.
