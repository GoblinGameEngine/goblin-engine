# Rowhouse / Townhouse

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1890-1920 (older brick rowhouse form, uncommon but plausible near a small city's oldest, densest blocks) and c. 1980-present (modern attached townhouse form, common in small-city infill and newer higher-density subdivisions). Include both as era variants of the same "attached, party-wall" footprint logic.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small cities (30,000-50,000) only — this type needs city-scale density to make sense; not expected in small towns or rural villages.
- **Exterior footprint**: Narrow and deep, 5.5-6.7m x 12.2-15.2m (18-22 ft x 40-50 ft) per unit, built in an attached row of 3-8+ units sharing party walls.
- **Story count**: 2-3 stories (older brick rowhouse) or 2 stories + optional attached garage (modern townhouse).
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Older: brick facade, flat or low-slope roof, stone or concrete front stoop. Modern: vinyl/fiber-cement siding or brick-veneer front, asphalt shingle gable or hip roof, attached 1-car garage at the ground floor front or rear alley-loaded.

## 2. Room list (the template — per unit)

Ground floor:
- Living room: 3.7-4.6m x 3.4-4.0m
- Dining area/room: 2.7-3.4m x 2.4-3.0m
- Kitchen: 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Half-bath: 1.5-1.8m x 1.2-1.5m
- Garage (modern variant only, ground floor front or rear): 3.0-3.4m x 5.5-6.1m

Upper floor(s):
- Primary bedroom: 3.4-4.0m x 3.0-3.7m
- Secondary bedroom(s) (x1-2): 2.7-3.4m x 2.7-3.0m
- Bathroom (full): 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.1m
- Bathroom (primary ensuite, modern variant): 2.1-2.4m x 1.8-2.1m
- Upstairs hall: 0.9-1.2m wide

Basement (older brick rowhouse variant):
- Rec room / storage (partially finished): 3.7-4.6m x 3.4-4.0m

**Room count**: 6-8 total rooms; 2-3 bedrooms; 1.5-2.5 bathrooms. Overall footprint per unit 95-160 sq m (1,000-1,700 sq ft) across 2-3 floors.

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Living room**: sofa (1), armchair(s) (1-2), TV/entertainment unit (1), coffee table (1), area rug (1)

**Dining area**: dining table + chairs (4-6), sideboard (0-1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), cabinets, small island or peninsula (0-1, modern variant), small appliances (1-3), trash can (1)

**Bedrooms**: bed (queen primary, full/twin secondary), nightstand(s) (1-2), dresser (1), closet (built-in, modern; wardrobe, older) (1)

**Bathrooms**: toilet (1), sink/vanity (1), tub/shower or shower stall (1), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

**Garage (modern variant)**: car (1, implied), storage shelving (0-1)

**Basement rec room (older variant)**: sofa or futon (0-1), storage shelving (1-2), washer/dryer (1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Party walls on both long sides (except end units, which get one exposed side wall with extra windows) — windows are only possible on the narrow front and rear facades plus, for end units, one side.
- Deep, narrow footprint pushes rooms into a front-to-back sequence per floor, connected by a side-loaded staircase (not centered, since the unit is too narrow for a central hall).
- Older brick rowhouse: kitchen typically at the rear ground floor near a small rear yard/alley; half-bath tucked under the stairs.
- Modern townhouse: ground floor is often garage + entry foyer, with the main living level one floor up (living/dining/kitchen), and bedrooms on the top floor — a vertically zoned "garage-living-sleeping" stack.
- End units in a row are more desirable/larger and are a good candidate for a "corner unit" variant with a slightly bigger footprint and extra side windows.
- Shared roof/party-wall construction means the whole row should be generated as one contiguous structure with repeated/mirrored unit modules, not as independent buildings.
