# American Foursquare

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1895-1935 (peak 1900-1920s); a Sears/Harris Brothers mail-order kit house era favorite. Common surviving stock in small-town and small-city cores built during early-20th-century growth spurts.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small towns (6,000-10,000) and small cities (30,000-50,000), especially older in-town neighborhoods near downtown. Occasionally the "best house in town" example in a rural village.
- **Exterior footprint**: Roughly square, 9-11m x 9-11m (30-36 ft x 30-36 ft). Large front porch spans most of the front facade, adding ~1.8-2.4m of depth.
- **Story count**: 2.5 stories (full 2 stories + usable dormered attic). Occasionally a raised basement.
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Wood clapboard, brick, or stucco cladding; low-pitched hipped roof with a central hipped or gabled dormer; wide boxed eaves; one-story full-width front porch with simple square or classical columns.

## 2. Room list (the template)

Main floor:
- Entrance hall / foyer: 2.1-3.0m x 1.8-2.4m
- Living room (front-left quadrant): 3.7-4.9m x 3.7-4.9m
- Dining room (rear quadrant): 3.4-4.3m x 3.4-4.3m
- Kitchen (rear quadrant): 3.0-4.0m x 2.7-3.7m
- Den / library / sunroom (front-right quadrant, optional variant): 3.0-3.7m x 3.0-3.7m
- Half-bath (later addition, optional): 1.2-1.8m x 1.2-1.5m

Upper floor:
- Primary bedroom: 3.7-4.6m x 3.4-4.0m
- Secondary bedroom (x2-3): 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Full bathroom (shared, one per floor): 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.4m
- Upstairs hall/landing: 1.2-1.5m wide

Attic (dormered, optional finished space):
- Bonus bedroom or storage room: 3.0-4.3m x 2.4-3.7m (sloped ceilings)

**Room count**: 7-10 total rooms; 3-4 bedrooms; 1-2 bathrooms. Overall footprint 140-230 sq m (1,500-2,500 sq ft) across 2-2.5 floors.

## 4. Placement notes

- Classic quadrant plan: living room and entry occupy the front, dining room and kitchen occupy the rear, split by a central staircase hall.
- Kitchen is placed at a rear corner for a rear/service door and exterior wall for windows; often adjacent to a small rear porch or mudroom.
- Dining room is directly adjacent to both kitchen (for serving) and living room (open archway or pocket doors typical of the era).
- Full bathroom placed centrally upstairs, stacked above the kitchen or half-bath below for shared plumbing stack.
- Primary bedroom is the largest upstairs room, typically front-facing (best light/porch roof view).
- Central staircase is the organizing spine of the house on both floors; upstairs hall connects all bedrooms to the single bathroom.
- Front porch is a strong social/exterior feature; place main entry door centered or slightly offset on the front facade.
