# Bungalow / Craftsman

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1905-1935 (peak 1910s-1920s); extremely common in early-20th-century streetcar-suburb neighborhoods nationwide, including Midwestern small cities and larger small towns.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small towns (6,000-10,000) and small cities (30,000-50,000); a "middle-class starter/family home" of its era, so found in high density in older residential grid neighborhoods.
- **Exterior footprint**: 8.5-10.7m x 9.1-12.2m (28-35 ft x 30-40 ft). Wide, low front porch (1.8-2.7m deep) spans most of the facade.
- **Story count**: 1 to 1.5 stories (low-pitched roof with dormers lighting an upstairs half-story).
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Wood shingle, clapboard, or brick lower walls; low-pitched gable roof with wide overhanging eaves; exposed rafter tails and knee braces; tapered/battered porch columns on brick or stone piers.

## 2. Room list (the template)

Ground floor:
- Entry/foyer (often open to living room, minimal separation): 1.8-2.4m x 1.8-2.4m
- Living room: 3.7-4.9m x 3.4-4.3m
- Dining room (open or semi-open to living room): 3.4-4.0m x 3.0-3.7m
- Kitchen: 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Primary bedroom (ground floor in 1-story variant): 3.4-4.0m x 3.0-3.7m
- Secondary bedroom (ground floor): 3.0-3.4m x 2.7-3.0m
- Bathroom: 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.1m
- Sunroom/porch conversion (optional): 2.4-3.4m x 1.8-2.4m

Half-story (1.5-story variant, dormered attic):
- Bedroom(s) (x1-2, sloped ceilings): 3.0-4.3m x 2.7-3.7m
- Small bathroom (optional second bath): 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-1.8m

**Room count**: 5-7 total rooms; 2-3 bedrooms; 1-1.5 bathrooms. Overall footprint 90-140 sq m (1,000-1,500 sq ft).

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Living room**: sofa (1), armchair(s) (1-2), built-in bookshelves/inglenook bench (0-1, signature Craftsman feature), fireplace with tiled or brick surround (0-1), coffee table (1), area rug (1), TV/entertainment unit (modern) or radio cabinet (period)

**Dining room**: built-in china cabinet/buffet (0-1, Craftsman hallmark), dining table + chairs (4-6), pendant light fixture (1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), built-in breakfast nook with bench seating (0-1, period-common) or small kitchen table + chairs (2-4), upper/lower cabinets, pantry cabinet (0-1), small appliances (1-3), trash can (1)

**Bedrooms (primary)**: bed - full/queen (1), nightstand(s) (1-2), dresser (1), built-in closet (1, small by modern standards) or wardrobe, desk (0-1)

**Bedrooms (secondary)**: bed - twin/full (1), nightstand (0-1), dresser (1), built-in closet (1)

**Bathroom**: toilet (1), pedestal sink (1), tub (1, tub/shower combo common by mid-century retrofit), mirrored medicine cabinet (1), towel rack (1)

**Sunroom (if present)**: wicker/rattan seating (1-2 pieces), small table (0-1), plants (decorative)

## 4. Placement notes

- Living room typically opens directly off the entry with minimal foyer separation, and often flows into the dining room through a wide opening or columned archway - open-plan feel despite being a pre-open-concept era.
- Built-in cabinetry (bookshelves, china cabinets, breakfast nooks, window seats) is a defining Craftsman feature - treat as fixed furniture pieces attached to walls rather than freestanding, reducing placement randomization for these specific items.
- Kitchen sits at the rear with a rear/service door, often with a small attached or adjacent porch.
- In the 1.5-story variant, the stair to the upper half-story is compact and often tucked beside the entry or kitchen; upstairs bedrooms have sloped ceilings from the roof pitch and require lower furniture placement near exterior walls.
- Front porch is a major social space - wide and shallow, oriented to the street, usually furnished procedurally as exterior dressing (rocking chairs, porch swing) rather than a "room."
- Primary bedroom, if on the ground floor, is usually placed at the rear or side for privacy from the front public rooms.
