# Colonial Revival

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1900-1950s (long-running, still built in simplified form today); a respectable, symmetrical "banker's house" style found on the better blocks of small towns and small cities.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small towns (6,000-10,000) and small cities (30,000-50,000), typically middle-to-upper-middle-class neighborhoods.
- **Exterior footprint**: 9.8-13.7m x 9.1-11m (32-45 ft x 30-36 ft), rectangular and symmetrical, sometimes with a smaller side wing/sunroom addition.
- **Story count**: 2 stories, occasionally 2.5 with a finished attic.
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Brick or wood clapboard, symmetrical facade with a centered front door (often with a decorative pediment/portico and sidelights), evenly spaced multi-pane double-hung windows, moderate-pitch side-gable roof, minimal-to-moderate classical trim detailing.

## 2. Room list (the template)

Ground floor:
- Center entry hall (with staircase, organizing spine): 2.4-3.4m x 2.1-2.7m
- Living room (one side of the hall): 4.0-5.2m x 3.7-4.6m
- Dining room (opposite side of the hall): 3.7-4.6m x 3.4-4.0m
- Kitchen (rear): 3.4-4.3m x 3.0-3.7m
- Den/study (optional, rear or side): 3.0-3.7m x 3.0-3.4m
- Half-bath (rear/hall, later retrofit common): 1.5-1.8m x 1.2-1.5m
- Sunroom (optional side addition): 3.0-4.0m x 2.4-3.0m

Upper floor:
- Primary bedroom (front, over living room): 4.0-4.6m x 3.7-4.0m
- Secondary bedroom(s) (x2-3): 3.4-3.7m x 3.0-3.4m
- Bathroom(s) (1-2): 2.4-2.7m x 1.8-2.4m
- Upstairs hall/landing: 1.2-1.8m wide

**Room count**: 8-11 total rooms; 3-4 bedrooms; 1.5-2.5 bathrooms. Overall footprint 150-260 sq m (1,600-2,800 sq ft).

## 4. Placement notes

- Rigid bilateral symmetry: a central hall with the staircase splits the ground floor into two matched halves (living room one side, dining room the other), mirrored by bedrooms upstairs.
- Front door is centered on the facade and opens directly into the entry hall, not into any single room.
- Kitchen sits at the rear, generally behind the dining room for ease of serving, with a rear/service door.
- Primary bedroom sits above the living room at the front for the best-lit, most prominent upstairs position.
- Where present, den/study is placed off the entry hall or dining room as a quiet room away from the kitchen bustle.
- More formal and symmetrical than the Foursquare - avoid off-center door placements or asymmetric massing when generating this archetype.
