# Shotgun House

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1880s-1920s; associated with working-class/industrial neighborhoods and railroad worker housing. Less iconic to the Midwest than the South, but a plausible narrow-lot infill type in older factory-town and downtown-adjacent blocks of small cities.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small cities (30,000-50,000), specifically dense older/industrial-adjacent neighborhoods; occasionally small towns with a rail line or factory history.
- **Exterior footprint**: Very narrow, 3.7-4.3m wide x 12-18m deep (12-14 ft x 40-60 ft), single-room width throughout. Small covered front porch (1.2-1.8m deep).
- **Story count**: 1 story (occasional "camelback" variant with a 2-story rear addition).
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Wood clapboard siding, simple gable roof running front-to-back (ridge perpendicular to the street), minimal ornamentation, high interior ceilings (2.7-3.4m) to compensate for narrow footprint.

## 2. Room list (the template)

Rooms arranged in a single-file line, front to back, with doors aligned on a common axis:
- Front porch (exterior, covered): 3.4-4.0m x 1.2-1.8m
- Living room / front room: 3.4-4.0m x 3.7-4.3m
- Bedroom 1 (middle room, doubles as pass-through): 3.4-4.0m x 3.4-3.7m
- Bedroom 2 (optional, second middle room in longer variants): 3.4-4.0m x 3.0-3.4m
- Dining room / middle room (alternate use of a middle bay): 3.4-4.0m x 3.0-3.4m
- Kitchen (rear): 3.4-4.0m x 3.0-3.7m
- Bathroom (rear addition, off kitchen or a small side bump-out): 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-2.1m
- Back porch/stoop (exterior): 1.5-2.1m x 1.2-1.5m

**Room count**: 3-5 rooms in a line; 1-2 bedrooms in the small variant, 2-3 in the "double shotgun"/longer variant. Bathroom is a single small retrofit. Overall footprint 55-110 sq m (600-1,200 sq ft).

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Front porch**: 1-2 chairs or a porch swing, small side table (0-1)

**Living room**: sofa (1), armchair (0-1), TV/entertainment unit or period radio hutch, coffee table (1), area rug (0-1), window unit A/C (0-1, common retrofit given no central HVAC)

**Bedroom(s)**: bed - full/queen in primary, twin/full in secondary (1), dresser (1), nightstand (0-1), small closet (built-in shallow closet or freestanding wardrobe) (1)

**Dining/middle room** (if used as such): small dining table + chairs (2-4), sideboard (0-1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + short counter run (1), few upper/lower cabinets (compact run), small table + 2 chairs (0-1 set, if room allows), trash can (1)

**Bathroom**: toilet (1), sink (1), shower stall or small tub (1), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Defining rule: all rooms are arranged in a single line, one behind the other, each with aligned doorways so a straight sightline (and airflow breeze) runs from front door to back door - the origin of the "shotgun" name.
- No hallway - each room is a pass-through to the next, meaning bedrooms are used as circulation space (a strong procedural-generation quirk to preserve: expect foot traffic through bedrooms).
- Kitchen and bathroom are always at the rear, closest to the back door/yard, for service access and later plumbing retrofit.
- Windows are placed only on the two long side walls (party-wall-adjacent lots often had none on one side); no windows on the front/back gable ends beyond the door.
- Camelback variant: a 2-story rear addition (extra bedroom(s) upstairs) sits only over the rear third of the house, keeping the street-facing massing single-story.
- Given the narrow lot, structures are typically built close together (0.6-1.5m side setbacks), reinforcing a dense streetwall on procedural blocks.
