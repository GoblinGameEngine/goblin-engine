# Cape Cod

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1930-1955 (peak late 1940s-early 1950s postwar boom); a compact, budget-friendly colonial-revival form built in huge numbers for young postwar families.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small towns (6,000-10,000) and small cities (30,000-50,000), in early postwar subdivisions and older in-town infill lots.
- **Exterior footprint**: Compact rectangle, 7.6-10.7m x 7.6-9.1m (25-35 ft x 25-30 ft).
- **Story count**: 1 to 1.5 stories (steep side-gable roof with 1-2 front dormers lighting upstairs bedrooms).
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Wood clapboard or shingle siding, minimal exterior ornamentation, steep side-gable roof, centered front door often with a simple pediment or small stoop, symmetrical window placement flanking the door.

## 2. Room list (the template)

Ground floor:
- Living room: 3.7-4.9m x 3.4-4.3m
- Kitchen (with dining space, often eat-in rather than a separate dining room): 3.4-4.6m x 3.0-3.7m
- Bedroom 1 (ground floor, original 2-bedroom plan): 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Bedroom 2 (ground floor): 2.7-3.4m x 2.7-3.0m
- Bathroom: 2.1-2.4m x 1.8-2.1m
- Small entry vestibule: 1.2-1.8m x 1.2-1.5m

Upper half-story (finished-attic dormer bedrooms, common expansion):
- Bedroom(s) (x1-2, sloped ceilings): 3.0-4.0m x 2.7-3.4m
- Small bathroom (optional, added on expansion): 1.8-2.1m x 1.5-1.8m

**Room count**: 5-7 total rooms; 2-4 bedrooms depending on whether the attic is finished; 1-2 bathrooms. Overall footprint 65-110 sq m (700-1,200 sq ft) unfinished-attic version, up to 130 sq m (1,400 sq ft) finished.

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Living room**: sofa (1), armchair(s) (1-2), TV/entertainment unit (modern) or radio console (period), coffee table (1), area rug (1), small fireplace (0-1)

**Kitchen (with dining space)**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), upper/lower cabinets (compact run), kitchen table + chairs (1 set, 4 chairs, doubles as the dining room), small appliances (1-2), trash can (1)

**Bedrooms**: bed - full in primary, twin/full in secondary (1), nightstand (0-1), dresser (1), small closet (built-in, often shallow) (1)

**Bathroom**: toilet (1), sink (1), tub/shower combo (1), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

**Attic bedrooms (if finished)**: bed - twin/full (1), dresser (1), small closet built into the kneewall (1), desk (0-1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Very compact, boxy footprint with almost no wasted circulation space; the front door often opens nearly straight into the living room with only a token vestibule.
- Original plan is frequently just 2 bedrooms and 1 bath on the ground floor; treat the finished-attic 1-2 extra bedrooms as an optional "expanded" variant to add square-footage/generation variety.
- Kitchen typically doubles as the dining space (no separate dining room) - place the kitchen table near a window rather than assuming a distinct dining room exists.
- Symmetrical front facade (door centered, windows flanking) should be reflected in the room layout: bedrooms often occupy the front corners flanking a central living room, or the living room spans the full front with bedrooms at the rear.
- Steep roof pitch allows dormered expansion later; procedurally this is a good "starter vs. expanded" toggle - base 2BR/1BA vs. expanded 3-4BR/1.5-2BA.
- Small full basement is common (regional variation) and often houses the furnace, laundry, and overflow storage.
