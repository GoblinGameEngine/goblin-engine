# Victorian / Queen Anne

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1875-1910; the "best house on the block" of its era, typically built by a town's founding merchant/banker/mill-owner families. Surviving examples cluster in the oldest, most prominent blocks of a settlement's original core.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small cities (30,000-50,000) downtown-adjacent historic districts primarily; occasionally a single standout example in a small town's oldest neighborhood (the "old money" house). Rare/absent in tiny rural villages except as a single landmark structure.
- **Exterior footprint**: Irregular massing, roughly 11-15m x 11-15m (36-50 ft x 36-50 ft) including bay windows, turrets, and wraparound porch projections.
- **Story count**: 2 to 3 stories plus often a turret/tower element and a finished or partial attic.
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Wood clapboard/shingle mixed cladding (often multiple textures per facade), steeply pitched and complex multi-gabled roofline, decorative spindlework/gingerbread trim, wraparound or partial wraparound porch, asymmetrical facade with a corner tower or turret.

## 2. Room list (the template)

Ground floor:
- Entry foyer/grand hall (with primary staircase): 2.7-3.7m x 2.4-3.0m
- Parlor / formal living room: 3.7-4.9m x 3.7-4.6m
- Family sitting room / second parlor: 3.4-4.3m x 3.4-3.7m
- Dining room (formal): 3.7-4.6m x 3.4-4.0m
- Kitchen: 3.4-4.3m x 3.0-3.7m
- Butler's pantry / servant's pantry (larger examples): 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-2.1m
- Library/study/den: 3.0-3.7m x 3.0-3.4m
- Half-bath (later retrofit): 1.5-1.8m x 1.2-1.5m

Upper floor(s):
- Primary bedroom (often with bay window/turret nook): 4.0-4.9m x 3.7-4.3m
- Secondary bedroom(s) (x2-4): 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Bathroom(s) (1-2, retrofit-era): 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.4m
- Upstairs sitting room/landing (larger examples): 2.7-3.4m x 2.4-3.0m
- Servant's room (attic level, larger examples): 2.7-3.4m x 2.4-3.0m

**Room count**: 9-14 total rooms; 3-6 bedrooms; 1-3 bathrooms (originally 0, retrofit over decades). Overall footprint 220-400+ sq m (2,400-4,300+ sq ft).

**Entry foyer**: hall tree/coat rack (1), grand staircase (structural), console table (1), area rug (0-1), decorative mirror (0-1)

**Parlor (formal)**: settee/sofa (1), armchairs (2-3), fireplace with ornate mantel (1), piano (0-1, common status item), curio cabinet (0-1), occasional tables (1-2), area rug (1)

**Family sitting room**: sofa (1), armchair(s) (1-2), TV/entertainment unit (modern retrofit) or radio cabinet (period), bookshelf (0-1), fireplace (0-1)

**Dining room (formal)**: large dining table + chairs (6-10), china cabinet (1), sideboard/buffet (1), chandelier (1), area rug (1)

**Kitchen**: stove/range (1), refrigerator (1), sink + counter run (1), cabinets, servant's call-bell box (0-1, period detail), pantry shelving (1), trash can (1) — often modernized/renovated relative to the rest of the house

**Butler's pantry (if present)**: built-in glass-front cabinets (1 run), counter space (1), china/silver storage

**Library/study**: desk (1), bookshelf(s) (2-3), armchair (1), fireplace (0-1)

**Bedrooms (primary)**: bed - queen/king (1), nightstand(s) (2), dresser (1), armoire/wardrobe (1), fireplace (0-1), sitting nook furniture in bay/turret area (0-1 chair + table)

**Bedrooms (secondary)**: bed - full/queen (1), nightstand (0-1), dresser (1), wardrobe (1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Irregular, asymmetrical massing: rooms wrap around a corner tower/turret and bay windows rather than fitting a rectangular grid - expect at least one room per floor with a non-rectangular footprint bump-out.
- Grand central staircase in the foyer is the visual and circulation centerpiece; place it prominently visible from the front door.
- Two distinct ground-floor social rooms (formal parlor for guests, informal family sitting room for everyday use) reflect Victorian social separation - keep them adjacent but distinct, often connected by pocket doors.
- Kitchen and service areas (butler's pantry) are pushed to the rear/side, historically for servant access via a separate rear service stair in larger examples.
- Primary bedroom is the largest upstairs room and often incorporates the turret/bay nook as a reading or dressing area.
- Bathrooms are almost always retrofits (indoor plumbing came later in this era) - plausible to have as few as 1 shared bath even in a large house, or 2-3 in a well-modernized example; stack them near existing kitchen/service plumbing.
- Wraparound porch is a major exterior feature - the more prominent the family/era, the more porch coverage.
