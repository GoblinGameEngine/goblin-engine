# Tudor Revival Cottage

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1910-1940 (peak 1920s-early 1930s); a picturesque, storybook-cottage style built as a step up from a bungalow but smaller/more affordable than a full Queen Anne or Colonial Revival.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small cities (30,000-50,000) mainly, in interwar-era middle-class neighborhoods; occasionally a single example in a larger small town.
- **Exterior footprint**: 9.1-11.6m x 8.5-10.4m (30-38 ft x 28-34 ft), irregular massing with a prominent front-facing gable.
- **Story count**: 1.5 to 2 stories.
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Brick or stone veneer (sometimes with decorative half-timbering in gable ends), steeply pitched cross-gabled roof, tall narrow windows often with diamond-pane leaded glass, a prominent front gable and/or small round-arched entry.

## 2. Room list (the template)

Ground floor:
- Entry vestibule (small, often with an arched opening to the living room): 1.5-2.1m x 1.2-1.8m
- Living room (often with a prominent fireplace as focal point): 3.7-5.2m x 3.4-4.3m
- Dining room: 3.4-4.0m x 3.0-3.7m
- Kitchen: 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m
- Sunroom or breakfast room (optional): 2.4-3.4m x 2.1-2.7m
- Half-bath (optional): 1.5-1.8m x 1.2-1.5m

Upper floor:
- Primary bedroom: 3.7-4.3m x 3.4-3.7m
- Secondary bedroom(s) (x1-2): 3.0-3.4m x 2.7-3.0m
- Bathroom: 2.1-2.7m x 1.8-2.1m
- Small hall/landing: 1.2-1.5m wide

**Room count**: 6-8 total rooms; 2-3 bedrooms; 1-1.5 bathrooms. Overall footprint 110-165 sq m (1,200-1,800 sq ft).

## 4. Placement notes

- Irregular, picturesque massing centered on a steep front gable - the living room typically sits directly behind this gable and often contains the home's showpiece fireplace.
- Entry is frequently off-center and modestly sized (an arched door recessed slightly), leading into a small vestibule rather than a grand hall.
- Diamond-pane or multi-light windows are narrow and vertically proportioned - place furniture (especially beds/sofas) with this in mind, avoiding wide unbroken exterior walls.
- Kitchen and service spaces are at the rear, similar to contemporary bungalows of the same era.
- Smaller and more vertically-oriented than a full Queen Anne Victorian, without a tower/turret - treat as the "modest cousin" of the Victorian archetype for procedural variety in interwar-era small-city neighborhoods.
