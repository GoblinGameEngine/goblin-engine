# Small Apartment Building (2-3 Story Walk-Up)

## 1. Overview

- **Era**: c. 1900-1930 (older brick walk-up, common near a small city's downtown/streetcar corridors) and c. 1960-1990 (simpler wood-frame walk-up, common near a small city's edges/arterial roads). Both eras share the same basic "central hall, stacked units" structure.
- **Population tier(s)**: Small cities (30,000-50,000) primarily; a single small walk-up (4-8 units) is also plausible at the edge of a larger small town (6,000-10,000) near its main street.
- **Exterior footprint**: 15.2-24.4m x 10.7-15.2m (50-80 ft x 35-50 ft) footprint, holding 6-16 units total.
- **Story count**: 2-3 stories, no elevator (hence "walk-up"), single or double-loaded central corridor per floor.
- **Exterior materials/roof**: Older: brick facade, flat or low-slope roof, single grand entry with a shared vestibule and mailboxes. Newer: vinyl/brick-veneer combo, low-pitch gable or hip roof, one or two entry points with exterior stairs/breezeways.

## 2. Unit type catalog (the template)

**Studio unit** (~10-20% of units): combined living/sleeping/kitchen area 5.5-7.0m x 4.0-4.9m (22-28 sq m / 240-300 sq ft total), bathroom 1.8-2.1m x 1.5-1.8m.

**One-bedroom unit** (~40-50% of units): living room 3.4-4.3m x 3.0-3.7m, kitchen (often galley or small L-shape) 2.4-3.4m x 2.1-2.7m, bedroom 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m, bathroom 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-2.1m. Total ~45-65 sq m (480-700 sq ft).

**Two-bedroom unit** (~25-35% of units): living room 3.7-4.6m x 3.4-4.0m, dining area (often combined with living room) 2.4-3.0m x 2.1-2.7m, kitchen 2.7-3.7m x 2.4-3.0m, primary bedroom 3.0-3.7m x 2.7-3.4m, secondary bedroom 2.7-3.4m x 2.4-3.0m, bathroom 1.8-2.4m x 1.5-2.1m. Total ~65-90 sq m (700-970 sq ft).

Shared building spaces:
- Entry vestibule/lobby (mailboxes, buzzer panel): 2.4-3.7m x 1.8-2.4m
- Central corridor per floor: 1.2-1.5m wide, running the depth or length of the building
- Stairwell(s) (1-2 per building): 1.2-1.8m x 3.7-4.6m footprint per floor
- Shared laundry room (basement or ground floor, common in walk-ups): 3.7-5.5m x 3.0-4.0m
- Basement storage lockers/mechanical room (0-1): 4.6-7.3m x 4.6-7.3m

**Unit count**: 6-16 units per building, typically 2-6 units per floor. Unit mix commonly skews toward 1-bedroom units as the plurality.

## 3. Per-room content catalog

**Studio (combined living/sleeping)**: sofa or sofa-bed (1), small TV/entertainment unit (1), compact table + 1-2 chairs (0-1 set), bed - full/queen (1, may be a murphy bed in older stock), small dresser (1), tiny kitchenette run (stove, fridge, sink) along one wall, small closet (1)

**One/two-bedroom living room**: sofa (1), armchair (0-1), TV/entertainment unit (1), coffee table (1), area rug (0-1)

**Kitchen (galley/small)**: stove/range (1, compact), refrigerator (1, compact-to-standard), sink + short counter run (1), a compact cabinet run, small appliances (1-2), trash can (1)

**Bedrooms**: bed (queen primary/only, full/twin secondary), nightstand (0-1), dresser (1), small closet (1)

**Bathroom**: toilet (1), sink (1), tub/shower combo (1), mirror (1), towel rack (1)

**Shared lobby**: mailbox bank (1), small bench (0-1), directory sign (0-1)

**Shared laundry room**: washers (2-4), dryers (2-4), folding table (0-1), coin/card machine (0-1)

## 4. Placement notes

- Units are stacked in repeating floor plates: the same 2-6 unit layout repeats on every floor (a strong procedural-generation shortcut — generate one floor template and stack it with vertical corridor/stair alignment).
- Central or double-loaded corridor is the circulation spine; units are entered directly off it, with no shared interior common rooms beyond the lobby and (optionally) a basement laundry.
- Corner units are more desirable and can get a slightly larger footprint/extra windows as a "premium unit" variant.
- Kitchens and bathrooms across stacked units are usually vertically aligned floor-to-floor to share plumbing chases — keep wet-wall placement consistent across the repeated floor template.
- Older brick walk-ups typically lack in-unit laundry (hence the shared basement laundry room); newer walk-ups increasingly include a small stacked washer/dryer closet in 2BR units instead.
- No elevator: 3rd-floor units are the least desirable/cheapest, useful as a rent-tier or vacancy-rate variable if the generator models economic status.
