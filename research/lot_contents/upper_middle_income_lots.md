# Upper-Middle-Income Lots (cross-cutting income tier)

## 1. Overview

The counterpart modifier file to `low_income_lots.md`: item-pool *additions and upgrades* that apply to an upper-middle/high-income household regardless of which era/setting file its house archetype normally belongs to. This tier correlates most strongly with **Colonial Revival, Tudor Revival Cottage, restored/well-maintained Victorian Queen Anne, larger Foursquare, and the larger end of Ranch/Split-Level**, but the modifiers below can layer onto any archetype that's been well-maintained/renovated. Use as a modifier layer on top of the relevant era/setting file's baseline (`prewar_intown_lots.md` or `postwar_suburban_lots.md`; this tier is uncommon in the rural farm-adjacent tier except as a hobby-farm/gentleman-farm variant).

## 2. General pattern

The defining signal here is **discretionary, permitted, professionally-finished amenities** and **landscaping as visible investment** — the opposite of the low-income tier's maintenance-deferral signal. Expect matched materials, intact paint/stain, mature/designed planting beds, and multiple simultaneous leisure amenities (pool + deck + firepit rather than just one).

## 3. Garage / driveway typology

- **2-3 car garage**: elevated rate (~85-95%) relative to the era/setting baseline, and 3-car garages specifically become common here (~25-35% of this tier) versus rare-to-absent on lower tiers.
- **Driveway material**: concrete or stamped/decorative concrete/pavers dominant (~60-70%); asphalt still appears but concrete or paver upgrades read as the higher-income signal; a circular or side-loading driveway configuration (garage doors facing the side yard rather than the street) becomes meaningfully more common at this tier's upper end, especially on Colonial Revival/Tudor lots, as a specific "not showing the garage door to the street" status marker.
- **Additional paved parking pad or motor court**: 15-20%, a genuinely upper-tier-only item (extra guest parking as a discretionary hardscape investment).

## 4. Outbuildings

- **Detached garage or carriage-house-style outbuilding** (in addition to or instead of an attached garage, especially on restored Victorian/Foursquare lots): 15-25%, often a converted/restored period carriage house — the one place in the catalog where the *pre-war* alley-garage pattern gets a high-income revival (a restored carriage house is a status-signaling renovation, not a budget default).
- **Detached workshop**: 15-25%, purpose-built (not a converted shed) and well-finished — hobbyist space as a discretionary investment.
- **Greenhouse**: 10-15%, the tier where a genuine free-standing glass greenhouse (rather than a cold-frame or hoop house) becomes plausible.
- **Pool house / cabana**: 5-10%, appears only at this tier, paired with an in-ground pool.

## 5. Gardens & landscaping

- **Vegetable garden**: present but often reframed as a "kitchen garden" — raised beds, defined edging, sometimes a small greenhouse/cold-frame attached — at a moderate rate (~30-40%), lower than the rural or low-income tiers' food-budget-driven rate, since this tier's gardening is hobby-motivated rather than cost-motivated.
- **Foundation plantings + designed ornamental beds**: near-universal (~95%+) and visibly more elaborate — mixed shrub/perennial beds, mulched, edged, sometimes professionally landscaped, versus the simpler shrub-only rows of the baseline tiers.
- **Mature specimen trees, formal hedge rows**: 30-40%, a genuine marker of either an older established lot (mature trees take decades) or a landscaping investment (large transplanted specimen trees).
- **Irrigation system (in-ground sprinklers)**: 20-30%, a discretionary infrastructure investment essentially exclusive to this tier.

## 6. Leisure/recreation

- **In-ground pool**: the clearest single income-legible item in the whole catalog — elevated well above the ~5-8% postwar-suburban baseline, plausibly 15-25% at this tier's upper end (national in-ground pool rate is concentrated in higher-income/warmer-climate markets, but even in the Great Lakes region an upper-middle-income lot is meaningfully more likely to have one than a middle-income lot).
- **Above-ground pool**: actually *lower* at this tier than at middle-income (~2-5%) — once in-ground is affordable, above-ground reads as a downgrade rather than an alternative, so don't stack both pool types as equally likely here.
- **Deck AND patio together**: 30-40% have both (rather than either/or as in the baseline tiers) — a multi-tier composite outdoor living space (deck off the house, separate paver patio with a firepit or outdoor kitchen further into the yard) is a genuine upper-tier landscaping trend.
- **Outdoor kitchen / built-in grill**: 10-15%, a discretionary item essentially exclusive to this tier.
- **Firepit**: built masonry/paver firepit (not an informal ring) at a high rate (~35-45%) when any firepit exists.
- **Basketball/sport court (half-court or full driveway court)**: 10-15%, a larger and more finished version of the baseline tiers' single hoop.

## 7. Vehicles/equipment stored outdoors

- Boat or RV, where present (comparable or slightly lower rate than the rural tier, since this tier is more often in-town/subdivision and subject to visibility ordinances), is more likely to be a newer model and may be paired with a dedicated RV parking pad with utility hookup — a specifically higher-income variant of an otherwise cross-tier item.

## 8. Fencing tied to yard use

- **Privacy fence around pool/patio specifically** (not the whole lot): the dominant fencing pattern at this tier (~50-60% wherever a pool or patio exists) — high-quality wood or composite privacy fencing rather than chain-link, both for pool-safety code compliance and for the discretionary "outdoor room" landscaping trend.
- **Decorative/low ornamental fencing at the front** (on restored Victorian/Foursquare lots specifically): 15-25%, a period-appropriate restoration detail rather than a functional boundary.

## 9. Utility items

- **Trash/recycling bin storage**: near-universal compliance with visibility ordinances, often in a purpose-built screened enclosure or integrated into the garage/side-yard landscaping (an enclosure structure is itself a discretionary landscaping investment) rather than just parked beside the garage.
- **Lawn ornaments/yard décor**: more restrained/curated (a single high-quality garden sculpture or fountain rather than a dense collection) — quality-over-quantity is the tier's general decor signal.

## 10. Building-to-lot relationship notes

- **`colonial_revival.md`** and **`tudor_revival_cottage.md`**: The archetypes most consistently paired with this tier's full amenity set — 2-3 car garage, in-ground pool, formal landscaping, privacy-fenced patio.
- **`victorian_queen_anne.md`** (restored/well-maintained examples): pairs with this file's carriage-house-revival and formal-garden modifiers layered onto `prewar_intown_lots.md`'s alley-access baseline — this is the one archetype where both files' garage patterns (alley-accessed AND high-income-restored) coexist rather than compete.
- **`foursquare.md`** (larger, well-maintained examples): similar restoration-driven pairing, somewhat more modest than Colonial Revival/Tudor.
- **`ranch.md`** and **`split_level.md`** (larger/upper-end examples, sometimes called "storybook ranch" or executive ranch in real-estate marketing): pair with this file's pool/deck-and-patio/outdoor-kitchen modifiers layered onto `postwar_suburban_lots.md`'s attached-garage baseline, typically with a 3-car rather than 2-car garage.
