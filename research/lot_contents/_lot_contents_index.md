# Residential Lot Contents — Index

Procedural-generation reference catalog for what's **outside** the house on residential lots in the station's Midwestern-American-style neighborhood zones — companion to `research/building_catalog/residential/` (indoor/building templates) and grounded in the same south-shore-Great-Lakes (OH/IN/MI/IL/WI) region as the rest of this research tree. Six detail files plus this index:

| File | Primary axis | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| `prewar_intown_lots.md` | Era + setting | Narrow gridded lots, rear-alley garage access, pre-1940 construction |
| `postwar_suburban_lots.md` | Era + setting | Wider curvilinear-subdivision lots, no alley, attached garage + driveway apron, 1945-1990 construction |
| `rural_farm_adjacent_lots.md` | Setting | Farmsteads and rural-village lots, unregulated/low-zoning, farmyard outbuilding cluster |
| `low_income_lots.md` | Income (modifier layer) | Reductions/substitutions applied on top of any era/setting file |
| `upper_middle_income_lots.md` | Income (modifier layer) | Additions/upgrades applied on top of any era/setting file |
| `great_lakes_climate_items.md` | Climate (cross-cutting layer) | Snow removal, storm windows, clotheslines, woodpiles, storm-prep — applies to all tiers with era/setting-specific probabilities |

**How to combine layers for the generator**: pick one era/setting file (prewar-in-town, postwar-suburban, or rural-farm-adjacent) as the base, apply one income modifier file (low-income or upper-middle-income) if the household should read as an income outlier — the "middle" of each era/setting file's own baseline needs no modifier — then layer `great_lakes_climate_items.md` on top using that lot's era for the storm-window/basement probabilities and its setting for the woodpile/snow-equipment probabilities.

## Cross-cutting findings worth calling out

- **Garage/driveway flip is the single biggest structural finding**: pre-war narrow lots hide the garage on a rear alley because they predate mass car ownership and had alley infrastructure already in place for service access; postwar lots have no alley, so the garage moved to the front facade and the driveway apron became a dominant front-yard feature. This one fact should drive most of the front-yard variation between the two era files.
- **Pool type is the single strongest income-legible item**: in-ground pool ($66k average) vs. above-ground pool ($1-6k) is a much sharper income signal than pool-vs-no-pool; national base rate is only ~8% of households (59% in-ground/41% above-ground split of that 8%), so pools should stay rare overall and the generator should weight in-ground toward `upper_middle_income_lots.md` and above-ground toward the low/middle tiers. Wherever any pool exists, sample a code-compliant self-latching fence/barrier at ~90%+ regardless of tier — this is a real, nearly-universal US safety-code requirement, not a discretionary landscaping choice.
- **Clotheslines are the clearest single income+era dial available**: highest at low-income and rural tiers (~35-45%), lowest at upper-middle-income (~3-8%), with several Great Lakes-region states (Illinois, Indiana, Wisconsin) having actual "right to dry" laws on the books specifically because wealthier HOA subdivisions tried to ban the practice as unsightly/poverty-coded.
- **Driveway material is a legible era+setting+income triple signal**: gravel dominant on rural lots (driveway length makes paving cost-prohibitive) and on the lowest-income in-town lots; concrete "ribbon strip" on pre-war alley aprons; asphalt or concrete apron dominant on postwar suburban lots (asphalt skewing cheaper/colder-climate, concrete skewing higher-status); stamped/decorative concrete and pavers exclusive to the upper-middle-income tier.
- **Vegetable gardens don't simply decline with income** — they follow a U-shape: highest at rural (~55-70%, food-production tradition + space) and low-income (~35-50%, food-budget motivation) tiers, dip at ordinary postwar-suburban middle income (~25-40%, supermarket-produce era shifted priority to lawn), then partially recover at upper-middle income (~30-40%) but reframed as a hobby "kitchen garden" with raised beds rather than a cost-driven row garden.
- **Backyard chickens are a distinct, ordinance-bound in-town exception**, separate from the farm-zone's already-catalogued chicken coop. Most Midwest municipalities that permit them cap flocks at roughly 4-8 hens, ban roosters outright, and require coop setbacks in the 10-25 ft (property line) / 25-50 ft (neighboring dwelling) range, sometimes with a small paid coop-registration/license; regulation is set city-by-city/village-by-village with no consistent state law in Ohio or Michigan specifically, so a chicken coop appearing on an in-town lot (as opposed to the rural tier, where it's essentially unregulated) should read as a larger-lot, village-edge, or specifically chicken-friendly-zoned property, not a default in-town item — sample at a low rate (~5-10%) on `prewar_intown_lots.md`/`postwar_suburban_lots.md` lots with generous side/rear yard space, well below the ~30-45% rural-tier rate in `rural_farm_adjacent_lots.md`.
- **Trash/recycling bin visibility is a real, citable municipal-ordinance topic** with numbers worth reusing directly: several Midwest cities (e.g., Bloomington, IN) specify bins may not be visible from the street/sidewalk more than 24 hours before collection and must be removed/hidden again the same day as collection; broader ordinances (e.g., Liberty, MO) require bins stored off the curb between collections. This is a good real-world justification for making bin visibility mostly a same-day/collection-day-only generator state rather than a permanently visible yard prop, except on the low-income modifier file where a realistic small compliance gap is appropriate.
- **Mailbox style is a clean three-way regional/setting signal** tying into the existing street-furniture system: wall- or porch-mounted box for pre-war in-town lots (urban delivery convention), single post-mounted curbside box for postwar suburban lots (USPS standard 41-45 in height, 6-8 in setback), and either a single roadside post box (older/established farmsteads) or a shared cluster box unit/CBU (newer rural routes, USPS centralized-delivery standard since a 1967 pilot and formalized under the STD-4C spec in 2005) for the rural tier.
- **Lawn ornaments/seasonal decor**: general web research returned little region-specific quantitative data beyond broad material-culture observation (regional norms exist — e.g., a stronger Halloween/harvest and Christmas-lighting seasonal-decoration culture is anecdotally and journalistically well attested in Midwest suburbs — but no citable percentage-style source was found). Treat lawn ornaments/seasonal decor as a **low-confidence, flavor-only** item: sample sparingly, skew toward seasonal (pumpkins/cornstalks in fall, lights in winter) rather than permanent garden statuary except at the upper-middle-income tier (see that file's "curated, quality-over-quantity" note), and do not treat the percentages in this bullet as sourced — this is the one category in the whole research set where the brief's requested numbers could not be well grounded.

## Assumptions and gaps

- Percentages throughout all six files are **estimates synthesized from the cited sources plus reasonable extrapolation**, not single-source hard statistics, except where a specific study/ordinance number is quoted directly (national pool ownership rate, pool-type split, shed permit-exemption threshold, wood-heating rates, storm-cellar/basement trend, right-to-dry state list, chicken-ordinance setback ranges). Treat all ranges as generator-tunable starting points, not ground truth.
- Multi-family archetypes (`apartment_small.md`, `apartment_garden_style.md`, `apartment_large.md`) are explicitly out of scope for this lot-contents pass — their "lot contents" would be shared site-plan features (parking lot, complex-wide landscaping/dumpster enclosure, community mailbox bank) rather than individual-household items, and would need a separate site-plan-level research pass if wanted later.
- Farm-zone content (working barns, grain bins, livestock infrastructure on active cropland) is intentionally treated as **adjacent to, not inside,** this catalog's scope per the brief's framing (backyard chickens are explicitly "distinct from the farm-zone chicken coop already catalogued") — `rural_farm_adjacent_lots.md` covers the *residential dooryard* around a farmhouse, not the working farm itself.
- No dedicated source was found for lawn-ornament/seasonal-decor regional statistics (see bullet above) — flagged as the weakest-grounded item category in this research pass.
