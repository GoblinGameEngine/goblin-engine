# Sandusky, Ohio (Erie County — county seat)

Population: 25,095 (2020 census; 24,174 est. 2024, -13.2% since 2000). Metro area 115,986. Land area 9.64–10.0 sq mi, water area 12.19 sq mi (the city's platted/legal boundary includes a large share of Sandusky Bay). Elevation 584 ft. Coordinates 41.45556°N, 82.71389°W. On Lake Erie's southern shore/Sandusky Bay, midway between Toledo (45 mi west) and Cleveland (50 mi east). Erie County seat.

## Core layout — Mid-size lake-resort/tourism-and-retiree archetype
Sandusky was founded in 1818 on the southeast shore of Sandusky Bay and incorporated as a city in 1824; the name derives from a Wyandot word meaning "water"/"cold water." The original town plat is a documented, distinctive pattern: surveyor **Hector Kilbourne** laid out **the Kilbourne Plat**, a modified grid with avenues cutting diagonally, reportedly echoing Masonic symbolism — a genuinely unusual, sourced downtown-plan geometry distinct from the standard courthouse-square or rail-spine grids used elsewhere in this research set. Downtown's historic waterfront industrial/rail zone has been substantially **redeveloped as marina dockage** (Battery Park Marina occupies a former railroad site) as 20th-century industrial/shipping activity declined — a clear "industrial waterfront → marina/tourism waterfront" conversion pattern, distinct from Avon Lake's power-plant-to-park version but the same underlying archetype (post-industrial lakefront repurposed for leisure use).

## Residential pattern
Standard platted grid off the Kilbourne diagonal-avenue plan; 13,351 housing units (2020), average household size 2.28 (2010 census). No specific lot-size data found.

## Water
**Directly water-founded, bay-and-lake-facing.** Sandusky sits on Sandusky Bay, a large drowned-river-mouth embayment of Lake Erie, giving it a sheltered harbor distinct from the open-lake-exposed towns in this set (Avon Lake, Ashtabula). This sheltered-bay siting is the geographic basis for its historic role as a Great Lakes shipping port and its present role as the **ferry gateway to the Lake Erie islands** (Kelleys Island, Put-in-Bay/South Bass Island, Middle Bass Island, North Bass Island).

## Distinctive buildings / tourism infrastructure
- **Cedar Point** amusement park ("America's Roller Coast," the second-oldest theme park in the U.S., 18 roller coasters) — the dominant economic and land-use anchor, occupying a peninsula at the harbor mouth; Six Flags Entertainment Corporation (Cedar Point's operator) is the top employer with 6,590 employees, an extraordinary single-employer concentration for a city this size.
- Adjacent resort/waterpark infrastructure: Cedar Point Shores, Castaway Bay, Great Wolf Lodge, Kalahari Resorts — a genuine cluster of large-scale seasonal-resort lodging distinct from ordinary small-town hotel stock.
- Historic house/maritime museums: Cooke-Dorn House, Follett House Museum, Maritime Museum of Sandusky, Merry-Go-Round Museum.
- Ferry terminal(s) serving the Lake Erie islands — a distinctive lakefront transit-infrastructure building type not present in inland towns.
- Battery Park Marina (former rail/industrial waterfront, now marina) — the "marina district" pattern.

## Demographics
- **Population trend**: declining, -13.2% since 2000 (25,095 in 2020 → 24,174 est. 2024) — despite the strong tourism economy, the resident population is shrinking, a useful counterpoint showing tourism revenue and resident population trend can diverge.
- **Median household income**: $51,053 (2024 est.), well below Ohio's $72,212 statewide median (was $31,133 in 2000).
- **Median age**: 36.5–39.5 years (sources vary).
- **% under 18**: 22.4%
- **% 65+**: 18.4%
- **Homeownership rate**: not isolated as a clean percentage in this pass (housing data shows 13,351 units, 2.3% owner vacancy rate — flagged gap for a clean ownership %).
- **Average household size**: 2.28 (2010 census).
- **Top employment sectors**: manufacturing (17.5%), healthcare & social assistance (14.5%), accommodation & food services (13.2% — the tourism-sector signature), retail trade (12.0%).
- **Poverty rate**: 25.6% overall (with a stark racial disparity: 19.5% white non-Hispanic vs. 34.6% Black vs. 26.1% Hispanic/Latino) — high despite the tourism economy, consistent with a seasonal/service-wage-dominated job base.
- **% bachelor's+**: not isolated numerically in this pass (flagged gap).
