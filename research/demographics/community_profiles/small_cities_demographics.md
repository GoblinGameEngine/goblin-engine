## Sources

- **Primary sources used:** Wikipedia city infoboxes/demographics sections (largely citing 2020 Decennial Census and 2016–2020 ACS 5-year estimates) for population trend, median age, age distribution (% under 18 / % 65+), average household size, and poverty rate; City-Data.com city profile pages for 2024-estimate median household income and top employment-sector percentage breakdowns.
- Where Wikipedia's income/poverty figures were stale (drawn from the 2000 Census rather than a more recent ACS estimate — this affected Michigan City, Manitowoc, Findlay, Bowling Green, and Bartlesville), both the older figure and the City-Data 2024 estimate are given, separated by "/".
- **Data gaps:** Homeownership rate and average household size were not reliably retrievable for several cities (Jefferson City, Cape Girardeau, Kearney, Salina, Fond du Lac, Michigan City, Manitowoc, Danville) — City-Data.com renders these figures via interactive/JS widgets that a text fetch could not extract, and Wikipedia often omits them. Bachelor's-degree-or-higher attainment is the weakest column overall: it was only confidently found for Mankato (37.6%), Fond du Lac (23.1%), and Hutchinson (14.4%); all other cities are marked n/a after multiple targeted lookups failed to surface a static, extractable figure. If precise values for these gaps are needed later, data.census.gov QuickFacts (blocked direct fetch access in this session — returned HTTP 403) or the Census Bureau's API would be the next source to try.
- Two cities — Mankato (Minnesota State University) and Cape Girardeau (Southeast Missouri State) and Bowling Green (Bowling Green State University) — are college towns; their unusually young median ages and elevated poverty rates are artifacts of large full-time student populations rather than general economic distress, worth noting if this data feeds into the station neighborhood's demographic tuning.
