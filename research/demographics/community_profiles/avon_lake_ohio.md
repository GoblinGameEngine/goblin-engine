# Avon Lake, Ohio (Lorain County)

Population: 25,206 (2020 census; up 11.6% from 22,581 in 2010; 26,342 est. 2024, +45.2% since 2000). Land area 11.13–11.1 sq mi, no significant inland water area (fronts directly on Lake Erie). Coordinates approx. 41°30′N 82°02′W, on Lake Erie's southern shore ~17 miles west of Cleveland, in northeastern Lorain County.

## Core layout — Growing exurban/bedroom-community archetype, directly on the lake
Avon Lake has no traditional courthouse-square or rail-corridor downtown; it developed as a **20th-century Cleveland exurb built directly along the Lake Erie shoreline**, not around a historic platted town core. The area was first settled in the 17th/early 19th century (Peter Miller House, built 1830, survives) but stayed rural/sparsely populated for over a century — 904 residents in 1920 — before exploding to over 9,000 by 1960 (when it was granted city status) and continuing to grow steadily since, reaching 25,206 by 2020. This is the clearest **"lake-adjacent bedroom-suburb growth" archetype** in the new set: population growth driven by Cleveland-metro commuting and lakefront residential desirability rather than by an internal economic engine.

## Residential pattern
Suburban post-1950s subdivision pattern typical of a Cleveland exurb; no historic platted grid core. Average household size 2.53 (2010 census; was 2.70 in 2000, a declining-household-size trend typical of maturing suburbs). Owner-occupied housing at **79.8%** (2020) — a strongly owner-dominated, single-family-home-typology suburb, notably higher than the declining rust-belt towns in this set.

## Traffic/zoning
No formal downtown to route around; commercial development is presumed corridor/strip-style along the main east-west arterials (consistent with a car-oriented postwar exurb), though no specific street-by-street data was found in this pass (flagged gap).

## Water
**Lakefront-founded, but only recently lake-oriented in its built form.** Avon Lake sits directly on Lake Erie with no water area distinct from the lake itself. For most of the 20th century the waterfront's dominant use was industrial: the **Avon Lake Power Plant**, a coal-fired plant built 1925–26 (one of the largest in the world when completed, $30M construction cost, burning 4,000 tons of coal/day by 1950, 652 MW nameplate capacity at peak) occupied a large stretch of the shoreline and drew Lake Erie water for cooling. The plant was decommissioned April 1, 2022, with final structures removed by implosion in December 2024; the city has announced plans to convert the former plant site into a **public lakefront park** — a concrete, dated example of a "industrial lakefront → post-industrial park conversion" pattern that the generator could model as a redevelopment overlay for aging lake-industrial sites.

## Distinctive buildings
- Peter Miller House (1830) — pre-industrial-era survivor.
- Former Avon Lake Power Plant site (decommissioned 2022, demolished by implosion Dec. 2024) — slated to become a public park, a strong "lakefront brownfield-to-park" case study.
- Industrial employers inland from the lakefront: Lubrizol, Ford Motor Company, PolyOne Corporation — manufacturing base coexists with the bedroom-suburb residential majority.

## Demographics
- **Population trend**: growing, +11.6% (2010→2020: 22,581→25,206); +45.2% since 2000 per City-Data (26,342 est. 2024) — the strongest growth rate found among the six new settlements.
- **Median household income**: $124,334 (2024 est.) — far above Ohio's $72,212 statewide median, and the highest income of the six new settlements by a wide margin.
- **Median age**: 44.4–45.0 years — notably older than the Ohio median (39.8) despite being a growth town, consistent with an established/maturing bedroom suburb rather than a young-family exurban frontier.
- **% under 18**: 23.1%
- **% 65+**: 21.3%
- **Homeownership rate**: 79.8% (2020 census) — the highest in this set of six.
- **Average household size**: 2.53 (2010 census).
- **Top employment sectors**: healthcare & social assistance (16.1%), professional/scientific/technical services (12.9%), plus a manufacturing base (Lubrizol, Ford, PolyOne).
- **Poverty rate**: 3.2–4.6% (sources vary slightly by vintage) — the lowest of the six new settlements by far.
- **% bachelor's+**: 49.3% — the highest of the six new settlements.
