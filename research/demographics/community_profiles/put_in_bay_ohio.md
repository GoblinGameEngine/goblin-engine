# Put-in-Bay, Ohio (South Bass Island, Ottawa County)

Population: 154 (2020 census; year-round residents only); 152 est. 2024 (+18.8% since 2000 per City-Data, though base numbers this small make percentage changes noisy). Land area 0.46–0.62 sq mi. Coordinates 41.65306°N, 82.81722°W, elevation 584 ft. Located on South Bass Island in Lake Erie, Ottawa County — 85 miles west of Cleveland, 35 miles east of Toledo. Note: the surrounding **Put-in-Bay Township** (which includes unincorporated parts of South, Middle, and North Bass Islands beyond the village itself) recorded 813 residents in 2020, up from 633 in 2010 — the village is only the incorporated core of a larger, still-small island township.

## Core layout — Extreme micro-scale seasonal lake-resort archetype
Put-in-Bay is the clearest **extreme seasonal-resort** case in the whole research set: a village of ~150 year-round residents whose built environment and economy are sized for a **tourist population that swells dramatically** during the April–October season (exact seasonal peak figures were not found in this pass, but the village's tourism infrastructure — ferry terminals, an airport, multiple large hotels/resorts historically, wineries, bars — is vastly oversized relative to its tiny resident base, the defining signature of the archetype). Incorporated in 1877, the village's founding driver was not agriculture, milling, or rail but **military/historical significance**: it served as Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry's naval base during the War of 1812, and the decisive Battle of Lake Erie (September 10, 1813) was fought just offshore, securing American control of the lake. That victory, commemorated by the 352-foot **Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial** (completed 1915), became the seed of the island's tourism identity, which then grew through the late 19th/early 20th century resort-hotel era.

## Residential pattern
Extremely small and dense relative to land area — 0.46–0.62 sq mi hosting the entire village; density figures cited (e.g., ~332 people/sq mi on a City-Data land-area basis) reflect only the tiny year-round population and dramatically understate in-season effective density. No formal residential-lot data found; village-scale layout is presumed compact/walkable, consistent with limited land and heavy tourist foot traffic.

## Traffic/zoning
Island access is by ferry and small aircraft only — no bridge to the mainland — so traffic patterns differ fundamentally from every mainland town in this research set (no highway grid, no rail spine; golf carts and bicycles are the dominant in-village transport mode per general knowledge of the island, though this specific detail was not independently re-verified in this pass — flagged as a likely-true but unconfirmed detail).

## Water
**Entirely and exclusively water-founded/water-dependent.** The village exists only because it sits on a Lake Erie island; its founding, economy, and infrastructure (ferries, marinas, airport) are 100% water/lake-mediated in a way no mainland settlement in this research set can match. This is the strongest possible example of the "founded because of the lake" driver category.

## Distinctive buildings
- **Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial** (352 ft, completed 1915) — the island's dominant landmark and a National Park Service site.
- **Perry's Cave** and **Heineman's Winery/Crystal Cave** (claimed to be the world's largest geode) — tourist-attraction infrastructure built around natural karst features.
- **Stonehenge Estate** — historic tourist estate.
- Former **Hotel Victory** (opened 1892, once one of the largest resort hotels in the world; burned in 1919 — only foundations remain) — a vivid example of boom-era resort-hotel infrastructure that didn't survive, a useful "ruin/remnant" detail for a resort-town archetype.
- Minimal year-round civic infrastructure: one K-12 school, one grocery store, one bank, no hospital — despite the large seasonal tourist economy, underlining how thin the permanent-resident-serving infrastructure is relative to the tourist-serving infrastructure (marinas, bars, hotels).
