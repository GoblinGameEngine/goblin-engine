# South Haven, Michigan (Van Buren County)

Population: 3,964 (2020 census), confirming the brief's approximate figure (~4,309 was slightly high; 2024 ACS estimate is ~4,017). **Fits the villages tier (500–5,000)**, toward its upper end. Lake-Resort/Tourism archetype: a Lake Michigan port town that converted from timber shipping to fruit farming to a full resort economy. Land area 3.41 sq mi. Coordinates roughly 42°24′N 86°16′W.

## Founding/water
South Haven sits directly at the mouth of the **Black River** on **Lake Michigan** — "because of its position on Lake Michigan, at the mouth of the Black River, South Haven has always been a port city." Incorporated as a village in 1869 and as a city in 1902. The town's economic arc is a clean three-stage Great Lakes pattern: (1) 19th-century timber shipping port, (2) conversion of cleared forestland to fruit orchards (peaches, apples, and especially blueberries — South Haven brands itself the "Blueberry Capital of the World," with a Blueberry Festival dating to 1970), (3) an early-1900s pivot to a lake-resort economy, when the town built "theaters, a casino, an opera house, an amusement park, and many resorts" to serve Chicago- and Midwest-origin lake tourism. The South Haven South Pierhead Light (1903) still marks the harbor mouth and remains publicly accessible from the beaches.

## Downtown layout
Downtown's spine is **Phoenix Street**, running from near the Black River harbor inland; the historic core is centered on the corner of **Phoenix and Center Streets**, where the town's first hotel (the 1853 Forest House) stood before being moved a few doors south, where it still stands, "though greatly modified." Civic/cultural buildings on Phoenix Street include the 1891 Scott Club (designed by John Cornelius Randall) and the 1906 Carnegie library, which has since been adaptively reused as the **South Haven Center for the Arts** — a pattern of civic-building reuse for arts/culture uses that recurs across the resort-town archetype (paralleling Saugatuck's arts-colony identity below). Downtown sits between the harbor/river mouth and the inland residential grid, the same river-mouth-plus-open-lake two-part water edge documented in the Manitowoc, WI profile, but scaled to resort/tourism use rather than industrial shipbuilding.

## Residential pattern
The single most distinctive figure in South Haven's housing data is a **45.6% housing-unit vacancy rate** (2020 census: 3,560 total units, only 1,935 occupied) — this is *not* the derelict/abandoned-building vacancy of a declining industrial town (see the Benton Harbor profile for that contrast) but overwhelmingly **seasonal/vacation-home vacancy**: lakefront and near-lake property is heavily second-home-owned by non-resident families who occupy it only in summer, consistent with median home values ($508,196, 2024 est.) far above what a year-round population this size and this income level would otherwise support. This is the key generator-relevant "lake resort" residential signature: a large stock of high-value, low-occupancy waterfront housing layered over a smaller, older, lower-income year-round population.

## Distinctive buildings
- South Haven South Pierhead Light (1903), harbor-mouth lighthouse, publicly accessible from the beach.
- Scott Club (1891) and 1906 Carnegie Library, both on Phoenix Street — the library now houses the South Haven Center for the Arts.
- The original 1853 Forest House hotel building, relocated a few doors south of its original Phoenix/Center corner, still standing in modified form.

## Demographics
- Population trend: declining, −10.0% 2010→2020 (4,403 → 3,964); longer arc peaked at 6,471 in 1970 and has fallen steadily since — typical for a small resort town whose year-round population has hollowed out even as seasonal/vacation demand (and property values) rose.
- Median household income: $66,753–$68,239 (2024 ACS est.)
- Median age: 61.0–61.7 (2024 est.) — very old for the tier, reflecting both an aging year-round population and retiree in-migration.
- % under 18: 16.2% (2020 Census)
- % 65+: 32.7% (2020 Census)
- Homeownership rate: 70.7% (2024 est.; note this figure counts second/vacation homes as "owned," so it overstates conventional owner-occupancy relative to a non-resort town)
- Average household size: 2.26 (2020 Census)
- Top employment sectors: Manufacturing, Accommodation & Food Services, Professional/Scientific/Technical Services (2024 ACS)
- Poverty rate: 15.8% (2024 est.)
