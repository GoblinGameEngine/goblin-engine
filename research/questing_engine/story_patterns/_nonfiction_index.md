# Nonfiction Story-Pattern Index

Synthesis of `nonfiction_*.md` — real, documented nonfiction narrative patterns (journalism,
longform reporting, books, local-news beats) mapped to the 6 community demographic archetypes
defined in `../../demographics/_demographic_archetypes.md`. Sourced via a mix of directly verified
web research (WebFetch against primary/encyclopedic sources — books, Wikipedia summaries of
verifiable facts, named research institutions) and established general knowledge of the nonfiction/
journalism landscape. Every pattern file cites named real sources; nothing here is invented.

This file is the "what recurs everywhere vs. what's archetype-unique" cross-reference — read it
before pulling individual patterns into a quest-template synthesis pass, so cross-archetype
patterns get varied treatment per archetype rather than reused verbatim.

## Cross-archetype patterns (same story shape, different meaning per archetype)

These recur across 3+ archetypes but should NOT be treated as one reusable template — the
economic mechanism, stakes, and building/location cast differ enough per archetype that a quest-
template synthesis pass should treat each archetype's version as materially distinct.

### "Single-institution dependency" — the town's fate rides on one entity
- **Ag/Manufacturing** (§2 single-plant dependency): a strong-but-not-dominant employer at 15-35%
  concentration; anxious-but-stable, not yet collapsed.
- **Rust Belt** (§1 factory closure via private equity): the same dependency *after* the entity has
  already failed or been hollowed out by an absentee financial owner (Glass House/Anchor Hocking,
  Janesville/GM).
- **College Town** (§2 small-college closure): the highest single-sector employment concentration
  of any archetype (19-29%), so the "single institution" here is an educational rather than
  industrial anchor, and its closure is a sudden institutional death rather than a gradual layoff.
- **County Seat** (§1, §4): government itself is the single institution, but because it's public
  (open records, elections) the recurring story is accountability/corruption journalism rather than
  economic-collapse journalism — the institution is stable, unlike the other three variants.
- **Note:** Mount Pleasant, Iowa is simultaneously a named example of the Ag/Manufacturing *and*
  County-Seat archetypes in `_demographic_archetypes.md`, and its real-world Iowa Wesleyan closure
  is documented in the College Town file — a genuine real-world case of one settlement plausibly
  rolling into more than one archetype's story-pattern list depending on which institution a quest
  generator foregrounds.

### "Outside money bids up local housing, displaces locals"
- **Growing Exurban** (§1, §6): commuters/newcomers buying into farmland-turned-subdivision;
  driver is permanent household formation.
- **Lake Resort** (§1, §2, §7): second-home buyers and short-term-rental investors; driver is
  seasonal/vacation demand, and the displaced group is specifically the hospitality workforce that
  the tourism economy itself depends on — a sharper irony than the exurban version.
- **College Town** (§3): student-renter demand ("studentification"); driver is academic-calendar-
  cyclical, and the displacement pressure resets and reapplies every school year rather than being
  a one-time wave.

### "Water infrastructure becomes a crisis"
- **Rust Belt** (§3): caused by fiscal collapse and deferred maintenance (Benton Harbor lead
  crisis) — a story about municipal poverty.
- **Lake Resort** (§4): caused by environmental/tourism-economy threat (Lake Erie algal blooms
  near Sandusky) — a story about the resource the whole local economy depends on.
- **Growing Exurban** (§7): caused by growth outpacing utility capacity — a story about success,
  not failure.
- Same building (`water_tower`, `town_hall`) can host three structurally opposite causal stories
  depending on archetype — useful if a generator wants to vary tone (crisis-of-poverty vs.
  crisis-of-abundance vs. crisis-of-environment) while reusing a location type.

### "School enrollment crisis" (opposite directions)
- **Ag/Manufacturing** (§3): slow consolidation from long-term mild decline.
- **Rust Belt** (§9): forced closures from acute fiscal collapse.
- **Growing Exurban** (§2): overcrowding and bond votes — the *opposite* direction (too much
  growth, not too little).
- **Lake Resort** (§5): funding referenda failing because an aging, mostly-childless electorate
  votes down levies.
- The same building type (`school`) generates four distinct, archetype-appropriate story tensions
  purely from which demographic trend is driving it — a good single-location, high-reuse case for
  a generator.

### "A longtime local business closes / Main Street identity is threatened"
- **Ag/Manufacturing** (§9): framed as generational succession/"end of an era," not economic
  alarm.
- **Rust Belt** (§5): framed as terminal decline evidence (dead mall, vacant storefronts).
- **Growing Exurban** (§5): framed as a contested retail market (chain competition, not vacancy).
- **County Seat** (§7, via news-desert lens): framed as an accountability-infrastructure loss.
- **Lake Resort** (§6): framed as routine seasonal-survival stress, not decline.
- Same surface event ("the shop closed"), five different emotional registers depending on
  archetype — arguably the single most reusable-with-variation pattern found in this research.

### "Political/electoral realignment story"
Present as explicit texture in Rust Belt (§8) and as implicit texture (local-election stakes,
retiree-vote dynamics) in County Seat (§4) and Lake Resort (§5); present only faintly elsewhere.
Treat as Rust-Belt-primary with two secondary echoes rather than a fully cross-archetype pattern.

## Archetype-unique patterns (no real equivalent found elsewhere)

- **Rust Belt only:** private-equity ownership churn as the specific closure mechanism (§1);
  state/emergency-financial-manager municipal takeover (§4); Superfund/industrial-waterfront
  contamination left un-redeveloped (§6, contrast with Lake Resort's redeveloped waterfront).
- **Ag/Manufacturing only:** the 1980s farm-debt-crisis template and its modern echoes (§1); rural
  meth epidemic per *Methland* (§7) — distinguished explicitly from Rust Belt's opioid epidemic by
  its agricultural/meatpacking-consolidation driver rather than deindustrialization.
- **County Seat only:** courthouse-square preservation as its own genre (§2); rural county-jail
  conditions reporting via the Marshall Project (§3); news-desert research as a *primary* story
  (elsewhere it's a secondary lens).
- **College Town only:** town-gown conflict as a named, centuries-old conflict genre (§1); campus
  free-speech controversy spillover (§5); athletics-driven seasonal economy (§4).
- **Lake Resort only:** short-term-rental regulation fights (§2); H-2B seasonal-labor shortage
  (§3); ferry/transportation-dependent micro-economy at the Put-in-Bay extreme (§8); art-colony
  gentrification as a distinct sub-pattern of general displacement (§7).
- **Growing Exurban only:** HOA/covenant disputes (§6); "bedroom community" civic-engagement
  deficit as a named sociological critique (§8, via Putnam's *Bowling Alone* framing).

## Most game-relevant single finding

The **"single-institution dependency"** cross-archetype pattern (factory / college / county
government / farm economy) is the most directly reusable finding: it is the same underlying quest
shape — a town's fortune tied to one entity whose health can rise, fall, or be threatened — that
recurs in 4 of 6 archetypes with a different institution type and a different real-world
documentation trail each time (Glass House/Janesville for factories, Iowa Wesleyan for colleges,
ProPublica-style investigations for county government, the 1980s farm-crisis literature for the
ag economy). A quest-generation system could parameterize a single "institution health" mechanic
and vary only the institution type, source flavor, and building cast per rolled archetype.
