# Fiction/Folklore Story-Pattern Index

Synthesis index for the three fiction/folklore research files in this directory. Covers
established, citable American literary and folklore traditions about small-town/rural
Midwest/Great-Lakes life, decomposed into structural patterns a procedural quest generator can
draw on. Note: this directory is also being populated by a parallel research effort on
**nonfiction** story-pattern sources (local history, journalism, oral-history archives, etc.) —
at the time of writing, no nonfiction files were yet present in this directory, so the table
below cross-references only the three fiction files. Re-check this directory for
`nonfiction_*.md`-style files before treating this index as exhaustive.

Files in this set:
- `fiction_smalltown_literary_tradition.md` — Anderson, Lewis, Keillor, Cather, Wilder
- `fiction_gothic_horror_folklore.md` — Jackson, King-template, rural noir/grit lit, ruin-porn
  genre, haunted-building/escaped-patient legends
- `fiction_regional_folklore.md` — lake monsters, campus ghost stories, town-gown, county fair
  folklore, summer-people-vs-locals

## Archetype coverage summary

- **Richest literary/folkloric material:** **Stable Agricultural/Manufacturing Service Town** —
  it is the direct or default setting for patterns 1, 3, 4, 6, 10, and 14 (six of fifteen), which
  tracks with the archetype file's own finding that this is "the single most common archetype in
  this dataset." Anderson, Lewis, Keillor, Cather, and Jackson are all substantially or wholly
  set in towns of exactly this demographic shape.
- **Thinnest literary/folkloric material:** **Growing Exurban/Bedroom Community** — appears only
  as a partial/secondary reskin target for pattern 2 (booster-satire, newcomer-vs-established
  friction) and has no dedicated source tradition of its own in this research pass. This mirrors
  the archetype file's own finding that Growing Exurban was "under-sampled" in the original
  demographic research (only one confirmed example, Avon Lake OH) — the literary tradition gap
  and the demographic-data gap point the same direction: this archetype is the newest/least
  culturally mythologized of the six, since post-war/2000s-era bedroom suburbs simply haven't
  accumulated the multi-generational folklore depth that older farm towns, industrial towns,
  county seats, college towns, and resort towns have. A generator populating a Growing Exurban
  settlement should expect to lean on cross-archetype patterns (1, 5, 10) more than
  archetype-specific ones.
- **Declining Rust-Belt Industrial** is the second-richest, and notably the *only* archetype
  with a dedicated genre-level pattern about how outsiders narrate the town itself (pattern 9,
  ruin-porn) — worth flagging to the generator as a place to build in explicit
  narrator-perspective/tone options (exploitative-tourist gaze vs. resident-respectful framing)
  rather than picking one framing by default.
