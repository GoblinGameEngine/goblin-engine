# Interior Props Master List — Procedural Map Generator

Deduplicated, categorized master list of every distinct 3D model needed to populate
the interiors of all 49 building archetypes (17 residential + 32 commercial/civic/farm)
plus the 7 demographic "diagnostic items." This is the union across all archetypes —
each entry is ONE model to build, not one per building.

Priority legend used below: **[UNIVERSAL]** = appears in most/all residential archetypes or
is otherwise an extremely high-reuse model — build first. **[VARIANT]** = flags where the
demographic framework (income tier, era, household structure) requires more than one visual
version of the same model. No flag = single model, reused as-is across every archetype that
calls for it.

## Methodology

**Source files read (50 total, complete coverage, no sampling):**
- All 17 files in `research/building_catalog/residential/` (apartment_garden_style, apartment_large,
  apartment_small, bungalow_craftsman, cape_cod, colonial_revival, duplex, farmhouse_vernacular,
  foursquare, mobile_home, ranch, rowhouse_townhouse, shotgun, split_level,
  starter_home_minimal_traditional, tudor_revival_cottage, victorian_queen_anne) plus its
  `_catalog_index.md`.
- All 32 files in `research/building_catalog/commercial_civic_farm/` (auto_repair_shop, bank_branch,
  barber_salon, barn_dairy, bar_tavern, chicken_coop, church, clothing_store, corn_crib, courthouse,
  diner_cafe, farm_equipment_shed, fire_department, gas_station, general_store, grain_elevator,
  grain_silo, hardware_store, hotel_inn_small, library, movie_theater, pharmacy_drugstore,
  pole_building_machine_shed, police_station, post_office, restaurant, school, small_factory_mill,
  small_grocery, town_hall, warehouse, water_tower) plus its `_catalog_index.md`.
- `research/interior_contents_why/residential_demographic_framework.md` for the 7 new diagnostic
  items (chest freezer, home gym equipment, religious/cultural iconography, multi-TV kids' rooms,
  plastic slipcovers, gun cabinet/safe, hobbyist workshop buildout).

**Non-interior / no-content archetypes noted but excluded from the pool extraction:** `water_tower.md`
and the bulk storage volumes of `grain_silo.md` and `grain_elevator.md` are flagged in their own
source files as "no walkable interior" / "non-furnished bulk volume" — their few enterable fixtures
(silage auger, truck scale, leg mechanism, valve house) are still captured under Industrial/Farm
Equipment above, but the archetypes contribute no seating/table/storage items.

**Deduplication approach:**
- Treated as the **same model** (one list entry) when the pools clearly describe the same physical
  object under different room-context labels — e.g. "kitchen table + chairs," "breakfast nook table,"
  and "eat-in table" all collapsed into one **Kitchen/breakfast table + chairs (compact)** entry
  distinct from the separate **formal Dining table + chairs** entry (a genuinely larger/different
  object in archetypes that have both, e.g. Ranch, Colonial Revival).
- Treated as **variants of one base model** (noted with a `[VARIANT: ...]` tag) rather than separate
  entries when the difference is scale, era-styling, or income-tier finish/condition — e.g. one
  "bed frame" model family with twin/full/queen/king proportions and a worn-vs-upscale material set,
  rather than 8 separate bed meshes. This follows the demographic framework's own instruction that
  income/era mostly modify *condition, quality descriptor, and count*, not the underlying item list.
- Treated as **genuinely distinct models** when the room/function context changes the object's form
  factor materially even though the name is similar — e.g. residential "sofa" vs. commercial "booth
  seating" vs. "church pew" vs. "theater seating row" are four different meshes despite all being
  "seating," because their geometry, scale, and fixed/freestanding nature differ.
- Retail/civic "counter" objects were split by function (checkout counter, teller counter, reception
  desk, dispensing counter, service counter) rather than merged into one generic "counter," because
  each carries distinct built-in geometry (register nook, teller cage, raised platform, PO-box
  backing, etc.) that a modeling team would build as different assets in practice.
- Explicitly excluded from the countable list: pure architectural/structural elements called out in
  the source files as non-furnishing (staircases, fireplaces-as-structure, built-in cabinetry noted
  as "attached to walls," dust curtains, non-walkable bulk-storage volumes) and bulk/set-dressing
  fill that is really a material or instancing concern rather than a distinct mesh (loose hay,
  canned-goods fill quantity, ear corn fill level) — these are noted inline where relevant but not
  counted as separate models.

## Findings

- **Grand total: 265 distinct items** across 13 categories.
- **Largest categories:** Retail/Commercial Fixtures (40), Industrial/Farm Equipment (30) and
  Small Props/Decor (30, tied), Civic/Institutional Fixtures (23), Appliances (22).
- **Near-universal items (build first):** sofa, armchair, coffee table, dining/kitchen table +
  chairs, bed frame, nightstand, dresser, refrigerator, stove/range, kitchen sink, toilet,
  bathroom sink/vanity, tub/shower, mirror, towel rack, TV + entertainment stand, cash register,
  bookshelf, and generic retail shelving all appear in a large majority of their respective
  residential or commercial archetype sets — these carry the highest reuse multiplier per model
  built and should be prioritized, along with their income/era material variants, ahead of any
  archetype-specific one-off (pew, altar, jury box, silo auger, etc.).
