# 2. History: from weather hulks to the Skein

The canonical timeline is `canon_sky.HISTORY`, written to sky_islands.json → `history`. It is fitted to the bible's
history (research/bible/03_history.md). This page explains the choices.

## Why the islands exist at all: the Steward's cloud engines
- **VY 212, the Last Snow.** After the Kettle Freeze, the Steward ends snowfall on the floor for the water balance.
  This event is already canon.
- **VY 214, the Hundred.** The Steward does not stop making precipitation the efficient way, as ice in cold cloud. It
  moves the cold up:
  - it launches a hundred **cloud engines**: foamed-stone platforms under great cylindrical envelopes, which the user
    calls "weather balloons";
  - each carries a sealed **Box** that chills and seeds the cloud layers;
  - the floor gets rain, and the snow stays aloft.
- To the floor they are "the weather hulks", part of the sky for sixty years. Tenders crawl over them, and every few
  years the Steward lowers each one to the **Mooring Fields** for service.
- This explains:
  - why there are exactly a hundred of them;
  - why they ride at cloud height;
  - why they have rock bottoms and cylindrical balloons;
  - why snow falls only on them;
  - why the Steward tolerates people on them: the Boxes still run, and the people keep the hulks flying.

## The Boardings (VY 273–362)
- **VY 271: the Third Lockdown and the Regression** (canon). About 3,200 people die in the decade after. In the Gray
  Years, people look up at the hulks, still serviced and still well.
- **VY 273, the First Boarding.**
  - The Wardens are being dissolved (canon, VY 274), and forty-one families of Warden lines know the hulks' service
    routine.
  - They climb the tethers of a hulk moored near Bellhaven and refuse to come down.
  - The Steward is bound to prevent death (canon: `steward.duty`). It will not shake them off, and it will not leave
    them to freeze on a hulk at the Mooring Field.
  - So it **raises the hulk to its height with them aboard** and stops servicing it. The Box keeps running, and the
    people may keep the rest.
  - That hulk is **Halyard** (SKY-001). The Steward's bargain is never spoken, but every island keeps it: *keep the hulk
    flying and leave the Box alone.*
- **VY 275–301, the Boardings.** Each boarding group becomes a clan:
  - Gray Years families;
  - Returner families after the Reconciliation (VY 280);
  - debtors;
  - Waymakers who wanted the sky;
  - Solano, Côte and Polish-Lakes families;
  - cannery workers.
- **VY 301:** the Mooring Fields close, and the Steward stops lowering the hulks. From then on, **the only way up is a
  line.** For more than a century the Aloft seldom went down, because coming back up was hard: until VY 417 it meant
  riding a container up a hoist line. The moorings changed that (VY 418 on).
- **VY 318, the First Lashing.** Halyard and Bowline are tied together by ropes fired from spring guns. The lashing
  breaks on the fourth day in a quarrel over water, and the three-day custom begins.
- **VY 350, the Lockout.** The Floor is set. The Aloft lose little because they had little, and they gain trade: the
  floor now needs what they make.
- **VY 362, the Hundredth Boarding.** The last hulk is taken, by children of other islands sent to found it, and is
  named **Ragamuffin**.
- **VY 371:** the Skein is named at a raft of nine, and the knot keepers set down the **Five Knots**.

## The Long Calm and the Present Age
- **VY 388, the Great Tangle.**
  - Seventeen islands lashed for a jubilee meet a squall line. The raft twists and lines part.
  - Piggledy, Higgledy's sister island, is torn loose, holed on Tumbledown's rim, and falls into the North Sea with
    212 people. Its wreck is still on the seabed: a dungeon, if wanted.
  - This leads to **the Ten Knot**: no raft of more than ten islands.
- **VY 405, the Gift of the Air** (canon: the Chutes release the floor's first aerostats). The Aloft trade for them
  within a year and strip them for parts.
- **VY 417, the first lark.** Topgallant's wrights build a two-person aerostat from Gift parts, skycloth and a winch
  motor. It climbs from the floor to an island in about four minutes. The plumbs stop riding containers.
- **VY 418, the Mooring Compact.** Every town keeps a mooring ground beside its weekend flea market, and the islands
  moor there to trade. Traders' Landing at Harrow Falls is the first.
- **VY 431, the first gondolas:** Andean-style jig-back cabins on track ropes, crewed by the new **Lines**. Within a
  decade every island keeps a mooring crew of twenty-four, and coming down becomes routine.
- **VY 446:** Fathom and Dogwatch start the **air post**.
- **VY 452, the first Line Rows:** settlers rebuild the old cannery streets behind Solana Point's harbour in their
  islands' styles. The South Shore becomes the Lowliners' heartland.
- **VY 471, the Lark Quarrel.** The Aerostat Pilots' Guild tries to bar larks from the Kettle Cup, and the Aloft win
  three of the next five.
- **VY 474, the Settlers' Shares Act:** island-born settlers receive a birth share after two years' registration.
  Until then the Registry had refused them shares, a wound still felt.
- **VY 486, the Full Book:** the weight books fill. The knot keepers agree that each family's children choose the Line
  or the Ground, and since then about half go down, some 1,500 a year.
- **VY 489, the Draw Housing Ruling:** settlers queue for Draw homes like everyone else. The South Shore's poorer
  streets resent it, and the Line Halls call it fair.
- **VY 500:** the Launch Day Notice reaches the Skein by dove in two days, and the knot keepers call a raft to talk of
  it. This is a hook.

## How long is that?
From the First Boarding (VY 273) to the present (VY 500) is 227 years: about **eight or nine generations** aloft.
That is long enough for:
- a dialect and a customary law;
- clan identities, and divergent customs from island to island;
- a mild, visible physical drift. Growing up at 0.89 g, with some selection for people who thrive aloft, gives slightly
  taller, lighter-built people. That is plausible over this time, but small (see 03).

It is not long enough for real speciation. The Aloft are a culture, not a different people.

## Fit with the bible
- Uses the existing events last_snow, third_lockdown, wardens_dissolved, the_reconciliation, the_lockout,
  gift_of_the_air and aerostat_guild, the factions, and the Steward's duty, and contradicts none of them.
- **Population:** the bible's census, 249,612 in VY 499, counts **the floor**. The Registry has never counted the
  Aloft: the Charter's Registry offices are on the floor, and the Aloft never signed. The Skein's own weight books give
  ~97,000. This is an open item for the user (see the report): the canon now makes the ship ~347,000 people.
