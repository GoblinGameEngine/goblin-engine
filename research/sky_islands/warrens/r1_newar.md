# newar: the Newar city of the Kathmandu valley (Bhaktapur, Patan, Kathmandu: Asan, Indra Chowk)

## Sources and examples
- **Newar architecture:**
  - Brick houses usually 4 storeys (2–5 storey range, upper storeys smaller).
  - Hip roofs receding in tiers on struts.
  - Carved wooden windows as the signature, with different window types per floor.
  - Courtyards (chowk or baha) at the heart of the house.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newar_architecture · https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Newar_window ·
    https://nepaltraveller.com/sidetrack/the-timeless-beauty-of-traditional-newari-homes
- **Bhaktapur:**
  - The Peacock Window; the 55 Window Palace (1754, latticed windows instead of glass).
  - Continuity and change in Durbar Square.
  - https://museums.cam.ac.uk/story/continuity-and-change-in-the-durbar-square-at-bhaktapur-nepal/ ·
    https://www.aaa.com/tripcanvas/attraction/55-window-palace-VA22577
- **Hiti (stone water spouts):**
  - Sunken stepped basins with carved makara spouts; Nag Pokhari (a pond inside the hiti with a serpent).
  - Ponds with spouts in their walls; jahru (stone reservoirs).
  - Pati, mandapa and sattal rest-houses beside them.
  - 389 spouts in the valley in 2010, 233 still in use.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanthu_Darbar_Hiti · https://www.backpackandsnorkel.com/Nepal/Day4/5412n-LunHiti/ ·
    https://www.bhaktapur.com/legacy-of-nepal-the-lho-hiti-stone-conduits/ · https://www.kvptnepal.org/project/tusa-hiti
- **Kathmandu (Indra Chowk, Asan):**
  - Six streets meet at Indra Chowk, the old India–Tibet trade route.
  - Goods are displayed on walls and temple plinths.
  - Tangled telephone, internet and electricity wires (the Ratopati report); undergrounding plans.
  - Whitewashed stucco houses rebuilt after the 1934 earthquake.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indra_Chowk · https://english.ratopati.com/story/73142/barbed-wire-on-the-streets-of-kathmandu-photos ·
    https://nepaltraveller.com/sidetrack/indrachowk

## Generator grammar
- **Cell and storeys:**
  - Grid 2.4 m (Newar rooms are small and low).
  - Storeys 2.1–2.4 m (very low!), the top floor 2.6 m under the roof.
  - Houses 3–5 storeys; frontages 4–8 m.
  - Courtyard blocks (bahal/baha) 15–30 m square.
- **Walls:**
  - **Fine red brick** `#a4472f`, `#b3533a` with very thin mortar joints.
  - The face brick of rich houses is wedge-shaped (dachi apa) and glazed in parts.
  - Plinths of stone.
  - Black-brown **carved timber** frames `#3a2a20`.
  - E3 houses whitewashed stucco or raw concrete, pastels.
  - Weathering: moss on roofs and brick, rain darkening, earthquake cracks with timber props (since 2015).
- **Mass:**
  - Terraced row houses wall to wall, forming a perimeter round **courtyards** (bahal: monastery courts with a
    shrine and chaitya stupas in the middle) that are linked by passages through the houses.
  - The town is a network of courts and squares, not just streets.
- **Streets:**
  - main streets 4–6 m paved in brick (herringbone), linking squares diagonally;
  - lanes 2–3 m;
  - **passages** (low tunnels 1.8 m high) through house ground floors into courtyards;
  - squares at every junction with a temple, a well or hiti and a pati.
- **Growth:**
  - E1: Malla-era palaces, temples and bahals.
  - E2: rows densify; floors added; roof terraces.
  - E3: concrete floors on top in pink, blue and cream; flat concrete roofs replace the tiles; shopfronts.
  - E4: neon in the bazaars (Asan), screens on concrete add-ons, prayer-flag LED strings, wires.
- **Openings (the mark): the carved window.**
  - Each floor has its own window type:
    - ground: shop openings with wooden shutters and a raised plinth;
    - 1st: small latticed tiki jhyal (fine lattice);
    - 2nd: the **sa jhyal** / three-part window (san jhyal), with carved lintels extending sideways like wings
      (tunala), 2.5–4 m wide;
    - the top projecting **bay window** (a gajhu-like balcony window) of lattice.
  - Window sills and lintels extend past the opening.
  - The carved timber reads black against red brick.
- **Roofs:**
  - houses: pitched (hip and gable) roofs of small **clay tiles** `#7a4a35`, with wide eaves on carved **struts**
    (tunala) at 45°;
  - temples: **multi-tiered pagoda roofs** (2–5 tiers, each smaller), with gilded finials, bells on the eaves and
    carved deity struts;
  - **shikhara** stone temples (Krishna Mandir, Patan) as a second temple type;
  - E3 flat roofs with water tanks and laundry.
- **Ornament:** carved wood (struts with deities, lintels, toranas: carved arches over doors), copper repoussé,
  bells, brass, eyes of the Buddha on stupas, prayer flags.
- **Public spaces:**
  - **Durbar Squares** (palace squares crowded with temples on stepped plinths);
  - neighbourhood squares (tole) with **dabali** platforms (raised brick platforms for dances and gatherings);
  - **pati / sattal** rest houses (open timber pavilions);
  - **bahal** courtyards;
  - **pokhari** ponds;
  - pottery squares (pots drying across the paving: Bhaktapur).
- **Water:**
  - **hiti** (sunken stepped stone spout basins, 2–6 m below the street, with makara spouts);
  - **pokhari** (big ponds: Siddha Pokhari, Nag Pokhari);
  - wells (inar);
  - **jahru** stone tanks.
- **Plants:**
  - **peepal** and **banyan** at squares;
  - **bamboo** clumps;
  - **rhododendron** (red flowers), **jacaranda** (purple) in Kathmandu;
  - **marigold** and **sayapatri** in pots and garlands;
  - **chilies and corn hung drying** from the windows;
  - **orange/mandarin** trees in courts;
  - moss and ferns on roofs;
  - **banana** plants.
- **Cyberpunk overlay:**
  - The carved windows become glowing lattices (amber).
  - Pagoda eaves lined with LED bells.
  - **Prayer-flag LED strings** criss-crossing between houses (the strongest motif).
  - The **wire tangles** of Asan as dense as Delhi.
  - Neon Devanagari and Newari-script signs.
  - Screens on the concrete add-ons; gilded finials pulsing gold.
- **Night:** low warm streets, glowing lattice windows, temple pagodas lit tier by tier, squares with butter-lamp
  LEDs, prayer-flag lights overhead.

## Kit
- **Common pieces:**
  - `wall_panel` (fine red brick + stone plinth), `window_lattice` (`screen_lattice` Newar preset), `bracket_corbel`
    (strut preset at 45°);
  - `roof_hip_tile` (clay, small tiles, wide eaves on struts), `roof_pagoda_tier` (multi-tier temple),
    `shikhara_spire` (stone temple);
  - `covered_alley` (low passage), `bench_platform` (dabali), `pati` (an open pavilion: a `roof_hip_tile` on
    `column_round`);
  - `hiti_sunken`, `pond_tank`, `stupa` (chaitya: a dome with a spire);
  - `festoon` (prayer-flag preset), `cable_bundle`, `sign_board`;
  - peepal, bamboo, rhododendron, banana.
- **Parameters that make it Newar:**
  - red brick and black timber;
  - very low storeys (2.2 m);
  - every floor a different carved window type, wider as you go up;
  - tiled pitched roofs on struts (the only pitched-roof family in r1);
  - pagodas on squares every 50–100 m;
  - courtyard networks linked by tunnels;
  - sunken hitis and big ponds.
- **Unique pieces (4):**
  - `newar_window_sanjhyal` (a three-part window with winged lintel);
  - `pagoda_tier` (a temple tier with struts and bells; may be `roof_pagoda_tier`);
  - `hiti_sunken` (stepped basin + makara spout);
  - `stupa` (chaitya).
