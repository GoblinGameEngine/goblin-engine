# Kowloon Walled City: the fused block

**Mark:** One continuous 10-14 storey mass with no gaps: 350 buildings fused into a single block, its alleys tunnels under it, its roof a separate village of aerials, tanks, ladders and children, an aircraft skimming overhead.

Sources: Kowloon Walled City, Hong Kong (c.1945-1993)

## How it grew
- 1847: a Qing fort with a stone wall 4 m high and 4.6 m thick round ~2.6 ha (the yamen at its centre).
- 1943: the wall pulled down for Kai Tak's runway; 1947 ~2,000 squatters in one-storey huts.
- 1950: a fire destroys 2,500 huts; rebuilt as 2-4 storey brick and concrete.
- 1960s-70s: developers combine plots and pour 10-14 storey reinforced-concrete towers without permits, each new tower built hard against the last, some on top of older ones; light only from the gaps between towers.
- late 1970s-80s: maximum size, the block fused; upper floors widen out over the alleys; caged balconies; rooftop huts, pigeon lofts and tanks; the roof a separate public level crossed by ladders and planks.
- Kai Tak's approach 800 m south capped it at 13-14 storeys: the roofline is almost flat -- one plateau.

## Generator grammar
- **Cell:** 3.0 x 3.0 m; storey 2.7 m; 10-14 storeys.
- **Walls:** raw/rendered reinforced concrete, unpainted or once-painted; tile spandrels; patched; 0.2 m thick; palette #8e8a80, #a9a497, #6f6c63, #c2bba9, #5d6b66, #b8a27a; weathering: black water streaks under every sill and pipe, rust bleed from cages, moss at the base, blotchy repaint patches.
- **Mass:** Fill the plot almost completely; towers of 4-12 m frontage abut on every side; every storey above 3 steps out 0.3-0.8 m over the alleys; a single near-flat roof plane at 13-14 storeys (+/- 1). Light wells: a few 2-4 m shafts punched down through the mass.
- **Streets:**
  - perimeter street (outside the mass): 8 m (the open edge; dentists' signs face it)
  - main alley (named, through the block): 2.0 m (covered for most of its length by the floors above; fluorescent tubes; open drain)
  - branch alley: 1.2 m (dead ends, steps, doglegs every 10-20 m)
  - upper passages: 1.0 m (corridors and stair links between towers at many levels: one can cross the city without touching the ground)
  - roof level: - (ladders and planks between roofs that differ by 0-2 m)
- **Growth, in layers:**
  - ground (oldest): 2-4 storey brick shops, workshops, temples (Tin Hau, Fuk Tak), the yamen courtyard; wells [brick, stone, timber]
  - towers: 8-12 storey concrete slab towers, 1-2 flats a floor, open stairs [concrete, tile, steel window frames]
  - overgrowth: caged balconies, wider upper floors, infill over alleys, tower-to-tower bridges, pipes and cables everywhere [steel mesh, pipe, plywood]
  - roof village: huts, pigeon lofts, water tanks, TV aerial forest, clothes lines, a playground [corrugated iron, timber, fibreglass tanks]
  - cyberpunk (Aloft): LED strips down the alley ceilings, signs crammed at every ground door, screens, cable trays, dot-matrix boards, rooftop antennas turned dishes [LED, polycarbonate, fibre]
- **Openings:** Small sliding steel-framed windows, most behind welded grilles or cages; louvres; AC boxes through walls; no ornament but signs.
- **Roofs:** Flat concrete roof slabs at slightly different heights, parapets 1 m, tanks on stilts, aerials 2-6 m.
- **Ornament:** None architectural; the signs are the ornament: dentists, doctors, fishball factories, temples.
- **Public spaces:** Almost none inside: the yamen courtyard, two temples, a few widened alley junctions; the roof is the plaza. On an island: keep the dense block but frame it with a broad paved perimeter street, a market square and a pond garden outside one face (the park that replaced it).
- **Water:** Wells inside (77), standpipes; open drains down alleys; drips from every pipe.
- **Street hierarchy:** perimeter street 8 m (paved, the market edge) > main alleys 2 m (tunnels) > branch alleys 1.2 m > upper passages 1 m > roof ladders

## Foliage
- Ficus microcarpa (Chinese banyan), self-sown: roots strangling walls and roof edges, 1-6 m, roof parapets, old wall faces, light-well bottoms (#3f6b3a)
- potted plants (bougainvillea, jade, chillies, aloe): clusters of pots and polystyrene boxes, 0.3-1.5 m, roof edges, cage balconies, ledges (#4f7f3d / #c4307a)
- the park round it (perimeter): Cantonese garden: willows, bamboo, pond, rockery, 3-10 m, the green frame outside the block (#5c8a4a)

## Cyberpunk overlay
- fluorescent tube runs along alley ceilings
- pipe and cable bundles 20-60 per alley
- projecting signs at every ground door (dentists, clinics, noodle makers)
- cage balconies lit from inside
- rooftop dish forest
- LED strip edges on the roof plateau
- **Night:** Dark mass with thousands of small lit windows; cold fluorescent green-white in the alleys; warm tungsten in flats; red/blue sign glow only at the edges and alley mouths; aircraft-warning lights on the roof plateau corners.

- `pipe_run`: vertical down every 2nd bay, horizontal along alley ceilings

- `antenna`: roof, 3-8 per tower
- `rooftop_shack`: roof, 20-40% of roofs

- `plank_bridge`: over light wells and roof gaps
- `laundry_line`: cage fronts and roofs
- `sign_blade`: every ground door, stacked 2-3 high
- `sign_board`: door heads
- `light_shaft`: 2-4 m shafts through the mass
- `covered_alley`: 1.2-2 m, roofed by the floors above

Pieces unique to this family:
- new: cantilever_step (an upper storey stepped 0.3-0.8 m over an alley: the overhang band)
- new: tower_seam (the joint where two towers meet: a vertical patched strip with pipes)
- new: roof_playground (a fenced rooftop court with a swing and a basket)

Parameters that make the common pieces read as this civilization:
- proportions: tall narrow towers 4-12 m wide, 10-14 storeys, one flat roof plane
- rhythm: irregular 3 m bays, no alignment between neighbours (each tower its own storey heights: 2.6-3.0 m)
- depth: grilles and cages make the facade 0.6-0.9 m deep; alleys roofed over
- colours: concrete greys, faded tile pastels, rust
- materials: concrete, mesh, corrugated iron on the roof
