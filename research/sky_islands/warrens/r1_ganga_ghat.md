# ganga_ghat: the river-ghat sacred city (Varanasi/Kashi; comparatives Maheshwar, Pushkar)

## Sources and examples
- **UNESCO tentative list, Varanasi riverfront:**
  - A 6.5 km stretch of ghats built at different times, each with 40–60 stone steps.
  - Octagonal raised platforms for public use; smaller rectangular ones near the water for Brahmins' rituals.
  - Stone ghats from the 14th century; immersion, rituals, festivals, cremation.
  - https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6526
- **The riverfront skyline:** massive sandstone palaces and steep steps, mostly built by Maratha and Rajput rulers in
  the 1700s–1800s. The west bank is crammed with buildings almost to the water; the galis wind inland.
  https://odessia.com/destinations/india/varanasi · https://english.onlinekhabar.com/varanasi-a-nepali-experience-in-the-timeless-city.html ·
  https://footlooseinme.com/varanasi-part-i/
- **Chet Singh Ghat palace:**
  - Red sandstone, mid-18th century, with chhatris, parapets, turrets and loggias.
  - https://www.kupi.com/en-ae/explore/india/varanasi/chet-singh-ghat ·
    https://dcp-production-container.library.cornell.edu/catalog/ss:3860268
- **Man Mandir Ghat palace (1600, Man Singh of Jaipur):** https://penang-traveltips.com/india/varanasi/man-mandir-ghat.htm
- **The galis (Banaras ki galiyan):** narrow lanes behind the ghats. https://www.onmanorama.com/news/nomadic-insights/2026/03/02/benaras-ancient-city-exploration-nomadic-insights.amp.html

## Generator grammar
- **Cell and storeys:**
  - Grid 3.0 m.
  - Storeys 3.2 m.
  - Riverfront palaces 4–8 storeys rising from the steps (the tallest at the waterline, as a cliff).
  - Gali houses 3–5 storeys on 4–8 m frontages.
- **Walls:**
  - **Chunar sandstone** in buff, grey and pinkish (`#c8b08a`, `#b9a07c`, `#c99d82`) on the palaces and ghats.
  - Plastered brick in the city: limewash and distemper in faded colours (saffron `#e59a3a`, sky blue, pink,
    lime green, yellow), often painted with slogans, gods and ads.
  - Weathering: flood lines (monsoon silt bands on the lower 3–10 m), soot, moss, peeling paint.
- **Mass:**
  - **The cliff**: the city meets the river as a continuous wall of palaces and temples, each standing on its own
    flight of steps.
  - Behind it, a dense gali warren of tall narrow houses and shrines.
  - Temples (shikhara spires) poke up everywhere.
- **Streets:**
  - **Galis** 1–2 m (often under 1 m), winding, with steps; shrines in wall niches; cows.
  - A few bazaar streets 3–5 m inland.
  - The **ghats** as the great public promenade: a 5–30 m wide band of steps and landings along the whole
    waterfront, linked continuously.
- **Growth:**
  - E1: ghats and palaces built by princely patrons.
  - E2: houses fill in behind; upper floors bridge the galis; small temples in every gap.
  - E3: concrete hotel floors on top, painted ads, wires.
  - E4: floating platforms and LED aarti rigs.
- **Openings:**
  - palace facades: **jharokhas**, arcaded loggias, chhatris and turrets (Rajput and Maratha forms);
  - gali houses: small balconies with iron railings, colourful wooden doors, shrine niches.
- **Roofs:**
  - flat terraces, **chhatris** on palace corners;
  - the **nagara shikhara** spires of temples (curvilinear towers, some gilded: the Kashi Vishwanath gold spire);
  - rooftop laundry and monkeys.
- **Ornament:**
  - carved brackets, temple carving, painted murals;
  - **marigold garlands** (orange and yellow) and **prayer flags/pennants** (saffron) everywhere.
- **Public spaces:**
  - **the ghats** (with **bamboo umbrellas**: large straw-and-bamboo parasols over the priests' wooden platforms,
    the iconic Varanasi skyline element);
  - the octagonal platforms;
  - the evening **aarti** stage (a row of priests on platforms facing the river);
  - temple courts;
  - small chowks with a peepal tree and a shrine.
- **Water:**
  - the **river** itself, which on an island becomes the island lake;
  - wells and kunds (stepped temple tanks, e.g. Durga Kund);
  - boats moored at every ghat.
- **Plants:**
  - **peepal** and **banyan** with shrines at their roots (red threads tied round the trunks);
  - **neem**;
  - **bel** and **ashoka** at temples;
  - **tulsi** in pots on raised stands;
  - **marigold** fields and garlands;
  - lotus in the kunds.
- **Cyberpunk overlay:**
  - The aarti fire is canon-banned (no flame), so it becomes LED lamp-stands and hologram lamps.
  - Ghat steps with light strips.
  - Floating lantern drones.
  - Temples' gilded spires with LED gold.
  - Bamboo umbrellas with fairy lights.
  - Painted facade ads as animated murals.
  - Galis full of glowing shrine niches.
- **Night:** a river of floating lamps (LED diyas), the ghats lit warm, aarti stages blazing gold, temple bells,
  dark galis with shrine glows.

## Kit
- **Common pieces:**
  - `wall_panel` (sandstone ashlar; painted plaster), `ghat_steps` (wide, long flights with landings);
  - `jharokha`, `arcade_pier` (loggia), `chhatri`, `round_tower` (turret), `shikhara_spire` (temple tower);
  - `wall_shrine` (niche), `bench_platform` (priest platform), `ghat_umbrella` (a big bamboo parasol);
  - `pond_tank` (kund), `festoon` (marigold and pennant strings), `cable_bundle`, `sign_board` (painted wall ads);
  - peepal, banyan, neem, tulsi stand.
- **Parameters that make it Varanasi:**
  - the island's lake edge is a continuous stepped ghat with a palace cliff of 4–8 storeys behind it;
  - galis of 1–2 m;
  - every wall painted a faded colour;
  - shikhara spires every 30–60 m;
  - marigold and saffron accents;
  - umbrellas on the steps.
- **Unique pieces (3):**
  - `shikhara_spire` (a nagara curvilinear temple tower, with or without gilding);
  - `ghat_umbrella` (a bamboo parasol on a pole over a wooden platform);
  - `flood_band` (a weathering decal: silt line).
