# mughal_walled: the North Indian Mughal walled city (Shahjahanabad/Old Delhi, Lahore Walled City, Old Dhaka)

## Sources and examples
- **Shahjahanabad street hierarchy (a study SW of the Jama Masjid):**
  - Four tiers: bazaar roads → public streets → narrow residential lanes → dead-end alleys.
  - The kucha, gali and katra each name a neighbourhood block; neighbouring kuchas form a mohalla.
  - https://architexturez.net/node/239588 · https://architexturez.net/doc/10-3130/jaabe-7-217
- **Sahapedia:**
  - Mohallas were homogeneous closed units, semi-private, with courtyard houses gated off.
  - Mohallas were named after their dominant haveli or a trade; chowks formed where secondary streets met.
  - https://www.sahapedia.org/shahjahanabad-physical-vis-vis-socio-cultural-space
- **Sitaram Bazaar:** big stone-clad haveli entrances near the main roads lead to larger courtyards; plainer, smaller
  ones lie deeper in. https://sarsonkekhet.in/2015/12/11/shahjahanabad-ix-sitaram-bazaar-road-area/
- **Chandni Chowk wires:**
  - Hanging wire tangles were installed decades ago for lower loads and are now a fire risk.
  - Lanes are too narrow to fix them without closing shops.
  - Bangles glitter under naked bulbs; side alleys are under 1.5 m.
  - https://www.alamy.com/a-tangle-of-utility-wires-and-cables-above-the-streets-in-chandni-image150932683.html ·
    https://cait.in/soon-a-solution-for-the-hanging-electric-wires-in-the-streets-of-chandni-chowk-relief-from-the-threat-of-short-circuits-and-fires-praveen-khandelwal/ ·
    https://pad.ma/HA · https://calogero.us/2007/10/12/chandi-chowk-old-delhi/
- **Lahore Walled City:**
  - Havelis look small outside but open onto courtyards with frescoes, balconies and hammams; most are Sikh- and
    Hindu-era.
  - Delhi Gate havelis: Haveli Barood Khana was the Basant kite hub.
  - Narrow streets, tall havelis, bazaars in winding lanes; Wazir Khan Mosque (tile and fresco).
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore · https://thefridaytimes.com/15-Jan-2016/spirits-and-fading-glories ·
    https://www.graana.com/blog/haveli-barood-khana-a-living-chronicle-of-lahores-heritage/amp/ · https://icms.lums.edu.pk/?p=688
- **Old Dhaka and Panam:**
  - Indo-Saracenic merchant townhouses in local brick with wrought-iron balconies, arches, columns and friezes.
  - Houses compete in the richness of their street facades.
  - Old Dhaka's crammed burnt-sienna streets; rickshaw art.
  - https://asianews.network/bangladeshs-panam-a-city-where-history-still-whispers/ ·
    https://thewire.in/culture/sonargaon-panam-nagar-ruins-bengal-history · https://www.thedailystar.net/news/tranquility-of-the-past ·
    https://csmonitor.com/The-Culture/2024/0606/bangladesh-dhaka-rickshaws-art

## Generator grammar
- **Cell and storeys:**
  - Grid 3.0 m.
  - Storeys 3.4 m (haveli ground 4 m).
  - Houses 2–5 storeys.
  - Haveli courtyards 6–15 m (large havelis have several courts).
  - Old Dhaka: very narrow deep houses with 3–5 m frontages (est.).
- **Walls:**
  - **Lakhori brick** (thin Mughal brick) in lime, with plaster: Delhi pinks and ochres `#d4a07c`, `#c98b6b`,
    whitewash.
  - **Red sandstone** `#a65a43` and white marble on the monuments.
  - Lahore: brick with **fresco and kashi tile** panels.
  - Dhaka: brick in burnt sienna `#9c5a3c` and umber, ornate stucco, iron.
  - E3 concrete in pastels.
  - Weathering: monsoon black streaks, plaster loss exposing thin brick, green damp near the ground.
- **Mass:**
  - **Havelis**: inward courtyard mansions with a gated entrance (deorhi, a bent entry).
  - **Katra**: a gated commercial-residential court.
  - **Shop-house rows** on bazaars.
  - All packed wall to wall, with frontages of 4–8 m on lanes.
- **Streets (hierarchy, from the studies):**
  - **Bazaar arteries**: Chandni Chowk 20–30 m historically with a central canal; Lahore's bazaars 4–8 m.
  - **Secondary streets**: 3–5 m between mohallas.
  - **Galis**: 1.5–3 m.
  - **Kuchas and katras**: gated dead ends, under 1.5 m to 3 m.
  - **Chowks** at junctions.
  - Overhead: balconies, **chhajjas** (stone or timber sunshades on brackets, 0.6–1.0 m deep) at every floor, and
    the **wire net** (tens of cables per span).
- **Growth:**
  - E1: Mughal monuments and havelis.
  - E2: havelis subdivided among dozens of families and workshops; courtyards infilled; floors added.
  - E3: concrete storeys on top, iron grilles, shops cut in, signage, wires.
  - E4: LED signage up whole facades; illuminated wire nets; rooftop glass pavilions for kite festivals.
- **Openings:**
  - **Jharokha**: a projecting closed balcony on carved stone brackets with cusped arches and a curved bangla
    roof, 1.0–1.6 m wide, 0.6–0.9 m deep.
  - **Chhajja** over every opening row.
  - Haveli gateways: tall cusped-arch portals in red sandstone with carved wooden double doors (with spikes).
  - Windows: cusped or round arches with **jali** (stone or wooden lattice) or wooden shutters.
  - Lahore: **projecting wooden balconies** (jharokha in wood, carved, painted).
  - Dhaka: wrought-iron balconies, Venetian shutters.
- **Roofs:**
  - flat terraces with parapets (pierced jali parapets), **chhatris** on corners (small domed pavilions on 4 posts);
  - **bangla** curved roofs on balconies;
  - the monuments: white marble or red sandstone **bulbous domes** (only on mosques and tombs), slender minarets
    with chhatri tops;
  - rooftops: kite flyers (Basant), water tanks, pigeon lofts (Old Delhi kabootarbazi), laundry.
- **Ornament:**
  - carved sandstone brackets and cusped arches;
  - stucco floral panels;
  - fresco (Lahore);
  - kashi tile (Wazir Khan);
  - Dhaka's iron filigree;
  - painted signage.
- **Public spaces:**
  - **chowks** (Chandni Chowk itself);
  - the Jama Masjid court on its raised plinth with great steps (a vast paved platform: the model);
  - the **canal-lined bazaar axis** (Chandni Chowk's original Nahr-i-Bahisht canal, as the island's grand street
    with a water channel down the middle);
  - gardens (Mughal char bagh: Roshanara, Shalimar in Lahore);
  - **baolis** (stepwells).
- **Water:**
  - the central street canal with pools at chowks (the original Chandni Chowk had a reflecting pool where moonlight
    shone: the name);
  - stepwells;
  - hand pumps and sabils;
  - garden channels.
- **Plants:**
  - **peepal** and **banyan** at chowks and shrines (huge);
  - **neem** in the galis;
  - **Ashoka** (columnar), **gulmohar**, **jamun**;
  - Mughal gardens: cypress, plane, fruit trees, rows of mango;
  - **tulsi** pots, marigold garlands;
  - Lahore: banyan and **mango** groves at the edge, **kikar**;
  - Dhaka: **coconut palm**, **banana**, **mango**, **kadam**, water hyacinth in ponds (pukur).
- **Cyberpunk overlay:**
  - The **wire nets** are the signature: dense cable canopies over every lane, lit with fairy lights and LED tags.
  - Facades papered with stacked lightbox signs in Devanagari, Urdu, Bengali and English.
  - Jharokhas retrofitted as glowing pods.
  - Rooftop kite festivals with LED kites.
  - Rickshaw-art panels on shutters (Dhaka).
  - Cycle-rickshaw-like pods; festival lights (Diwali, Eid strings) permanent.
- **Night:** bulb-lit bazaars glittering with bangles and sequins, dark galis with tube lights, a firefly canopy of
  wires, chowk shrines under banyans lit with oil-lamp LEDs, and the white marble domes moonlit.

## Kit
- **Common pieces:**
  - `wall_panel` (thin brick + plaster, pastel), `jharokha` (`bay_window_box` preset with bangla roof), `chhajja`,
    `bracket_corbel`;
  - `arch_opening` (cusped, Mughal), `screen_lattice` (jali star and hex), `door_studded` (haveli gate),
    `gate_portal`;
  - `balcony_iron` (Dhaka), `gallery_timber` (Lahore wooden balconies);
  - `chhatri`, `parapet_screen` (jali parapet), `dome_onion` (monuments only), `minaret_small` (with chhatri top);
  - `cable_bundle` (dense net preset), `sign_board`, `lightbox`, `shopfront_shutter`, `pigeon_loft`, `water_tank`;
  - `falaj_channel` (canal preset with chowk pools), `stepwell` (via `ghat_steps`);
  - peepal, banyan, neem, ashoka.
- **Parameters that make it Mughal-walled:**
  - chhajja lines at every floor;
  - jharokhas at 1st–2nd floor every 6–10 m;
  - cusped arches;
  - gated katras and dead-end kuchas;
  - the densest overhead wire net;
  - facades signboard-covered to the 2nd floor;
  - pinks and ochres with red-sandstone monuments and marble domes;
  - a great canal axis with chowk pools.
- **Unique pieces (3):**
  - `bangla_roof` (a curved hut-roof cap for jharokhas and pavilions);
  - `cusped_arch` (an `arch_opening` profile, if missing);
  - `wire_net_canopy` (a cable-bundle mesh that tiles over a lane).
