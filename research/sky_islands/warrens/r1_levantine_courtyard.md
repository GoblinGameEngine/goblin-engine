## Generator grammar
- **Cell and storeys:**
  - Grid 3.0 m.
  - Ground floor 4.0–5.0 m (high reception rooms); upper floors 3.0 m.
  - Houses 2–3 storeys; courtyards 8–20 m square.
- **Walls:**
  - **Ground floor:** limestone `#d9cdb4`, `#cbbd9e` and **ablaq** banding (basalt `#3b3a38` and limestone, in
    courses 0.25–0.4 m) on courtyard facades and portals; street walls are plain rubble with lime render.
  - **Upper floors (Damascus):** timber frame with mud-brick infill, lime-plastered in white or pale ochre
    (`#efe6d4`, `#e2cfa8`).
  - **Jerusalem:** all masonry of dressed pale-gold limestone (`#e3d5b3`) with cross vaults.
  - **Weathering:** plaster cracks showing the timber grid, rust stains, grey soot on stone.
- **Mass:** introverted courtyard houses. Each block is a cluster of 4–12 houses reached from a single cul-de-sac
  (darb) whose gate can close.
- **Streets:**
  - Arterial suqs: straight-ish (Damascus keeps the Roman Straight Street, Via Recta, 5–8 m), covered with barrel
    vaults or timber and tin.
  - Secondary lanes 2–3 m; cul-de-sacs 1.2–2 m, branching like a tree.
  - **Jettied upper floors (qasr, the overhanging room)** project 0.5–1.5 m over the lane on timber brackets and
    raking struts. Opposite jetties nearly touch, turning lanes into tunnels.
  - **Sabats** bridge cul-de-sacs.
  - Jerusalem has stairs and long arcaded vaulted streets.
- **Growth:**
  - E0: the Roman/Hellenistic grid fossilised in the suqs.
  - E1: courtyard houses.
  - E2: upper floors jettied, extra rooms on roofs, courtyards subdivided among heirs, more dead ends, sabats.
  - E3: concrete upper floors, iron balconies, corrugated roofs over the suqs.
  - E4: suq vaults become LED canopies; light jettied pods stacked on the old timber jetties.
- **Openings:**
  - **Street:** small grilled windows, one door with an ablaq-framed arched portal (muqarnas hood on rich houses).
  - **Upper jetties:** rows of wooden-shuttered windows with lattice (Damascene mashrabiya is lighter than
    Cairo's).
  - **Courtyard:** big windows, the liwan arch, stained glass (qamariyat), carved stone frames.
- **Roofs:**
  - Damascus: flat mud-and-timber roofs with parapets, much clutter (tanks, dishes).
  - Jerusalem: **a domed roofscape** of shallow stone domes and cross-vault caps, walkable, with low parapets.
  - Mosques have ribbed domes and slim octagonal minarets.
- **Ornament:**
  - ablaq stripes, carved stone and inlaid marble around courtyard fountains and the liwan;
  - painted wood ceilings ('ajami);
  - the outside is deliberately plain.
- **Public spaces:**
  - the Great (Umayyad) Mosque court (sahn), a big paved square;
  - covered suqs; khans (caravanserais) with domed courts;
  - hammams; small squares at gates.
  - The game adds grand paved axes on the decumanus.
- **Water:**
  - a fountain in every courtyard (octagonal or square bahra);
  - street fountains (sabil) at corners;
  - Damascus' Barada canals feed the houses.
- **Plants:**
  - courtyards: **bitter orange and lemon** (4–6 m rounded crowns of dark leaves and orange dots), pomegranate,
    fig, **grapevine on trellis**, **jasmine** climbing walls (white flowers), Damask rose, potted basil;
  - lanes: an occasional fig or vine bridging overhead;
  - Jerusalem: olive and cypress in rooftop gardens and courts, rosemary.
- **Cyberpunk overlay:**
  - Jettied timber floors repaired with acrylic and LED-backlit lattice.
  - Suq vaults strung with festoons and data cables.
  - Hologram-like calligraphy at mosques.
  - Rooftop domes host dishes and antennae.
  - Courtyard fountains are lit from within.
  - Signage in the suq: lightboxes above each stall.
- **Night:**
  - The lanes are dark, with pools of warm light under the jetties.
  - Courtyards glow green and gold through the gates.
  - Suqs are bright tunnels of shop light.

## Kit
- **Common pieces:**
  - `wall_panel` (stone, ablaq option; timber-frame plaster), `band` (ablaq);
  - `jetty_upper` + `bracket_corbel` (raking struts);
  - `bay_window_box` (light lattice oriel);
  - `arch_opening` (pointed liwan arch; portal with muqarnas hood), `door_studded`;
  - `sabat_bridge`, `covered_alley`, `vault_barrel` (suq) + `skylight_dome`;
  - `dome_small` / `cross_vault_roof` (Jerusalem), `minaret_small` (octagonal);
  - `fountain_basin`, `vine_trellis`, citrus trees.
- **Parameters that make it Levantine:**
  - plain street walls, rich courtyards;
  - ablaq on portals;
  - jetties 0.8–1.5 m every second storey over the lanes;
  - ground floors taller than uppers;
  - a pale stone and white plaster palette with black ablaq;
  - courtyards every 12–20 m with a fountain and citrus.
- **Unique pieces (2):**
  - `ablaq_band` (two-tone course; may already exist);
  - `muqarnas_hood` (portal head; exists in components_a).
