# R3: Ottoman timber towns (Istanbul's Fener-Balat and Zeyrek, Safranbolu, Plovdiv, Berat)

**Island culture:** a stone ground floor carries timber-framed upper storeys that project over the lane on angled brackets. Bays (*cumba*, *erker*) push further out still, until opposite houses almost touch. Windows run in long rows; Berat is "the city of a thousand windows". Inside, rooms ring a central hall (*sofa*, *hayat*). Houses climb the hill so the windows stack one over another. The mark is the "upside-down wedding cake": every storey wider than the one below.

## Examples
- Safranbolu: half-timbered houses with infill between the timbers; no windows on the street at ground level (garden walls are continuous with the house front).
  - The upper floors project on all sides on brackets of differing lengths. The hayat panelling hides the brackets, so the upper mass seems to float.
  - The *sofa* is a central hall linking the rooms.
  - Sources: [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/614), [Turkish Ministry of Culture](https://ktb.gov.tr/EN-113773/samples-for-the-safranbolu-houses.html), [grandsirkeci](https://grandsirkeci.com/articles/traditional-turkish-houses-of-safranbolu)
- Istanbul: Zeyrek's fabric is mostly timber houses, built that way because of the seismic fault, and given up after fires.
  - Fener and Balat have two-storey timber houses built end to end, with the *cumba* (a small enclosed oriel) for watching the street.
  - Sources: [ITU research](https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/traditional-timber-houses-of-istanbul-heritage-values-and-preserv/), [Wooden İstanbul (DAI)](https://en.iae.org.tr/Publication/Wooden-Istanbul/143/7), [goturkiye](https://goturkiye.com/istanbul/fener-and-balat-neighbourhoods)
- Plovdiv: Revival houses with symmetry around an oval central hall and corner rooms. Bay windows and "gossip rooms", painted facades, upper floors leaning over the cobbles ("upside-down wedding cake"). [Bulgarian Ministry of Tourism](https://tourism.government.bg/bg/tourist-destinations/2799/5759), [inyourpocket](https://www.inyourpocket.com/bulgaria/plovdiv/articles/national-revival-architecture), [gallivance](https://gallivance.net/2016/02/02/upside-down-wedding-cakes-and-gossip-rooms-bulgarian-revival-houses/), [Raycheva paper](https://aup-journals.rtu.lv/article/download/aup.2012.003/48)
- Berat, Mangalem: two-storey houses with red tile roofs and big timber windows, climbing to the castle so the windows appear stacked. "The city of a thousand windows". UNESCO 2008. [khaleejtimes](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/lifestyle/arts/travel-a-passage-through-berat-central-albania-unveils-unique-traditions-and-architectural-forms?amp=1), [meer](https://www.meer.com/en/112420-what-lies-beyond-berats-famous-windows)
- Mostar: Ottoman frontier town (15th–16th century), stone roofs on Kujundžiluk (the coppersmiths' bazaar). [UNESCO doc](https://whc.unesco.org/document/154662), [viatravelers](https://viatravelers.com/neighborhoods/old-town-mostar/)

## Generator grammar
- **Cell:** 3.5–4 m bays. The ground floor is stone, 3.0 m; timber upper floors are 2.8–3.0 m. 2–4 storeys. Each upper floor projects 0.6–1.2 m on angled brackets, and the cumba a further 0.8 m.
- **Walls:**
  - Ground: rubble stone 0.6–0.8 m.
  - Upper: timber frame (*hımış*) with lath-and-plaster or brick infill, rendered and painted, or clapboard (Istanbul).
  - Palette: ochre `#d9a441`, Ottoman red `#9a3b2e`, blue `#4f74a8`, sage `#8fa58a`, white `#f0ece2`; timber brown `#5a3d28`, silvered timber `#8a8a80`.
  - Roofs: Turkish tile `#b6553a`, or stone slabs `#6f6c66` (Mostar, Gjirokastër).
  - Weathering: silvered grey clapboard, sagging lines, paint peeling to timber.
- **Mass:** detached or terraced houses on sloping plots, each with a walled garden or court at the back. Upper floors swell to square off irregular plots over crooked lanes. Long rows of windows wrap the corners.
- **Streets:**
  - Lanes 2.5–4 m, cobbled, bending with the contours.
  - The overhangs close the sky to 1 m in places.
  - Steep stepped lanes up to the castle.
  - The bazaar street (*çarşı*): rows of 3×4 m shop cells with folding shutters.
- **Growth:**
  - Layer 0: a Byzantine or Roman stone base and a castle (*kale*) on the summit.
  - Layer 1: Ottoman stone ground floors.
  - Layer 2: timber upper storeys and cumbas.
  - Layer 3: 19th-century Revival painted facades and symmetrical mansions.
  - Layer 4: 20th-century concrete infill.
  - Layer 5: cyberpunk.
- **Openings:** long ranks of tall timber sash windows (0.7×1.6 m, 4–8 per facade), *kafes* lattice screens on some, and the cumba (an enclosed 3-sided oriel). The door is in the courtyard wall.
- **Roofs:** low hip roofs at 25° with wide eaves (1.0–1.5 m) and soffits, tile; stone slabs in the Balkan variant. Chimney pots.
- **Ornament:**
  - Carved brackets.
  - Painted eave soffits (Plovdiv sunbursts).
  - Plaster mouldings framing window rows.
  - Fountain niches (*çeşme*) on street corners.
- **Public space:**
  - The *çarşı* bazaar street.
  - A mosque or church square with a plane tree and a fountain.
  - Coffee-house terraces.
  - Walled gardens.
- **Water:** *çeşme* street fountains, a *şadırvan* (ablution fountain) in the mosque court, a pond garden.

## Street hierarchy
- **Çarşı (main market street):** 5–7 m, roofed in parts.
- **Residential lanes:** 2.5–4 m.
- **Stepped lanes:** 2 m.
- **Squares:** at the mosque or church with a plane tree, the castle forecourt at the top.

## Plants
- Oriental plane (*çınar*): huge, 20–25 m, the shade tree of every square.
- Linden.
- Walnut and mulberry in the gardens.
- Grape-vine pergolas.
- Roses, hollyhocks and geraniums on the window sills.
- Cypress in the cemeteries.
- Fig and quince.
- Ivy on the stone ground floors.
- Wisteria on the timber.

## Cyberpunk overlay
- Cumbas as glass-and-LED pods, each a little glowing room over the lane.
- Timber frames sistered with steel and carbon.
- Clapboard skinned in solar shingles.
- Drone perches on the brackets.
- The bazaar under a neon-tube vault.
- **Night:** rows of warm windows wrap the corners (`#ffc46b`); light falls through kafes lattices in patterns. Brackets are uplit from below, so the overhangs read as floating bands of light.

- **Parameters:**
  - Each upper storey 0.6–1.2 m wider than the one below.
  - Windows packed in continuous rows, wall to window 45–60% on the upper floors against under 10% on the ground floor.
  - Wide eaves (1.2 m).
  - Timber upper versus stone base: a colour and material break at the first floor.
  - Facades symmetrical in the Revival variant, asymmetrical in the old variant.
