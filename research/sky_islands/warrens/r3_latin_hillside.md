# R3: Latin hillside self-built cities (Rio's favelas, Medellín's Comuna 13, Valparaíso's cerros)

**Island culture:** a hill covered to the top in self-built houses. In the favelas these are concrete-frame-and-brick boxes, each roof slab (*laje*) a terrace waiting for the next storey. In Valparaíso they are timber houses clad in corrugated iron painted in ship colours, climbing above funiculars (*ascensores*). Stair alleys climb everywhere and murals cover every wall. Public escalators and cable cars stitch the slopes together. The mark is the slab terrace and the painted tin: an endless, colourful, still-growing staircase city.

## Examples
- Rio, Rocinha:
  - Homes grow mainly by rooftop additions (*construção de laje*) on the upper slab.
  - Roof rights are sold to future builders; houses are later structurally upgraded for more storeys.
  - Little light or ventilation at street level.
  - Sources: [Wiley: Sustainable Development 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/sustdv/v27y2019i2p205-213.html), [ISDRS 2017](https://programme.exordo.com/isdrs2017/delegates/presentation/407/), [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/167354/rocinha-urban-strategy-kyle-beneventi/{{url}}), [Favelas 4D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.03235)
- Medellín, Comuna 13:
  - Outdoor escalators (2011–12): 6 sections, about 385 m, rising the height of a 28-storey building, cutting a 35-minute climb to 6 minutes. Free, part of a plan with parks, libraries and a cable car.
  - Cement and brick structures like Rio's favelas, streets too steep for vehicles, houses now brightly painted.
  - Sources: [colombiareports](https://colombiareports.com/medellin-inaugurates-almost-finished-mega-escalator/), [alongdustyroads](https://www.alongdustyroads.com/posts/2015/3/6/comuna-13-street-art-escalators-medellin), [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/comuna-13)
- Valparaíso:
  - Houses painted red, green, yellow and blue as identification in the 19th century, with murals since the 1960s.
  - Corrugated iron cladding: said to have come from ships' packing, containers or ballast, and painted with boat paint.
  - Almost no building regulations: wood and twisted iron. Late-1800s ascensores built with British mining technology.
  - A maze of stairways, funiculars and alleys.
  - Sources: [iol](https://www.iol.co.za/travel/a-colourful-escape-to-valparaiso-2007867), [Stanford photo notes](https://ai.stanford.edu/~latombe/mountain/photo/chile-2015/valparaiso-2015.htm), [Tripadvisor Cerro Concepción](https://www.tripadvisor.com/--d318249.html), [thirdeyemom](https://thirdeyemom.com/tag/cerro-alegre/?amp=1)

## Generator grammar
- **Cell:** 3–5 m boxes (a room each), storeys 2.6–2.9 m, 2–6 storeys, irregular.
- **Walls:**
  - Favela: a reinforced concrete frame (columns 0.2 m, slabs 0.12 m) with hollow clay brick infill `#b2603e` left raw or rendered and painted.
  - Valparaíso: timber frame clad in corrugated iron sheets (ribs vertical, 0.05 m pitch).
  - Palette: favela paint lime `#a8d14a`, sky `#5bb7e5`, pink `#f07ca8`, yellow `#f6cf3a`, orange `#f08a2a`, raw brick, grey concrete `#8f8d88`. Valparaíso tin in ship colours: red `#c23a2e`, cobalt `#2a55a8`, yellow `#f0c43a`, green `#3a9a5a`, teal `#2a8a8a`, white trim.
  - Weathering: rust streaks on the tin, half-painted walls (paint stops mid-wall), exposed rebar at the top.
- **Mass:**
  - Boxes packed edge to edge up the slope, each offset and stepping, sharing walls.
  - New storeys are smaller or cantilever over alleys.
  - Every roof slab is a terrace: water tank, laundry, kites, bbq, a stair to the next.
  - Valparaíso: houses on stilts (*palafitos*) over ravines, balconies and bay windows over the drops.
- **Streets:**
  - The main road winding up the hill (*estrada*, 5–7 m, buses and moto-taxis).
  - Alleys (*becos*) 0.8–2 m, branching endlessly.
  - Concrete stairs everywhere with handrails painted in colours.
  - Public escalators and funiculars on the fall line.
  - Cable cars over the top.
  - Mural walls along the stairs.
- **Growth:**
  - Layer 0: the hill, the old fort or chapel at the top, the 19th-century port town at the foot (Valparaíso's Victorian merchant houses).
  - Layer 1: shacks (timber and tin).
  - Layer 2: brick-and-concrete first storeys.
  - Layer 3: laje additions upward (2–6 storeys).
  - Layer 4: state upgrades (escalators, plazas, libraries, cable cars).
  - Layer 5: cyberpunk.
- **Openings:** aluminium or timber windows of mismatched sizes, iron security grilles (*grades*) on everything, roller shutters on corner shops; Valparaíso bay windows and enclosed balconies.
- **Roofs:** flat laje slabs with rebar stubs, blue water tanks (`#2a6ab0` fibreglass, round), corrugated fibre-cement sheets; Valparaíso shallow tin gables.
- **Ornament:** murals (huge, everywhere), painted stairs (each step a colour, or a mosaic image across the flight), graffiti, saint niches, painted football crests.
- **Public space:**
  - A plaza at the escalator or cable stations (library park, *parque biblioteca*).
  - Football pitches (a concrete court cut into the hill).
  - Lookout terraces.
  - Rooftop lajes (the true social space).
  - Stair landings with benches.
- **Water:** blue roof tanks, water channels down the stairs, a pond in the library park.

## Street hierarchy
- **Hill road (main, switchback):** 5–7 m.
- **Escalator and funicular axis:** 3 m.
- **Alleys:** 0.8–2 m.
- **Stairs:** 1.2–2 m.
- **Squares:** a plaza at each station, a sports court every 150 m, lookouts.

## Plants
- Banana plants in the gaps (3–4 m, big leaves).
- Papaya.
- Mango trees.
- Bougainvillea over walls.
- Philodendron and monstera spilling from roofs.
- Coconut palms at the foot (Rio).
- Potted herbs and aloe on every laje.
- Valparaíso: palms (Chilean wine palm), eucalyptus, geraniums, ice plant on the slopes, hydrangeas.

## Cyberpunk overlay
- Laje stacks keep going: 10+ storeys of improvised modules.
- Rooftop antenna and dish forests.
- Moto-taxi drones.
- Escalators lit in LED.
- Cable cars as neon gondolas.
- Murals as animated screens.
- "Gato" (illegal power tap) cable bundles webbing every pole into black clouds.
- **Night:** the whole hill becomes a dense field of tiny mismatched lights: white LED (`#e8f4ff`), warm (`#ffcf7a`) and TV-blue. Stair handrail LEDs zigzag up the slope. The cable-car line is a necklace of moving lights.

- **Parameters:**
  - Irregular stacking: per-storey footprint jitter ±1 m, cantilevers to 1 m.
  - A storey count random 2–6 per box, rising toward the crest.
  - Each box gets its own colour (paint coverage 30–100%, the rest raw brick).
  - Windows small and randomly placed (no alignment).
  - Every roof has a water tank.
  - Mural walls cover 15% of blank faces.
