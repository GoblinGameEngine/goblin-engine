# yemeni_tower: the Yemeni highland tower city (Sana'a, Shibam, Thula, Hadhramaut)

## Sources and examples
- UNESCO, Old City of Sana'a: "an extraordinary density of rammed earth and burnt brick towers rising several
  storeys above stone-built ground floors, decorated with geometric patterns of fired bricks and white gypsum".
  https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/385
- Sana'a heritage notes: houses rise to nine storeys, stone below and lighter brick above.
  https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/yemen/sanaa.htm
- Green architecture of Old Sana'a (PDF): https://holmakhdar.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/EN_OldCityOf-Sanaa_amodel_of_green_architecture.pdf
- Beit al-Mansour, a restoration case: https://archnet.org/sites/1161
- Al-Sabahi (1995 PhD), the bustan and the sarhah square, and how the grid broke the clusters:
  https://oro.open.ac.uk/64346
- Al-Samman, Space Syntax of the old city (dense, compact, highly connected, few angular changes):
  https://archnet.org/publications/13024
- "Sanaa, a hybrid city": growth around the souq and the Great Mosque, and segregated quarters:
  https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-01335672v1/file/Sanaa_A_hybrid_city.pdf
- Tim Mackintosh-Smith, "The secret gardens of Sanaa": 43 maqshama gardens inside the walls, from 3 ha down to under
  1,000 m², each quarter built round a mosque, a bathhouse and a garden, watered with the ablution greywater:
  https://muslimheritage.com/the-secret-gardens-of-sanaa/
- Qamariya and stained-glass fanlights, alabaster panes "set high above the alleyways, diffusing subtle, enigmatic
  light" at night: https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/199903/moonglow.from.underground.htm and
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_al_Yemen
- Shibam, the "Manhattan of the desert": towers 5–8 storeys (about 30 m), walls about 1 m thick at the base
  thinning to about 30 cm at the top, mud-plastered with lime on the upper storeys, the city walled on a mound:
  https://whc.unesco.org/en/soc/1640 · https://dome.mit.edu/handle/1721.3/109390 ·
  https://www.aliph-foundation.org/en/projects/preserving-the-unique-earthen-architecture-of-shibam

## Generator grammar
- **Cell and storeys:** a plan grid of 3.6 m (est.). Storey heights:
  - ground (stables, stores): 3.0 m;
  - living floors: 2.8 m;
  - the top-floor mafraj (a panoramic sitting room): 3.2 m.
  
  Towers run 5–9 storeys in Sana'a and 6–8 in Shibam. Footprints are small: 8–12 m × 8–12 m.
- **Walls:**
  - **Sana'a:** a dressed basalt or limestone ground floor (`#4a4743`, `#6b665f`), then fired brick in
    `#8a5a3c`/`#9c6a45` with raised-brick zigzag and chevron bands, and white gypsum `#f2efe6` outlining every
    window and band.
  - **Shibam:** mud brick on a stone footing, mud-plastered `#a07850`/`#b58b5e`. The upper third is lime-washed
    `#ece6d6`, which drips and streaks.
  - **Batter:** wall thickness 0.9–1.0 m at the base down to 0.3 m at the top. Outer faces lean in about 1.5% (est.).
  - **Weathering:** rain streaks under every gypsum frame and drain spout, and darker earth near the ground.
- **Mass:** free-standing or paired towers packed edge to edge. Each tower is one family's vertical house. There
  is no courtyard inside; the outdoor rooms are the roof terraces and the bustan gardens between blocks.
  Shibam: a near-solid grid of towers inside a rectangular wall, lanes 2–4 m wide.
- **Streets:**
  - Arterials wind to the gates: Bab al-Yemen, then the souq.
  - Lanes are 2–4 m wide, short and gently bent, ending at sarhah squares 15–30 m across.
  - Few true dead ends, because the gardens open the blocks.
  - Shibam has a regular grid with occasional 0.5 m air gaps between towers. There, skybridges link towers at
    upper floors (est.; they appear in photographs).
- **Growth (layers):**
  - E1: towers rise floor by floor as sons marry (a common Yemeni practice). Each addition is a lighter
    material: stone, then fired brick, then mud brick.
  - E2: mafraj rooms are added on the roofs, and gardens are encroached on.
  - E3: concrete floors topped with aluminium windows (photographs show qamariya craft being displaced by
    aluminium: Reuters 2014).
  - E4: lightweight boxes on roofs, linked by bridges.
- **Openings:**
  - **Windows:** rectangular lower lights 0.6–0.8 m wide × 1.0–1.2 m tall, crowned by a **qamariya**: a
    semicircular or pointed fanlight 0.6–0.9 m wide of alabaster or stained glass in gypsum tracery. Windows
    stack in vertical columns, 2–4 per facade per floor.
  - **Small openings:** small square vents above.
  - **Mashrabiya boxes:** small and wooden (a projecting cooling niche for water jars), one per floor on the
    street side.
  - **Doors:** heavy carved wood, 1.2 × 2.2 m, with a gypsum frame.
- **Roofs:** flat terraces with a crenellated parapet. The stepped merlons are whitewashed gypsum, rounded at the
  corners in Shibam. Wooden drain spouts project 0.6 m. There are no domes on houses; domes appear only on
  mosques and baths (small whitewashed domes clustered).
- **Ornament:** white gypsum frames around every opening, brick-relief friezes between storeys (in Sana'a, the
  bands thicken toward the top), and white frieze lines.
- **Public spaces:** the Great Mosque court, the souq al-Milh (a covered market of stalls), sarhah squares,
  samsara (caravanserai) courtyards, and the **bustan/maqshama gardens**: green rectangles fed by the mosque's
  greywater channel.
- **Water:** the mosque ablution pool (birka), a channel to the maqshama, and wells in courtyards of the samsara.
- **Plants:**
  - Maqshama gardens: low dense vegetable beds (alfalfa, leeks, radish, herbs) in a grid of 4 × 6 m beds. Trees:
    fig, pomegranate, a lone jujube (sidr, *Ziziphus spina-christi*: a broad dark crown, 8 m), qat shrubs (2–3 m),
    grapevine pergolas.
  - Basil pots on parapets.
  - Shibam: date palms in the palm grove ringing the town at the wadi.
- **Cyberpunk overlay:**
  - Qamariya fanlights become LED-backlit stained panels that glow in colour all night: the city's signature.
  - White gypsum frame lines double as LED strips.
  - Rooftop mafraj rooms become glass boxes.
  - Satellite dishes on every parapet; water tanks.
  - Neon is sparse: shop-front signs in the souq in green and white Arabic script.
  - Cable runs follow the brick bands.
- **Night:** dark mass towers dotted with jewel-coloured semicircles: amber, ruby, emerald, cobalt. The white
  frames catch moonlight. The souq is lit by bare bulbs.

## Kit
- **Common pieces:**
  - `wall_panel` (stone ground floor; brick and mud above);
  - `band` (brick relief, gypsum line), `parapet_crenel`;
  - `window_small` + `qamariya_fanlight`, `door_studded`;
  - `bay_window_box` (preset: small mashrabiya box);
  - `sabat_bridge`, `skybridge` (Shibam);
  - `roof_flat_terrace` with drain spouts;
  - `dome_small` (mosques and baths only);
  - plants, `water_tank`, `satellite_dish`, `cable_bundle`.
- **Parameters that make it Yemeni:**
  - towers taller than wide (height/width 1.8–3.0), tapered;
  - the window column rhythm: one bay per 1.8 m, every opening framed white 12 cm;
  - qamariya over every main window;
  - brick-relief bands every storey from the 2nd up, denser toward the top;
  - the palette: dark basalt, brick red-brown, mud brown, gypsum white;
  - roofs crenellated white.
- **Unique pieces (3):**
  - `qamariya_fanlight` (stained-glass and alabaster variants; emits at night);
  - `brick_relief_band` (zigzag, chevron and lozenge variants);
  - `parapet_crenel_rounded` (the soft white Shibam merlon).
