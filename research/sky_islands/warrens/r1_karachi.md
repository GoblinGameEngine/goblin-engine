# karachi: the Sindhi port metropolis (Kharadar, Mithadar, Saddar, Lyari, Orangi)

The user named Karachi, so this family is the richest.

Karachi is not one style but the **layered** city par excellence:
- a fortified Sindhi merchant town (1720s);
- a British colonial stone city (1840s–1940s), whose Hindu, Parsi, Goan, Ismaili and Memon builders mixed Venetian
  Gothic, Indo-Saracenic and Art Deco;
- concrete post-1947 accretion;
- the vast informal self-built city (katchi abadis).

All of it is wrapped in truck-art colour, hand-painted signage, cables, water tanks and kites.

## Sources and examples
- **Kharadar and Mithadar** take their names from the two gates of the 17th–18th-century fortified merchant
  settlement: the Khara Dar (salt-water gate) faced the sea, the Mitha Dar (sweet-water gate) the Lyari river.
  The gates and walls were dismantled by the British; this is the oldest Karachi.
  https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/610896 · https://jang.com.pk/thenews/mar2007-weekly/nos-18-03-2007/kol.htm
- **Old-town architecture:**
  - Builders (local Silawat stonemasons) worked in Venetian Gothic, Indo-Saracenic and Art Deco.
  - Buildings have balconies, verandas and teak staircases.
  - More than 1,700 premises are listed.
  - Rapid redevelopment puts multi-storey residential blocks in place of heritage.
  - https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/pakistan/karachi-heritages-laboured-breath-1.2189240
- **Marvi Mazhar, "Exploring Old Town Karachi":**
  - The Sarnagati Building is red sandstone.
  - Stone carvings get painted in garish colours; jharokhas are poorly renovated.
  - Heritage rules keep only the "skin", so interiors decay behind facades.
  - Different religious and community influences show in the buildings.
  - https://www.marvimazhar.com/blog-1/2019/5/2/exploring-old-town-karachi
- **Sarafa Bazaar and Gao Gali walk:** https://thekarachiwalla.com/2018/08/12/city-walk-sarafa-bazaar-gao-gali-and-others/
- **Saddar:**
  - The largest concentration of colonial architecture in Karachi.
  - **Empress Market** (1884–89, James Strachan): Gothic, round a courtyard, with a 120 ft clock tower.
  - **Frere Hall** (1863–65, H. St Clair Wilkins): Venetian Gothic in yellow and grey stone.
  - The KMC building used red Jodhpur stone, while the nearby **Gizri hills were quarried for yellow stone**.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddar · https://architexturez.net/node/186399 ·
    https://scroll.in/article/740638/reflections-of-bombay-across-the-border-a-photographer-visits-karachis-iconic-empress-market ·
    https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Frere_Hall
- **Orangi Town:**
  - The largest katchi abadi: about 94,000 households and about 800,000 people in 1989.
  - Self-built with informal contractors.
  - **The lane is the unit**: 20–30 houses, each lane organised to build its own sewer (Orangi Pilot Project);
    6,347 lanes.
  - https://riceinstitute.org/blog-post/a-sanitation-project-that-worked-2/ · https://archive.urbz.net/node/683 ·
    https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/articles/transforming-a-karachi-slum-into-a-tidy-suburb
- **Lyari:**
  - Painted lanes: Baghdadi Lane No. 10, about 22 house facades painted rainbow colours (Kiran Foundation).
  - Murals on Khoja Street; a Sunday market on the footpaths and green belt.
  - https://tribune.com.pk/story/1592656/lyari-street-painted-colours-rainbow ·
    https://thekarachiwalla.com/2016/12/05/city-culture-street-art-in-lyari/ · https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1143599
- **Truck art:**
  - Saturated fluorescent colour; floral, peacock, landscape and calligraphy motifs.
  - **chamak patti**: reflective cut-sticker work on metal, with Karachi's school known for reflective tape.
  - Mirrors and bells hang from the trucks; Mauripur Road is the painting district.
  - https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/general%2Fa-driving-passion-3-1.83714 · https://1-catf.pixels.com/blogs/catf-and-truck-art.html ·
    https://southasia.com.pk/2020/02/17/art-on-wheels/
- **Water and density:**
  - Private water tankers; residents pay for their own tanks.
  - A high-rise ban was lifted with a 26-storey cap.
  - 588 hazardous structures, 444 of them heritage.
  - Kites are banned for metal strings, yet still flown from roofs.
  - https://zameen.com/blog/ban-on-high-rise-buildings-lifted-karachi-gets-its-mojo-back.html ·
    https://tribune.com.pk/story/1638935/kite-flying-dying-sport-open-public-spaces-rapidly-decrease-across-karachi ·
    https://tribune.com.pk/story/2620821/karachis-ticking-time-bomb
- **Street trees:**
  - Conocarpus monoculture (2008, banned 2016) is being replaced by neem, gulmohar and native species (banyan,
    peepal).
  - Fruit trees (jamun, tropical almond, chikoo) are planted on roads.
  - https://urckarachi.org/?p=14113 · https://www.environmentandsociety.org/arcadia/plant-politics-karachi ·
    https://tribune.com.pk/story/2471575/canocarpus-on-shaheed-e-millat-rd-choped

## Generator grammar
- **Cell and storeys:**
  - Grid 3.0 m.
  - Colonial storeys 4.0–4.5 m (ground) and 3.6 m above.
  - Old-town merchant houses: 3–5 storeys on 6–9 m frontages, 15–25 m deep.
  - Post-1947 concrete: 2.9 m storeys, 6–12 storeys.
  - Orangi: 1–3 storeys on 60–120 m² plots (est.).
- **Walls (by era):**
  - **E1 Sindhi merchant town:** rubble and lime, whitewashed or ochre, with wooden balconies (photos of Kharadar).
  - **E2 colonial stone:**
    - Yellow Gizri sandstone `#d8b679`, `#c9a465` in rusticated ground floors.
    - Carved upper facades and red Jodhpur stone `#a8553f` for public buildings.
    - Grey-green Manora stone accents (est.).
    - Lime pointing; cornice and parapet with a date plaque ("Built 1914") and a family name in relief.
  - **E3 concrete:** grey render `#9a9890`, distempered pastels (mint `#a8d8b9`, salmon `#e8a48a`, sky `#9cc4e4`,
    cream), cracked, streaked black under every slab edge by monsoon rain.
  - **Orangi:** bare grey concrete block `#8c8a84`, rebar stubs on the roof for the next floor, a painted ground
    floor.
- **Mass:**
  - Old town: **deep narrow merchant blocks wall to wall** along streets (shop below, residence above, inner light
    shaft), street facades 3–5 bays.
  - Saddar: big colonial blocks with arcades and corner domes or cupolas.
  - Lyari: 2–4 storey houses packed along lanes, some courtyard compounds.
  - Orangi: a regular lane grid of plots, each house growing upward floor by floor.
- **Streets (hierarchy):**
  - **Bazaar arteries:** 8–15 m with arcaded colonial frontages, chaotic traffic (rickshaws, decorated buses, carts).
  - **Old-town galis:** 3–5 m between 4-storey facades with balconies stacked on both sides.
  - **Lyari lanes:** 2–4 m.
  - **Orangi lanes:** 3–6 m, straight, with an open drain at the side and the lane sewer manholes.
  - **Kochas:** dead ends 1.5–2 m.
  - Overhead, **cable canyons**: electricity, telephone and cable-TV bundles strung at 2–3 levels, sagging,
    knotted at poles, plus kite strings.
- **Growth (Karachi grows by addition, and every building shows it):**
  - Stone colonial floors 1–2 or 1–3.
  - Then 1–3 concrete storeys added on top, set back or flush, in a different colour.
  - Then a **rooftop tin shack** or a **steel-frame room**, with water tanks (black 500–1000 l plastic drums on a
    steel stand), a satellite dish and a solar panel.
  - Balconies get enclosed with grilles, glass and tin.
  - Shopfronts cut into colonial arcades, with roller shutters.
  - Signboards stacked up the facade.
  - E4 adds skybridges between rooftops, neon-lit tea stalls on roofs, and LED billboards bolted to the parapets.
- **Openings:**
  - **Colonial:** pointed Gothic or segmental arches, deep verandas on cast-iron columns, louvred wooden shutters
    (dark green `#2f4f3a`, blue).
  - **The jharokha** (a projecting stone or wooden balcony on carved brackets, with a small cusped arch and a
    chhajja hood).
  - **Wooden balconies** with turned balusters and cast-iron brackets.
  - **Concrete era:** box balconies with steel grilles in decorative patterns (sunburst, diamond), aluminium
    sliding windows, AC units in every second window.
  - **Ground:** roller-shutter shops 3–4 m wide.
- **Roofs:**
  - flat roofs with parapets: colonial balustrades, urns, date plaques, small domes or chhatris on corner towers;
  - most of all, the **rooftop life layer**: water tanks, dishes, laundry lines, kite flyers, pigeon coops (a
    Karachi passion), shacks, bedframes (charpai) for sleeping in the heat.
- **Ornament:**
  - colonial carving (foliage, Gothic tracery, Indo-Saracenic brackets);
  - painted over in garish colours (red, green and gold-painted carvings: Mazhar);
  - **truck-art** spread to shutters, rickshaws, signboards, buses and walls: fluorescent floral borders, peacocks,
    eyes, calligraphy, chamak patti;
  - hand-painted Urdu nastaliq signboards in red, yellow and green on white or black;
  - political party flags and graffiti.
- **Public spaces:**
  - the **Empress Market** court and plaza;
  - **chowks** (Pakistan Chowk, Lea Market) at street crossings with a monument or clock;
  - the waterfront (Kemari, Native Jetty);
  - **parks** (Frere Hall gardens, the Hill Park) and the **green belts** along roads, used for Sunday markets;
  - Lyari's football grounds (a Lyari passion) and boxing rings;
  - mosque and temple courts (Swaminarayan Temple), and Parsi and Ismaili halls.
- **Water:**
  - **water tankers** and street water points;
  - rooftop tanks (iconic);
  - a few colonial fountains (in Saddar, est.);
  - the sea and the creek (Chinna Creek);
  - the game adds ponds in the park.
- **Plants:**
  - **neem** (dense, dark green, 10–15 m, a broad street tree), **gulmohar** (*Delonix regia*: an umbrella crown
    with flame-red flowers, 8–12 m), **banyan** and **peepal** (huge, aerial roots, at shrines and chowks);
  - **coconut** and **date palms** in Clifton and the old gardens; **bougainvillea** spilling over walls in
    magenta and orange; **conocarpus** hedges and stubs (the old monoculture);
  - fruit trees: jamun, chikoo, tropical almond (large horizontal tiers);
  - potted money plants on balconies, and tulsi.
- **Cyberpunk overlay (the strongest of all the families):**
  - **Truck-art LED**: chamak-patti reflective patterns become animated LED panels on facades, shutters and
    skybridges, in fluorescent magenta, lime, cyan, orange and gold.
  - **Cable canyons** with fairy lights threaded through them.
  - Stacked **LED signboards** in Urdu and English up every facade.
  - Rickshaw- and bus-style decorated pods as rooftop rooms.
  - Wedding lights: festoons of thousands of bulbs draping whole buildings (the Karachi wedding facade).
  - Neon on the colonial cornices; rooftop kites with LED tails.
- **Night:** the brightest of the 15. Wedding-light curtains over buildings, cyan and magenta truck-art LEDs, warm
  sodium in the galis, flickering tube lights in shops, the stone colonial facades floodlit gold, rooftop tea
  stalls with string lights.
