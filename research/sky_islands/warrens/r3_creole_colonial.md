# R3: Creole colonial port cities (Havana Vieja, Cartagena, San Juan, Willemstad, Guanajuato, Oaxaca)

**Island culture:** a tropical colonial grid of thick-walled, high-ceilinged courtyard houses in candy pastels. Arcades (*portales*) shade the main streets. Stained-glass half-moon fanlights (*medio puntos*) cap tall shuttered windows, and wooden balconies hang heavy with bougainvillea. There are plazas with royal palms and flame trees. In the mountain variant (Guanajuato), houses are stacked up a ravine and streets run in tunnels underneath. The mark is the pastel arcade, the coloured glass fan and the flowering balcony: lush, hot and saturated.

## Examples
- Havana:
  - Arcades along the main thoroughfares shield walkers from sun and downpours; shops at street level, homes above.
  - Casa del Marqués de Arcos (1741), a typical arcaded baroque building with *medio punto* stained-glass half-moons that filter heat.
  - Plaza Vieja's arcades and tall stained-glass windows. Thick walls, balconies and courtyards built for heat and breezes.
  - Sources: [Sarasota Magazine](https://www.sarasotamagazine.com/articles/2001/10/1/treasure-of-the-caribbean), [routard](https://www.routard.com/guide_voyage_lieu/1727-la_havane_(la_habana).htm), [caribbean-beat](https://www.caribbean-beat.com/issue-64/feeling-havana)
- Havana trees:
  - 28 royal palms in Parque Central for Martí's birthday.
  - Flamboyant (flame) trees common on streets, parks and highways.
  - Jagüey (*Ficus*) widely planted in the parks, its roots breaking the pavement.
  - Sources: [Havana Times palms](https://havanatimes.org/?p=122100), [Havana Times flame trees](https://havanatimes.org/photo-feature/a-flame-tree-explosion/), [Havana Times giants](https://havanatimes.org/?p=242142)
- Cartagena: the walled city (UNESCO 1984), with cobbled alleys, balconies covered in bougainvillea, colonial fronts "painted like tropical fruit", wooden overhanging balconies, courtyards, Andalusian-style houses. [cartagenacolombiarentals](https://cartagenacolombiarentals.com/2024/10/spanish-colonial-architecture-cartagena/), [nomadotravel guide](https://www.nomadotravel.app/en/guides/cartagena-colonial-architecture-guide)
- Willemstad, Curaçao:
  - Handelskade gables in ochre, orange, blue and pink.
  - The story that the 1817 governor ordered colour because white glare gave him headaches.
  - The Penha building (1708), with clock gables.
  - The Queen Emma pontoon bridge. UNESCO.
  - Sources: [KLM](https://www.klm.com/travel-guide/inspiration/historic-clock-gables-in-punda), [1000 awesome things](https://1000awesomethingsaboutcuracao.com/2013/10/22/627-curacaos-capital-willemstad-amsterdam-in-the-tropics/)
- Guanajuato:
  - Multicoloured houses climbing a narrow valley, flat roof terraces, 14 named callejones.
  - Flood tunnels turned into underground roads.
  - The Callejón del Beso, where balconies almost touch.
  - Sources: [saitowitz](https://www.saitowitz.com/guanajuato), [oaxacaculture](https://oaxacaculture.com/2012/08/tunnels-of-guanajuato-city-unesco-world-heritage-site), [escapetomexico](https://escapetomexico.com/guanajuato-city/)
- Laws of the Indies (1573): a plaza mayor anchoring a grid; arcades (*portales*) round the plaza and its four main streets; the plaza square or rectangular, typically about 600×400 ft ideal. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_the_Indies), [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1024343/whatsapp:/send), [WTTW ordinances](https://www.wttw.com/node/3951)

## Generator grammar
- **Cell:** 5 m bays. Storeys are 4.5–5.5 m (very high ceilings for heat), 2–3 storeys plus a mezzanine (*barbacoa*). Blocks are about 80–100 m on the grid; courtyard houses 15×30 m.
- **Walls:**
  - 0.6–1.0 m coral stone or rubble with lime stucco, painted.
  - Palette: flamingo `#f29aa8`, mango `#f6b03c`, turquoise `#3ac0c0`, lime `#b8d85a`, sky `#6ab8e8`, lavender `#b8a0d8`, coral `#f07a5a`, white trim `#fbf7ee`.
  - Shutters and balconies green `#2e7a4a`, blue `#2a5aa8` or brown `#5a3a28`. Roof tile `#b8603a`.
  - Weathering: tropical. Peeling paint in layers (old colours showing under new), black mould streaks, ferns in the cornices, collapsed upper floors (Havana).
- **Mass:**
  - Grid blocks of contiguous courtyard houses with a central patio (arcaded galleries on two levels, a fountain, plants).
  - The *zaguán* entrance with a carriage door.
  - Blocks deep, facades continuous.
  - Guanajuato variant: houses stacked on terraces up the ravine sides, with narrow callejones and stairs, and roads in tunnels under the town.
- **Streets:**
  - Grid streets 6–10 m, cobbled; main streets with arcades (*portales*, 4–5 m deep) on one or both sides.
  - Sidewalks narrow (1 m) elsewhere.
  - Callejones 1–3 m (Guanajuato).
  - Tunnel roads 8 m wide, arched, stone-lined.
- **Growth:**
  - Layer 0: the walled fortress and bastions, the cathedral.
  - Layer 1: 16th–17th-century houses with timber balconies.
  - Layer 2: 18th-century baroque palaces with portales and medio puntos.
  - Layer 3: 19th–20th-century neoclassical and Art Deco infill, Dutch gables (Willemstad).
  - Layer 4: decay and improvised additions (*barbacoas* inside the high rooms, rooftop shacks).
  - Layer 5: cyberpunk.
- **Openings:**
  - Floor-to-ceiling windows (1.2×3.5 m) with turned timber bars (*barrotes*) or iron, and louvred shutters.
  - Medio punto stained-glass lunettes (r 0.6–0.9 m, radiating panes in red `#c8202a`, blue `#2050b0`, amber `#e8a020`, green `#2a9a4a`).
  - Wooden balconies on corbels (Cartagena: long covered balconies with turned balusters).
  - Carriage doors with small inset doors.
- **Roofs:** flat roof terraces (*azoteas*) with parapets and urns; tile roofs on some; Dutch curved and clock gables (Willemstad); bell towers and domes.
- **Ornament:** baroque stone portals, pilasters, cornices, wrought iron, coloured glass, painted borders round the windows, tiled house numbers.
- **Public space:**
  - The plaza mayor or Plaza de Armas: a square of royal palms and flame trees, a fountain, benches and a gazebo (*kiosco*), arcades round it.
  - Secondary plazas at churches.
  - The malecón (sea promenade, with the sea wall).
  - Patio gardens (semi-public).
  - Market plazas.
- **Water:** plaza fountains, patio fountains and cisterns, the bay (pond), a malecón-edge pool.

## Street hierarchy
- **Arcaded main streets (calle Real / Obispo):** 8–10 m plus portales.
- **Malecón promenade:** 20 m on the island rim.
- **Grid streets:** 6–8 m.
- **Callejones and stairs:** 1–3 m.
- **Tunnel road** (a lower level).
- **Squares:** the plaza mayor (100×70 m), a plaza every 3–4 blocks.

## Plants
- Royal palm (*Roystonea*: smooth grey trunk, crownshaft green, 15–20 m).
- Flamboyant (flame tree: umbrella crown, scarlet `#e8402a`, 10 m).
- Ficus / jagüey (huge, aerial roots).
- Bougainvillea on every balcony (magenta `#c2185b`, orange `#e86a2a`, white).
- Hibiscus (`#e8304a`).
- Banana and plantain in the patios.
- Ferns in hanging pots.
- Ceiba (giant, buttressed) in the main plaza.
- Willemstad: divi-divi, cacti.
- Guanajuato: jacaranda, laurel (Indian laurel fig, clipped round) in the plazas.

## Cyberpunk overlay
- Medio puntos as RGB LED fans.
- Portales hung with neon signs and screens.
- Patio courts roofed in solar glass, with hydroponic jungle.
- Vintage-car-shaped e-taxis.
- Balconies stacked with AC condensers, dripping.
- Murals of revolutionaries as holograms.
- Malecón with sea-wall light strips.
- **Night:** pastels go deep under warm sodium (`#ffb060`). The fanlights glow jewel colours from inside (a signature). The plazas are lit with bulb strings in the palms. Music bars spill coloured light from the portales.
