# A1. Arabic old towns: what the sky islands can take from them

Research stream A, 2026-10-14. The figures are typical and rounded. Where a source gives no number, the value is an
estimate, marked "≈", from common survey practice (Lewcock et al., *San'a: An Arabian Islamic City*, 1983, is the
standard reference for Yemen).

## Sana'a, Yemen: the tower house (bayt)
- **Form:** tall, narrow, free-standing or terraced towers of **5–9 storeys** (4–9 in some counts), about 6,000
  surviving. Storey height ≈ 3.0–3.5 m; total height ≈ 20–30 m. Footprint ≈ 8–12 m × 8–14 m.
  [studiomatrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/architecture-canon/sana-a-old-city), [pilotguides](https://www.pilotguides.com/articles/sana-and-architecture-of-ancient-skyscrapers)
- **Material by height:** a dressed-stone or rammed-earth base (≈ 1–2 storeys, dark basalt/grey stone), then fired and
  sun-dried brick above, lighter as it rises. External gypsum plaster protects the brick.
  [al-bab](https://al-bab.com/yemeni-architecture)
- **Floors, bottom to top:**
  - ground: animals and timber;
  - mezzanine: grain and stores;
  - 1st–2nd: the diwan (reception) and the women's floor;
  - 3rd–4th: bedrooms, bath, kitchen (at the top, for the smoke);
  - top: the **mafraj**, a panoramic room with low windows on three sides;
  - roof terrace with a parapet.
  [Jeffery, Arid Lands](https://cales.arizona.edu/OALS/ALN/aln28/jeffery.html), [Archnet Bayt al-Belayli](https://next.archnet.org/sites/3808)
- **Ornament (the look we want):**
  - **white gypsum bands** framing every window and running as friezes between floors, on dark brick (≈ #6b4a35 brick, #f2efe6 lime white);
  - patterns like latticework, jewellery and textiles;
  - **qamariya**: a semicircular fanlight over each window, of alabaster or stained glass in gypsum tracery. At night it glows coloured from inside. **This is the single best "night" element of the whole region.**
- **Windows:** a rectangular lower light (≈ 0.8 × 1.2 m) with wooden shutters, and a qamariya arch over it
  (≈ 0.8 m wide, radius 0.4 m). They stack in vertical columns, 2–4 per facade per floor.
- **Streets:** 2–4 m lanes, with *bustan* gardens inside blocks.
- **Takhrima:** the Yemeni name for a projecting lattice box.

## Shibam, Yemen: "the Manhattan of the desert" (the stacked-warren example)
- About **500** mud-brick towers, **5–8 storeys** (some up to 11), about **30 m** tall, packed inside a rectangular
  wall. Plots are about **90 m²** (≈ 9 × 10 m).
  [studiomatrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/architecture-canon/shibam-manhattan-of-the-desert), [urbanitarian](https://www.urbanitarian.com/masterplans_post?id=342), [UNESCO 192](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/192)
- **Batter:** the walls are very thick at the base and taper as they rise (≈ 1.0 m at the base to 0.4 m at the top).
  The silhouette leans in slightly, and the top storeys are lime-whitened. Mud-brown body ≈ #a8835c, lime cap ≈ #efe8da.
- **Ground and first floors are blind** (storage, animals); the windows start above that and get bigger the higher they are.
- **Bridges** linked the upper floors between towers: "streets in the air", also used as escape routes. This is the
  direct precedent for sky-island skybridges.
- Steep stairs run inside the thick walls. Re-plastering is a communal, cyclical job, which works as a social custom
  for the Aloft.
- **For us:** a dense grid of tall, slim, tapering towers about 2–4 m apart, with bridges at storeys 4–6 and white
  crowns. At night: small warm windows high up and a dark base.

## Jeddah, Al-Balad (Hejaz): coral-stone houses with roshans
- **Form:** coral-stone houses of 4–7 storeys (Nasseef House: 4 at the front, 7 at the back; the tallest in Jeddah
  until the 1970s), with wooden tie-beams every ≈ 1 m of height.
  [aramcoexpats](https://aramcoexpats.com/articles/al-balad-the-jewel-of-jeddah), [perceptivetravel](https://perceptivetravel.com/blog/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/15268), [houseofsaud](https://houseofsaud.com/?p=30756)
- **The roshan:** large projecting timber bay windows, stacked floor over floor, sometimes joined into one
  continuous screen 2–3 storeys tall. Turned-wood lattice; openings small at the bottom and larger higher up. It
  projects ≈ 0.6–0.9 m; one bay ≈ 1.5–3 m wide × 2.5–3 m tall.
  [Mashrabiya (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashrabiya)
- **Colours:** wood weathered grey-brown, or painted green, blue or turquoise (≈ #2f6f6a, #3b7fa6, #8a5a3b) on
  limewashed coral (≈ #e9e1cf).
- **For us:** **"roshan_bay"**, the defining component. A facade of stacked lattice boxes, lit from inside at night
  like lanterns.

## Cairo: the Islamic quarter, mashrabiya, wikala and rab'
- **Wikala of al-Ghuri (1504–05):**
  - a rectangular block of about 5 storeys around a paved courtyard, with a two-level arcade round it;
  - the lower two levels (stone) are stores, with ≈ 30 vaulted storerooms on one;
  - the top three levels hold **29 triplex apartments (rab')**, each three storeys tall.
  [Archnet 4258](https://archnet.org/sites/4258), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikala_of_al-Ghuri)
- **This is a ready-made sky-island "market block":** trade below, homes above, one courtyard, one gate. A
  **wikala/khan** is literally a merchants' inn, which suits a society of roaming traders.
- **Mashrabiya:**
  - a cantilevered lattice oriel on upper floors, with turned-wood balusters;
  - pattern types: hexagonal, Kanaysi (long vertical), Maymoni, cross, and Sahrigi (wide mesh);
  - water jars on the sill cool the air.
- **Streets:** **ablaq** (striped stone, red/white or black/white), muqarnas portals, minarets, and the shop-lined
  qasaba spine (the Muizz street).
- **Night today:** strings of bare bulbs, Ramadan **fanous** lanterns (pierced tin with coloured glass), and green
  neon on minarets.

## Fez and Marrakesh: riad and dar
- **Plan:** an inward courtyard house; a *riad* has a garden quartered by paths with a fountain at the cross, a *dar*
  a paved court. Galleries ring the court, arcaded on the inside with solid walls outside, rooms branching off
  them. The entrance is **bent**, so nobody can see in from the street.
  [Historic house architecture in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_house_architecture_in_Morocco)
- **Fez:** tall, narrow houses of 2–4 storeys around a narrow, deep court (≈ 6 × 6 m to 10 × 12 m) open to the sky.
  Rooms are long and shallow (≈ 2.8–3.5 m deep × 8–12 m long), each with tall double cedar doors onto the court.
- **Roofs:**
  - terraces, with a ***menzeh*** (a little rooftop belvedere tower) on rich houses;
  - a ***douiria*** (an upper guest flat).
- **Decoration:**
  - **zellij** tile dado (≈ 1.2–1.5 m high);
  - carved stucco above it;
  - carved and painted cedar ceilings;
  - horseshoe and ogee arches.
- **Colours:**
  - Marrakesh walls are rose, red or ochre (≈ #c4634a, #d98a5f);
  - Fez is cream and bone (≈ #e6dcc6) with green tile roofs (≈ #2e7d55) and zellij blue, green and white.
- **Night:** pierced brass and coloured-glass lanterns casting star patterns, candle niches, souk stalls under reed
  roofs with hanging bulbs.

## Muscat and Nizwa, Oman
- **Mud and stone houses:**
  - built with palm-frond and palm-trunk roofs;
  - **carved wooden doors** with brass studs are the signature of the region (Al Hamra houses are 200–400 years old);
  - Misfat al-Abriyeen's are stone, on terraces over falaj (irrigation) channels.
  [Peregrine](https://peregrinetreks.com/blog/omans-traditional-villages-and-forts), [scottdunn](https://www.scottdunn.com/hk/oman/things-to-do/nizwa-fort-and-souq-with-visit)
- **Nizwa Fort:**
  - its **round tower** is solid earth and stone for the first ≈ 14 m;
  - it has crenellations (triangular merlons) and murder holes.
- **For us:**
  - "falaj" open water channels between buildings, fed from cloud nets and feeding the ponds;
  - crenellated round towers as island landmarks (a clan's tower);
  - the studded doors.

## Damascus and Aleppo
- **Courtyard house (bayt):**
  - the street face is blank; the courtyard has a fountain and citrus trees;
  - an **iwan**: a vaulted hall open on one side, facing north (a summer room);
  - two levels, with the upper floor used in winter and the ground floor in summer;
  - **ablaq** stone striping (≈ #d8cbb0 limestone, #3a3633 basalt).
  [Bayt Farhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayt_Farhi), [syrian-treasures AlAqqad](https://syrian-treasures.com/en/alaqqad-house/)
- **Upper floors:** jettied timber-framed upper floors overhang the lanes, sometimes nearly meeting across them.
  **Sabat**: rooms bridging the street.

## Ghadames, Libya: the town with two levels
- **Ground level:** covered alleys (**sabat**) under the houses, almost a tunnel network, for the men's routes.
- **Rooftops:** **joined into one open cityscape** for the women, who cross from roof to roof.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_town_of_Ghadames), [UNESCO 362](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/362)
- **House:** stores on the ground floor, the family above, an open terrace on top.
- **Interiors:** whitewashed and painted red with geometric motifs, hung with mirrors and brass pots.
- **Layout:** seven tribal mahallas.
- **For us, a whole island typology:** **"two-level towns"** where roofs are the main street (fits the canon's roof
  access, roof gardens and lark cradles) and the ground is covered arcades.

## Siwa, Egypt: Shali
- **Material:** *kershef*, salt-crusted mud blocks bound with mud and rock-salt mortar, plastered with clay. Palm
  trunks form the roofs and olive wood the doors and frames.
  [Archnet Shali revival](https://next.archnet.org/sites/21598), [CNR salt architecture](https://data.cnr.it/data/http:/www.cnr.it/ontology/cnr/individuo/prodotto/ID19449)
- **Form:** 4–5 storeys, a labyrinth of packed buildings; it melted in the rain of 1926.
- **For us:**
  - an eroded, melting, sculpted look: soft rounded parapets, organic massing;
  - the palette: salt-white crust over grey-ochre (≈ #cfc6b3, #9c8f78);
  - the canon's snow makes it apt: buildings that "weather" visibly.

## Signs and night in these streets (for SkySigns)
- **Lanterns:** fanous lanterns in pierced tin with coloured glass, and Moroccan brass star-lanterns.
- **Bulbs and neon:** strings of bare warm bulbs across lanes, green neon outlining minarets and domes, and
  **coloured qamariya fanlights**.
- **Small shop signs:** painted or tin boards, hand-lettered signs in painted calligraphy, and the modern LED
  scrolling signs found in today's souks.
- **Dense layering:** awnings, hung carpets, and goods hung on facades as advertising.
