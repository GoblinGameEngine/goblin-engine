## Ghardaïa and the M'zab pentapolis (Algeria)
- **Layout:** five walled hilltop ksour (UNESCO 1982). Each is a **cone of cubic houses in concentric rings climbing a
  hill to a mosque** at the top, whose **truncated pyramidal minaret** is also a watchtower and store. Lanes are a
  maze; the **rahba** is a rectangular arcaded market square low down.
  [M'zab (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%27zab), [Ghardaïa (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghardaya), [kupi](https://kupi.com/en/explore/algeria/ghardaia/history)
- **Houses:**
  - sand, clay and gypsum; 2–3 storeys; a central covered court (*wast ed-dar*) with a roof opening (*chebek*, a grille over the hole);
  - roof terraces with arcaded loggias; each house is about 8 × 10 m to 12 × 14 m.
- **Colours:** whitewash, pale blue, ochre, rose and sand (≈ #f2efe8, #b9d3e6, #e2c79a, #e7b9a6, #d8c3a0) on irregular
  hand-made surfaces (no straight edges).
- **For us:** **the rolling-hill islands become Ghardaïa cones.** Rings of cubes step up the hill to a pyramid minaret
  (the island's crown, sign mast and lark lookout), with the market square at the foot by the bridges.

## Ksar Ouled Soltane and the ghorfa ksour (southern Tunisia)
- **Layout:** a fortified **granary** of barrel-vaulted cells (*ghorfas*) stacked **four storeys** high round **two
  courtyards** (the second 19th-century), ≈ **400 ghorfas**. Each cell has one door, reached by **steep external
  stairs** and protruding stones and beams. Adobe; repaired with cement in 1997.
  [Ksar Ouled Soltane (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksar_Ouled_Soltane), [Amusing Planet](https://www.amusingplanet.com/2014/11/ksar-ouled-soltane-fortified-granary.html), [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/pois/1595816)
- **The cell (≈):** 2.5–3 m wide, 5–10 m deep, 2–2.5 m high, a vaulted top; the facade is a honeycomb of rounded
  vault-ends with small doors.
- **For us:** **the perfect stacked warren for trade islands.** Ghorfas become shops, stores and tiny homes, stacked
  4–6 high round courts, with external stairs and plank landings everywhere, and a lit sign over every door. (A
  ghorfa is a repeatable component: one vault cell, stacked.)

## Aït Benhaddou and the Draa/Dades kasbahs (Morocco)
- **Ksar:** a dense hillside ksar of rammed-earth buildings. The lower storeys are rammed earth and the **upper
  storeys lighter adobe**; lower walls are thick and windowless. **Corner towers taper upward with geometric
  motifs** (chevrons, lozenges) on their tops. It climbs to a hilltop granary (agadir).
  [Aït Benhaddou (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFt_Benhaddou), [studiomatrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/architecture-canon/ait-benhaddou)
- **Tighremt (fortified house):**
  - a square block with **four lean corner towers**, 3–4 floors and a central court for ventilation, ringed by arcades and corridors;
  - floors: cattle and granary on the ground floor, the winter home on the 2nd, the summer home and guests on the **roof terrace between the towers**.
  [Kasbah Amridil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasbah_Amridil), [hiddenarchitecture: Amridil](https://hiddenarchitecture.net/amridil-el-correo-1-3/)
- **Colours:** red-ochre earth (≈ #b5643f, #c97e52, #9a5233) with darker motif reliefs (shadow only).
- **For us:** **tighremts as apartment blocks**: a square body with four tapering corner towers (stair cores, sign
  masts), and the roof terrace as a garden between the towers.

## Matmata (southern Tunisia): the troglodyte pits
- A **circular pit courtyard** 5–10 m deep and 7–15 m across, with **rooms carved round it** on one or two levels,
  linked by tunnels. Some have several pits joined underground. There is a well in the court, and it is up to 15 °C
  cooler than outside.
  [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/matmata-underground-houses), [Kanaga](https://www.kanaga-at.com/en/trip-info/tunisia-en/matmata-and-chenini-berber-traditions/)
- **For us:**
  - **sunken courts in the deck** (an island's skystone is deep enough; the rock glb's basins can do it): rooms round a pit, shelter from the wind and snow aloft;
  - **from above, at night, glowing round holes in the deck**: a striking motif;
  - the pits can hold the canon's ponds (the well).

## Chefchaouen (Morocco): the blue medina
- **Medina:** founded in 1471 as a fortress by Andalusian refugees, so it has whitewashed houses with inner courts and red
  tile roofs. Walls, doors and **stairs are all washed in shades of blue**, often with a white top; the kasbah walls
  are pisé. Steep stepped lanes.
  [Arch2O](https://www.arch2o.com/?p=240071), [Afar](https://www.afar.com/magazine/the-story-behind-moroccos-most-colorful-town)
- **Palette:** cobalt, azure, powder blue and indigo (≈ #2f5fa8, #4f8fd0, #a9c8ec, #2b3f7a), white #f4f6f8, and
  terracotta pots (#c4643f).
- **For us:** **a whole island in blue** with stepped lanes up its hills, a Spain–Morocco bridge (it is literally
  Andalusian). At night, blue walls under warm lamps give a strong complementary contrast.

## The Fez and Tunis medina warrens; Cairo's stacked quarters
- **Fez el-Bali:** ≈ 9,000 lanes, many under 1 m wide. Houses climb over each other, with **sabat rooms bridging
  the lanes**, and the tanneries' dye vats are a city of pits (Chouara).
- **Tunis:**
  - the medina's courtyard houses face inward to the *sahn*, with arcaded corridors;
  - palaces have porticoes round four sides and T-shaped rooms;
  - **covered vaulted souks** run in lines round the Zitouna mosque, and **sabat** passages run under houses.
  [UNESCO urban heritage atlas: Tunis](https://whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/tunis), [Dar Othman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_Othman)
- **Cairo (haret, the old lanes):** 4–7 storey houses packed along dead-end lanes, with **mashrabiyas almost meeting
  across the lane**. The rooftops carry pigeon towers, shacks and laundry; rab' apartments sit over wikalas.
- **For us:**
  - **"medina warren":** buildings joined across lanes by rooms (sabat), and vaulted covered souk streets running through the ground floors;
  - the roofs are a second city.

## Warren rules (for the generator)
1. **Cells, not buildings:** a warren is a lattice of repeated cells (ghorfa vaults, rooms of a tower house, tool-house
   floors) that the generator stacks, staggers and bridges. The shared components are the cells.
2. **Climb the hill:** on rolling islands, ring the hill with stepped cubes up to a crown (Ghardaïa, Aït Benhaddou).
3. **Stairs outside:** external stairs, ramps, ladders and plank landings on every warren (also the canon's roof access).
4. **Bridge the lane:** sabat rooms and skybridges over every other lane.
5. **Light every door:** a cell door is a shop or a home, and at night each has its own lamp or sign. A ghorfa wall
   lit up is a honeycomb of light.
