# A5. Fantasy art: what it exaggerates in these traditions (and what we take)

Research stream A, 2026-10-14. Sources are game and film art direction. Stream B covers fantasy art more widely;
this note covers only fantasy versions of the Arabic, Spanish and Indian traditions.

- **Assassin's Creed (2007): Damascus, Acre, Jerusalem.**
  - The cities were built at real size from site visits. They sit "between historical fidelity and creative
    freedom", with **rooftop traversal as the core design**.
  - The cities were criticised for too grey a palette; reviewers wanted more colour in banners and cloth.
  - What it exaggerates: **continuous walkable roofscapes** (planks, beams and awnings bridging roofs), wooden
    scaffolds and lookout towers.
  - Lesson: **use colour** (awnings, banners, hung textiles).
  [GameDev: Raphael Lacoste](https://gamedev.net/tutorials/industry/interviews/notes-on-gamedev-raphael-lacoste-r3258), [Visual Review: the urban landscape in Assassin's Creed](https://visualcompublications.es/revVISUAL/article/view/3736)
- **Agrabah (Aladdin, 1992) and Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time.**
  - Exaggerated **onion domes** in candy colours, impossibly slender minarets, stacked domes on domes.
  - Pennants on every top, **hanging bridges and ropes between towers**, palace terraces stepping up.
  - What it exaggerates: the **silhouette** (a city read as a crown of domes and spires). Lesson: give every island
    a **roofline crown**: 2–4 tall landmark tops (minaret, dome, chhatri cluster, clock tower) so each one reads from
    kilometres away.
- **Spirited Away's bathhouse and Howl's Moving Castle (Ghibli; this already fits the game's look):**
  - Buildings **accreted** like Dharavi: additions bolted onto additions, pipes, chimneys, balconies on balconies.
  - Red lanterns at night. (Stream B covers these in depth; they are noted here only as the bridge to Dharavi and
    Shibam.)
- **Dune's Arrakeen and desert kasbahs (Ouarzazate, Aït Benhaddou, and the films shot there):**
  - Massive **battered (sloped) walls**, crenellated tops, corner towers, few windows.
  - What it exaggerates: **mass and taper**. Lesson: Shibam-like tapering towers read as "ancient" even when the
    walls are light skystone.
- **Moebius and Arabian-Nights illustration (Dulac, Nielsen, the Rackham school):**
  - Night cities of **lanterns floating at every window**, domes against a starry blue.
  - Lesson: the **night palette**: deep blue sky (the Spindle's night already is), warm amber windows, gold-leaf
    domes catching the light, coloured-glass lanterns.

## Rules this gives the generator (for every style)
1. **A crown on every island:** a few landmark tops much taller than the mass (domes, minarets, chhatris, clock or
   lantern towers, signage masts).
2. **Bridges between buildings** above the deck (Shibam, the Gothic Quarter, Damascus's sabat, Ghadames's roofs):
   one to four per block. They make the roofs walkable, which the canon's roof access needs.
3. **Accretion on top:** a rooftop layer of shacks, tanks, pigeon lofts, lark cradles and laundry. Its density varies
   by style.
4. **Cloth and colour:** awnings, banners and hung carpets as colour carriers, so the islands aren't grey or beige.
5. **Light as ornament:** coloured fanlights, lantern clusters, bulb festoons, outlined domes.
