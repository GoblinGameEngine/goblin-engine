# A2. Spanish architecture

Research stream A, 2026-10-14. Values marked ≈ are typical estimates.

## Andalusia: pueblos blancos and the Córdoba patio house
- **Pueblos blancos:**
  - lime-washed cubic houses of 2–3 storeys stepping down hillsides;
  - small deep windows with iron **rejas** (grilles) and flower pots;
  - lanes ≈ 2–4 m wide; a castle or church on top.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Towns_of_Andalusia), [NatGeo](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/andalusia-white-towns-were-forged-by-past-epidemics)
  - Paint-layer studies suggest most were ochre-tinted until the 1920s, which allows ochre variants (≈ #e7c98f) beside the white (#f6f3ec).
  - The base is often a painted dado: ochre, grey, or blue band (≈ 0.8 m high).
- **Córdoba patio house:**
  - an inward-facing house: a porch (zaguán) with a grille gate (cancela), then a patio with a well or fountain;
  - a 2-storey gallery round the patio; the lower floor is used in summer, the upper in winter;
  - **blue- and red-painted pots hung all over the whitewashed walls** with geraniums and jasmine;
  - pebble "Chinese" paving.
  [jardineriaon](https://en.jardineriaon.com/Courtyards-of-Cordoba.html), [archeyes](https://archeyes.com/?p=6533)
- **Roofs:** Arabic barrel tiles (≈ #b5552f), and flat azoteas for drying laundry.

## Moorish and Mudéjar
- **Alhambra vocabulary:**
  - **horseshoe and lobed arches**, slender paired columns;
  - **muqarnas** (honeycomb vaults);
  - carved stucco panels (sebka lattice);
  - **zellij/alicatado** tile dados;
  - reflecting pools (the Court of the Myrtles) and four-part gardens.
- **Mudéjar (Aragon, Teruel):**
  - **brick towers covered in geometric brick relief and green-and-white glazed ceramic inlays** (El Salvador, San Martín, the cathedral tower; 12th–14th c.);
  - painted wooden coffered ceilings (Teruel cathedral, the "Sistine Chapel of Mudéjar").
  [UNESCO doc](https://whc.unesco.org/document/163273), [goaragon](https://www.goaragon.eu/discover-the-best-of-mudejar-art-in-teruel-aragon-spain/)
  - Palette: brick ≈ #b9714a, glaze green ≈ #2f7a4d, glaze white #f0ece0, turquoise ≈ #2a8c8c.
- **Artesonado:** an interlaced geometric wooden ceiling. **This is the ceiling-tile precedent:** a coffered grid
  of square or star panels. (The "ceiling_tile" component uses it, along with the modern suspended grid.)

## Cuenca: the hanging houses (casas colgadas)
- Houses **cantilevered over a gorge** on internal walls and timber beams: 3–4 floors, a masonry base, and timber
  upper storeys with **long wooden balconies hung over the void**. On record since the 15th century; only 3 of many
  survive.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanging_Houses_of_Cuenca), [Sacyr](https://sacyr.com/en/-/asi-se-levanta-una-casa-que-cuelga-de-un-acantilado)
- **For the sky islands:** buildings at the deck edge that **overhang the rim**, with balconies over the 750 m drop.
  It fits the islands better than any other precedent. (The layout keeps fences 1 m in, so a "rim" style puts
  cantilevered rooms past the fence with their own railings.)

## Barcelona
- **Eixample (Cerdà, 1859):**
  - blocks ≈ **113 × 113 m** with **45° chamfered corners**;
  - originally built on two sides with a garden inside; later filled in;
  - the height cap was ≈ 16 m at first; now 5–8 storeys;
  - facades have ≈ 1.2 m iron balconies on every floor, a principal floor with bigger openings, and **galerías**
    (glazed enclosed balconies) facing the inner court.
  [latitudefortyone](https://latitudefortyone.com/the-visionary-urban-design-of-the-eixample-district-barcelona), [barcelonaurbana](https://barcelonaurbana.com/en/blog/eixample-barcelona-guide)
  - **Useful for the big islands:** chamfered corner blocks around a courtyard garden.
- **Modernisme (Gaudí):**
  - Casa Batlló: an undulating facade, "bone" columns, bulging mask-like balconies, a **dragon-back roof** of scaled tiles, and **trencadís** (broken-tile mosaic) chimneys and walls.
  - Casa Milà: wave-like stone, kelp-like wrought-iron balconies, and **helmet chimneys** on a roof-terrace landscape.
  [berkshirefinearts](https://berkshirefinearts.com/01-31-2010_antoni-gaudi-s-casa-batllo-and-casa-mila.htm), [thebettervacation](https://thebettervacation.com/barcelona/casa-mila-or-casa-batllo/)
  - Palette: Batlló blues and greens (≈ #3d8fb0, #6fb39b), mosaic accents (#e2b33f, #c8513d); Milà cream stone ≈ #e3d8c0.
  - **For us:** organic roofscapes (the canon's roofs are walked on), mosaic chimneys and vents, wavy balcony rails.
- **Gothic Quarter:** narrow 3–6 m lanes, 4–6 storey stone houses, **bridges between buildings** (the Pont del Bisbe),
  and arcaded squares.

## Canary Islands, Basque Country, Galicia
- **Balcón canario:**
  - long covered wooden balconies on turned posts, in local resinous pine (pino tea, ≈ #6b3f22 dark or #8a5a33);
  - they project over the street, shade the ground floor and ventilate the rooms above;
  - Casa de los Balcones, La Orotava.
  [webtenerife](https://www.webtenerife.co.uk/tenerife/art-and-culture/architecture/), [spainseeker](https://spainseeker.com/en/poi/la-orotava-casa-de-los-balcones)
- **Basque caserío:**
  - a big gabled block in timber and stone with ledged balconies on the first floor and a deep porch;
  - steep roofs (≈ 30–35°), timber galleries under the eaves.
  [Tourism Euskadi](https://tourism.euskadi.eus/webtur00-content/en/contenidos/h_cultura_y_patrimonio/0000012891_h5_rec_turismo/en_12891/12891-ficha2.html)
  - (Our roofs must be flat, so this gives us timber galleries and a roof "porch" pavilion, not the roof shape.)
- **Galicia (A Coruña):** whole facades of **white-framed glazed galerías**, a "city of glass" that glows at night.
  (Well known; not searched this round.)

## Azulejos and signs
- **Azulejos:** tin-glazed tile panels on facades and street nameplates. Blue-white (≈ #2b4f9a on #f4f1e8), or polychrome Sevillian (yellow, green, manganese).
- **Signs:**
  - painted tile shop signs and wrought-iron hanging signs;
  - neon "Farmacia" crosses, cinema marquees (Gran Vía);
  - the Tío Pepe neon rooftop sign (Madrid) as a precedent for **giant rooftop letter signs**.
- **Night:**
  - iron wall lanterns (farolas) with warm light;
  - feria (Seville April fair) arches of **thousands of bulbs and paper lanterns**: the "Christmas tree" precedent;
  - Valencian **Fallas light arches** (Russafa), elaborate LED gateways the size of a building.
