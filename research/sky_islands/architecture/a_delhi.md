# A3. Delhi

Research stream A, 2026-10-14. Values marked ≈ are typical estimates.

## Shahjahanabad (Old Delhi, 1639–48): the urban structure
- The walled city is split into **mohallas** (neighbourhoods), **kuchas** (lanes of houses around courts) and
  **katras** (gated commercial enclaves: a court of shops behind one gate), organised by trade and community.
  [homegrown: havelis of Chandni Chowk](https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-explore/inside-the-disappearing-havelis-of-chandni-chowk), [joinpaperplanes](https://www.joinpaperplanes.com/the-arched-gateways-of-havelis-in-old-delhi/)
- **Chandni Chowk:**
  - a broad market spine (a canal ran down its middle once);
  - off it, **galis** ≈ 1.5–3 m wide, overhung by 3–5 storey buildings;
  - **ground floors are all shops** (≈ 2.5–4 m wide stalls with shutters).
- Wholesale markets cover whole lanes by trade (Kinari Bazaar trims, Dariba Kalan silver, Khari Baoli spices).
  **That matches the canon's specialty islands: one trade per lane.**

## The haveli
- **Plan:**
  - one or more **courtyards**, from public outer rooms to private inner ones;
  - a gateway with a decorated arch, often a carved wooden or stone **darwaza** with a small wicket door;
  - a *deorhi* (entrance vestibule), a raised platform (*chabutra*) at the door;
  - zones for men (mardana), women (zenana) and animals.
- **Height:** 2–4 storeys.
  [homegrown](https://homegrown.co.in/article/806227/inside-the-disappearing-havelis-of-chandni-chowk)
- **Materials:**
  - **lakhori brick** (thin, ≈ 4 cm) with lime and stucco;
  - **red sandstone** brackets, columns and slabs (≈ #a4513b);
  - stucco in white, lime or pastel (≈ #efe6d2, #d9c27e, #9fb8a6).
- **Facade elements:**
  - **Jharokha:** an enclosed balcony window projecting on **carved brackets/corbels**, closed by a **jali**, often
    capped by a **small chhatri dome** or a curved bangla roof. ≈ 1.2–2 m wide, 0.6–1 m deep, 1.8–2.4 m tall.
    [Jharokha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharokha)
  - **Chhajja:** a thin projecting eave slab on brackets over every opening and every storey (≈ 0.6–1.0 m deep). It
    is the strongest horizontal rhythm.
  - **Jali:** a pierced stone screen (geometric or floral), also used as a parapet.
  - **Chhatri:** an umbrella-domed rooftop pavilion on 4–8 columns. **Ideal as a rooftop stair head, lark cradle or
    lookout.** [Chhatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatri)
  - Cusped (multifoil) arches, baluster columns, carved wooden doors with brass bosses.
- **Today:** havelis are subdivided into warehouses and shops, overbuilt with brick additions, and covered in
  **dense hanging bundles of wire, shop boards and banners**. That messy overlay is part of the character.

## Mughal and the contrasts
- **Mughal set pieces:**
  - red sandstone with white marble inlay (the Red Fort, Jama Masjid);
  - onion domes in white marble (≈ #f1ede4);
  - **striped domes** (Jama Masjid has black-and-white marble stripes);
  - four-part gardens (charbagh), pishtaq (tall arched portals).
- **Lutyens' Delhi:** a stripped-classical/Indian hybrid (chhatris on classical blocks, chhajjas as cornices), in
  buff and red Dholpur stone. **This is the "civic" register for halls and infirmaries.**
- **Paharganj Main Bazaar:**
  - one of the densest concentrations of neon in the city: hotel names in **double-lined neon**, now giving way to
    LED and flex banners;
  - narrow lanes branch off the strip, packed with budget guesthouses (500+ hotels and lodges).
  [The Wire photo essay](https://thewire.in/society/photo-essay-look-signage-contemporary-paharganj-gole-market), [sydneycriminallawyers](https://www.sydneycriminallawyers.com.au/blog/the-main-bazaar-in-delhis-paharganj-digital-images-of-the-organised-chaos/)

## Signs and night
- **Signs:**
  - hand-painted shop boards in Devanagari, Urdu and English;
  - vertical signs stacked up building corners;
  - neon hotel signs; **LED chaser borders**.
- **Lights:**
  - wedding and festival lighting (**Diwali "jhalar" strings: cascades of tiny bulbs hung down whole facades**);
  - marigold garlands; lit gateway arches (pandal gates).
- **For SkySigns:**
  - "jhalar" vertical light curtains down a facade;
  - chaser-bulb borders on arches;
  - double-line neon names.
