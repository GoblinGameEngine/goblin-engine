# The two large beach cities

Note: the brief named **Charlotte** and Nantucket for the North Sea city. Charlotte NC is inland; the
earlier list named **Charleston**, so this uses Charleston (to confirm with the user).

## North Sea: Charleston + Nantucket (East Coast)
Photos: `COAST-Charleston-*`, `COAST-Nantucket-*`.

**Charleston SC**
- Layout: the **Grand Model** (1672/1680) -- a grid oriented to the compass on the peninsula tip between
  two rivers, ~300-350 half-acre lots, reserved civic, church and market lots; the **Four Corners of
  Law** at Meeting x Broad (city hall, courthouse, post office, church). East Bay St began as the first
  wharf line (1680-96); wharves came right up to it. **King St** is the long retail spine; the
  **Battery / White Point Garden** is a seawall promenade and live-oak park at the tip; Waterfront Park
  and the City Market (long market shed down the middle of Market St).
  ([CCPL Grand Model](https://www.ccpl.org/charleston-time-machine/grand-model-john-culpepers-1672-plan-charles-town),
  [CCPL East Bay](https://www.ccpl.org/charleston-time-machine/genesis-east-bay-street-charlestons-first-wharf-1680-1696),
  [Broad St](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Street_(Charleston,_South_Carolina)))
- Buildings: the **Charleston single house** (one room wide, gable end to the street, two rooms per floor,
  a long **piazza** on the south or west side for the sea breeze, a street door into the piazza); the
  Charleston cottage; **Rainbow Row** -- Georgian row houses (1740s-1845) with shops below and merchants'
  living quarters above, party walls, individual roof lines, painted colours; churches with tall
  steeples; the market sheds; wharf warehouses.
  ([Single house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston_single_house),
  [Charleston Mag: piazza](https://charlestonmag.com/features/piazza), [Rainbow Row](https://charlestonmag.com/features/rainbow_row))
- Economy: tourism ($14.0 bn impact in 2024, about half of South Carolina's) and the **port** (6th in
  the US by cargo value in 2020, $72 bn). ([Post & Courier](https://www.postandcourier.com/business/tourism/charleston-sc-tourism-record-billion-visitors-economy/article_ecbd8420-dd34-4fa9-85a3-68ae605f02ef.html),
  [Port of Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Charleston))

**Nantucket MA**
- Layout: the town (1673) sits on the harbour; **Main Street** (cobbled 1837, widened after the 1846
  fire) runs ten blocks from Quaker Rd down to **Straight Wharf** (1723); parallel wharves (Straight,
  Old South, Commercial, Steamboat) with shingled shacks on them; lanes of close-packed houses
  climbing from the harbour. ([APA Great Places](https://www.planning.org/greatplaces/streets/2011/mainst.htm),
  [Nantucket HD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantucket_Historic_District))
- Buildings: early lean-to houses; the **typical Nantucket house** (four-bay facade, central chimney,
  weathered grey cedar shingle, roof walk); Federal and Greek Revival captains' mansions; later Victorians;
  wharf shacks. Everything regulated by the Historic District Commission since 1955 (the whole island).
- Economy: whaling -> summer tourism and second homes; population ~5x in summer.

**Amalgam for the North Sea city** (a harbour city on a bay between headlands):
- a harbour front with **parallel wharves** (Nantucket) stepping down from an **East Bay-style wharf street**
  (Charleston), shingled wharf shacks, a ferry/steamboat wharf, a yacht club;
- a **compass-grid old town** behind (Charleston Grand Model) with a Four Corners civic crossing, a long
  retail spine (King St) running inland from the harbour, and a cobbled **Main Street** down to the central wharf;
- a **Battery**: seawall promenade and live-oak park on the headland point;
- a painted **row-house** block on the waterfront street (Rainbow Row); single houses with piazzas and
  grey-shingled Nantucket houses mixed in the residential blocks; church steeples as landmarks;
- the ocean beach on the far side of the headland with a pier and beach houses.

## South Sea: Dana Point + Huntington Beach (California)
Photos: `COAST-DanaPoint-*`, `COAST-HuntingtonBeach-*`.

**Dana Point CA**
- The **harbour** has three parts: the landside strip under the bluffs, an **island** joined by a bridge,
  and two marina basins (East and West, ~2,500 slips) behind a long breakwater; plus a launch ramp, dry
  boat storage, a fishing pier, a shipyard, a fuel dock, three yacht clubs and sport-fishing. Bordered by
  the **Dana Point Headlands** and the Old Cove marine preserve to the west and Doheny State Beach
  to the east. ([OC harbour revitalisation plan](https://bos5.oc.gov/sites/bos5.egovoc.com/files/2024-02/DPH%20LCP%20Amendment%20-%202011.10.06.pdf),
  [OC Parks](https://parks.oc.gov/beaches/dana-point-harbor/history))
- The **Lantern District** town centre sits on the bluff top above the harbour (walkable, nautical
  shops); Pacific Coast Highway runs along the bluff; streets named "Street of the Golden Lantern" etc.

**Huntington Beach CA ("Surf City USA")**
- **Main Street** runs inland from the **pier** at Pacific Coast Highway: restaurants, surf shops,
  bars. The pier (1,850 ft, longest in California) has **Pier Plaza** at its foot (230-seat amphitheatre,
  mural, stairs to the beach and lawn). **Pacific City** (open-air shopping and dining) and "The Strand"
  flank it along PCH. The beach runs for miles in front of PCH with lots and meters.
  ([City of HB](https://www.huntingtonbeachca.gov/business/economic-development/redevelopment/mainpier.cfm),
  [Surf City USA](https://www.surfcityusa.com/things-to-do/attractions/huntington-beach-pier/))

**Amalgam for the South Sea city**: a long straight beach (Huntington) on one side, a harbour in the lee
of a rocky headland (Dana Point) on the other. The coast highway runs along the bluff top; Main Street
climbs inland from the pier with Pier Plaza at its foot and a Pacific City-style low mall beside it; a
bluff-top Lantern District above the harbour; the harbour with a breakwater, two marina basins, an
island with a bridge, a fishing pier, a boatyard and a launch ramp. Stucco and Spanish-revival buildings,
mid-century surf shops, flat-roofed beach houses, palms.
