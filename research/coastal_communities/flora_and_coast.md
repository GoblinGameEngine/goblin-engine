| Region | Beach / foredune | Back dune / shrub | Trees / street trees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jersey Shore** | American beachgrass (*Ammophila breviligulata*), beach pea, seaside goldenrod | bayberry, wax myrtle, beach plum, Virginia rose, eastern baccharis, poison ivy | eastern red cedar, pitch pine, American holly, black cherry |
| **Nantucket** | beachgrass, seaside goldenrod | **beach rose (*Rosa rugosa*)** (first shrub in from the coast), bayberry, beach plum, huckleberry, lowbush blueberry | scrub oak (to ~15 ft), pitch pine; in town: privet hedges, hydrangeas |
| **Charleston** | sea oats | wax myrtle, yaupon holly, palmetto | **southern live oak with Spanish moss**, **sabal palmetto** (state tree), southern magnolia, crape myrtle |
| **Florida** | **sea oats**, railroad vine | **sea grape**, saw palmetto, Spanish bayonet, wax myrtle, yaupon | sabal (cabbage) palm, live oak in the hammock |
| **Great Lakes** | **marram grass**, sand reed grass, little bluestem | sand cherry, common/creeping juniper, bearberry, red-osier dogwood | **cottonwood**, jack pine, white and red pine, paper birch |

For the station: the North Sea coast uses the Atlantic set (beachgrass, rose, bayberry, pitch pine,
cedar; live oak and palmetto in the Charleston city), the South Sea uses the California set (bare
sand, sage scrub and ice plant on the bluffs, fan palms along the coast road), Florida pier town gets
sea oats, sea grape and sabal palms, and Lake Tamsin's shores use the Great Lakes set.

## The natural coast
Real coasts alternate **rocky headlands** and **sandy bays**: waves refract onto the headlands and
erode them into cliffs, while the sheltered bays between collect sand into **headland-bay (crenulate,
log-spiral) beaches**; small ones (under ~1 km) are **pocket beaches**. The bay beach is curved, tight
behind the up-drift headland and straightening down-drift.
([Coastal Wiki: embayed beaches](https://www.coastalwiki.org/wiki/Embayed_beaches),
[DTIC equilibrium headland-bay beaches](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA483142.pdf),
[tutor2u bays & headlands](https://www.tutor2u.net/geography/reference/gcse-geography-landforms-of-erosion-bays-and-headlands-coastal-landscapes-4))

For the map: each sea coast is a chain of headlands (rocky cliffs 10-30 m, boulders at the foot) and
crenulate bays with sandy beaches 40-120 m wide backed by dunes; harbours sit in the lee of a headland
behind a breakwater; the beach towns take the longest beaches.
