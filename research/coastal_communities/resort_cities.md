# Coastal resort cities: East Coast, West Coast, Great Lakes

Placement rule (user, 2026-09-24): **West Coast styles on the South Sea, East Coast styles on the
North Sea, Great Lakes styles on the Kettle River and Lake Tamsin.**  Buildings are chosen from
**1970 onward**, going older only where nothing newer exists.  Photos: `remake/reference/COAST-*`,
`remake/reference/BOARDWALKS/<coast>/` (geotagged), `remake/reference/BRIDGES/`.

East Coast building language after 1970: raised (flood-code) beach houses on pilings with cedar
shingle or vinyl siding and big decks; 1970s-80s condominium blocks and motels with exterior
galleries; shingle-style revival (1980s+) in New England; Lowcountry revival (metal roofs, deep
porches) in the Carolinas; boardwalk arcades in concrete block with big signs.

West Coast building language after 1970: stucco and Spanish-revival low-rises, 1970s-80s
beach condos with balconies stepping up the bluff, mid-century surf shops and taco stands,
Craftsman revival beach cottages, board-formed concrete lifeguard HQs, palms.

Great Lakes building language after 1970: timber-frame and board-and-batten waterfront shops,
condominium towers on the bluffs (1970s-80s), marina buildings with big roofs, Victorian-revival
infill downtowns, pole-built boat storage, harbour lights on steel towers.

## Zoning pattern common to all three
Waterfront / marine commercial on the harbour, a downtown or resort-commercial district one block
back, resort residential and condominium districts on the beach and bluffs, parks and beaches
public (badged on the Jersey Shore), protected dunes, and in California the Coastal Act's
local coastal programmes (public access to every beach, height limits near the shore).
