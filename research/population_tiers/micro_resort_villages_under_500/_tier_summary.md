# Tier Summary: Micro Resort Villages, Population Under 500

This is a **new, single-example "outside range" subfolder**, distinct from the three standard population tiers (villages 500-5,000, small towns 6,000-10,000, small cities 30,000-50,000) and from the new lakeshore-towns 10,000-30,000 tier. It exists to hold one deliberately extreme outlier — **Put-in-Bay, Ohio** (year-round population 154, 2020 census) — which falls far below even the villages tier's 500-person floor.

Put-in-Bay is included not because its raw population is representative of any generator population band, but because its **archetype** (extreme micro-scale seasonal lake-resort) is genuinely distinctive and not producible by scaling down any of the other 36+ researched settlements: it is a Lake Erie island village whose built environment (ferry terminals, a small airport, hotel/marina/winery infrastructure) is sized for a seasonal tourist population many times its ~150-person year-round base, rather than for the resident population itself. No other settlement in this research project shows that population-vs.-infrastructure mismatch at this extreme a ratio.

Generator usage note: treat this file as a labeled special case, not as the low end of a population-driven scaling curve. If the map generator ever needs a "tiny seasonal resort" station-sector variant, this file (and the parallel `research/demographics/community_profiles/put_in_bay_ohio.md` full profile) is the reference; it should not be blended into the villages_500_5000 tier's numeric parameters, since Put-in-Bay's built-form logic (two population states: small always-present core + large seasonal overlay) does not follow the same founding-type/downtown-organization rules derived from that tier's mainland, non-resort towns.
