# Put-in-Bay, Ohio (South Bass Island, Ottawa County)

Population: 154 (2020 census; year-round residents only); 152 est. 2024. Land area 0.46–0.62 sq mi. Located on South Bass Island in Lake Erie — 85 miles west of Cleveland, 35 miles east of Toledo. The surrounding **Put-in-Bay Township** (unincorporated parts of South, Middle, and North Bass Islands beyond the village itself) recorded 813 residents in 2020, up from 633 in 2010 — the village is only the incorporated core of a larger, still-small island township.

## Core layout — Extreme micro-scale seasonal lake-resort archetype
Put-in-Bay is the clearest **extreme seasonal-resort** case in the entire research set: a village of ~150 year-round residents whose built environment and economy are sized for a tourist population that swells dramatically during the April–October season. Incorporated 1877; its founding driver was military/historical rather than agricultural, milling, or rail-based — it served as Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry's naval base during the War of 1812, and the decisive Battle of Lake Erie (September 10, 1813) was fought just offshore. That victory, commemorated by the 352-foot **Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial** (completed 1915, a National Park Service site), seeded the island's tourism identity, which grew through the late 19th/early 20th-century resort-hotel era. The village core sits on the southwestern side of the island alongside the small airfield, with an isthmus separating it from a smaller, purely residential northeastern side of South Bass Island.

## Seasonal population swing (the archetype's defining trait)
The single most distinctive pattern for this micro-resort archetype: **~150 year-round residents vs. a tourist population that is a large multiple of that on summer days and an order of magnitude or more higher on peak event weekends** (exact day-by-day tourist counts were not confirmed against a primary source in this pass — flagged as a gap — but the scale mismatch is well-documented qualitatively: multiple ferry lines running continuous service from Port Clinton and Sandusky, a small commercial airport, and a hotel/bar/winery infrastructure base that is vastly oversized relative to the resident population). Civic/year-round infrastructure is correspondingly minimal — one K-12 school, one grocery store, one bank, no hospital (EMS with helicopter transport instead) — underlining how thin permanent-resident-serving infrastructure is relative to tourist-serving infrastructure (marinas, hotels, bars, wineries). This is the generator-relevant pattern: a micro-resort settlement should be modeled with two population states (a small always-present built-up core sized for ~150–800 residents, and a seasonal overlay of hotel/marina/ferry-terminal capacity sized for a much larger transient population) rather than a single fixed population figure.

## Residential pattern
Extremely small and dense relative to overall land area — 0.46–0.62 sq mi hosts the entire village core. No formal residential-lot data found; layout is presumed compact/walkable, consistent with limited land and heavy tourist foot/golf-cart traffic. Island access is ferry and small-aircraft only (no bridge), so there is no highway grid or rail spine; golf carts and bicycles are the dominant in-village transport mode per general knowledge of the island (not independently re-verified in this research pass).

## Water
**Entirely and exclusively water-founded/water-dependent** — the strongest example of the "founded because of the lake" driver in the whole research set. The village exists only because it sits on a Lake Erie island; its founding (naval base), economy (tourism/ferries), and infrastructure (ferry terminals, marinas, small airport) are 100% water/lake-mediated in a way no mainland settlement in this project's research can match.

## Distinctive buildings
- **Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial** (352 ft, completed 1915) — the island's dominant landmark.
- **Perry's Cave** and **Heineman's Winery/Crystal Cave** (claimed to be the world's largest geode) — tourist infrastructure built around natural karst features.
- Former **Hotel Victory** (opened 1892, once one of the world's largest resort hotels; burned 1919, only foundations remain) — a vivid "boom-era resort ruin" detail for a resort-town archetype.

## Demographics (summary — full table in `research/demographics/community_profiles/put_in_bay_ohio.md`)
**Data-reliability flag: at ~150 residents, ACS/Census margins of error are very large; treat all figures as broadly indicative.** Nominally growing (+18.8% since 2000, City-Data) but this is a handful of residents at this scale, not a meaningful trend. Median household income $64,485 (2024 est.); median age 54.7–55.7 (notably old for a year-round population — retirees/business owners staying island-based off-season while the seasonal workforce is transient/non-resident); average household size 2.2; top employment sectors accommodation & food services (25.7%), transportation & warehousing (18.8% — ferry/marina ops), public administration (13.7%) — a textbook tourism-economy employment signature unlike any mainland settlement in this project.
