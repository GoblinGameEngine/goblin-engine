# Fairfield, Iowa

Population: 9,416 (2020 census) — county seat of Jefferson County, southeast Iowa.

## Founding / water relationship
Founded 1839, named for the natural beauty of the site by an early settler. Unlike river-mill towns (e.g. Charles City), Fairfield is a **prairie/inland county-seat founding** — sited for its central position in the county rather than a specific watercourse, though small creeks run through the surrounding township. This is the other common Iowa county-seat founding pattern besides the river-mill type: pure geometric county-center placement, common where the state legislature designated county seats near the county's geographic center regardless of water access.
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield,_Iowa), [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Fairfield-Iowa)

## Downtown / Main Street layout
- Fairfield uses a classic **courthouse town square**: the historic Jefferson County Courthouse sits at the center of a square block, with commercial storefronts (1-2 story brick) facing inward on all four sides — the archetypal "Iowa courthouse square" pattern (shared with hundreds of Midwest county seats platted after the Ohio/Indiana "Shelbyville square" model).
- Newly designated an official Iowa **Main Street community** (Aug. 2025), meaning downtown revitalization/design-standard funding is now flowing to the square, and mixed international-cuisine/retail/public-art infill is being actively added — a sign this square is a living, still-commercially-viable core, not a hollowed-out shell (relevant if the generator wants a "still-active downtown" preset).
- Historic Carnegie Library and Fairfield Arts & Convention Center anchor civic activity near, but not on, the square itself.
Source: [Opportunity Iowa — Main Street designation](https://opportunityiowa.gov/press-release/2025-08-05/fairfield-becomes-iowas-newest-main-street-community), [Visit Fairfield history](https://www.visitfairfieldiowa.com/about-our-vibe/history)

## Residential zoning (from city zoning title 20)
Fairfield's Title 20 Zoning Code defines multiple residential districts (R-1, R-2, R-3, R-5). Confirmed text found: **rear yard setback of 35 ft applies on through-lots deeper than 95 ft**, and on through lots the front-yard setback rule applies to *both* street frontages — i.e. corner/through lots are treated as having two "front" yards, a common small-town zoning quirk that keeps consistent street-facing setbacks on every platted frontage (relevant for generator: through-lots at block edges should not get a "cheat" backyard setback facing a second street).
Source: [City of Fairfield Title 20 Zoning](https://cityoffairfieldiowa.com/DocumentCenter/View/12190/Title-20---Zoning), [Ordinance 1217 setback amendment](https://www.cityoffairfieldiowa.com/DocumentCenter/View/11951/1217-Amending-Setback-Requirements)

## Distinctive building types
- Jefferson County Courthouse — central square anchor, the largest building mass in town by footprint besides big-box retail at the highway edge.
- Carnegie Library (housing the Carnegie Museum) — standard-issue early-1900s Carnegie library building type found in hundreds of small Midwest towns: symmetrical, 1-1.5 story, classical-revival stone/brick, small footprint (roughly 60-80 ft per side is typical for small-town Carnegie libraries generally).
Source: [Visit Fairfield history](https://www.visitfairfieldiowa.com/about-our-vibe/history)
