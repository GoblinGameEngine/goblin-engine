# Rice Lake, Wisconsin

Population: 9,040 (2020 census) — Barron County, northwestern Wisconsin.

## Founding / water relationship
Founded 1864 when the Knapp, Stout & Co. logging firm built a storage dam on **Rice Lake** (named for wild rice historically harvested there by the Ojibwe). This is the tier's clearest **logging/mill-dam founding** case — distinct from the grain-agriculture river-mill towns of Ohio/Iowa: the water body here is a lake formed/enlarged by a log-driving dam, tied to the North Woods timber economy rather than flour milling or grain shipping. Incorporated as a city 1887 (pop. 2,130 in 1890); electric lights and water utility arrived 1892, installed by the lumbering firms themselves — i.e., **private industry, not municipal government, built the first utilities**, a common pattern in company-dominated small towns of this era.
Source: [Rice Lake Chamber history](https://ricelakechamber.org/local-attractions/history/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_Lake,_Wisconsin)

## Downtown / Main Street layout
- Historic downtown centers on **North Main Street**; the Carnegie library (opened 1897, occupied its Carnegie-funded building 1905-1976) exemplifies the standard small-town Carnegie library building type also seen in Fairfield, IA.
- Rice Lake functions as a regional retail hub for a roughly 50-mile trade radius (the largest community between Eau Claire, 60 mi south, and Superior, 100 mi north) — meaning its downtown/commercial footprint is somewhat larger relative to in-town population than a typical isolated small town, because it serves a much larger rural catchment. Useful generator parameter: "regional hub" small towns can have oversized downtown commercial area relative to a purely population-driven formula.
Source: [Rice Lake Chamber history](https://ricelakechamber.org/local-attractions/history/)

## Distinctive water/property-value relationship
Because the town's namesake lake is a scenic/recreational amenity (not just an industrial water source), Rice Lake illustrates the "lake resort small town" variant: lakefront and near-lake residential property is typically the most desirable in town, distinct from the river-mill towns where the highest-value land is the historic downtown core itself. Downtown is not immediately lakefront — it sits a short distance from the shoreline, with the prime lake-adjacent land instead used for residential/recreational purposes.
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_Lake,_Wisconsin)
