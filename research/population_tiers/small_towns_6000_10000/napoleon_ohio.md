# Napoleon, Ohio

Population: ~8,561 (2023 est.) — county seat of Henry County, northwest Ohio, 6.2 sq mi city area.

## Founding / water relationship
Founded 1832 in what was then the "Great Black Swamp," opened to settlement after the 1794 Battle of Fallen Timbers. Sited directly on the **Maumee River**, which runs through town and was the primary transportation/trade artery. The **Miami and Erie Canal**, completed 1843, paralleled/used the river corridor and brought a wave of German immigrant settlement; by the 1880s the canal plus two rail lines had pushed the population past 3,000. This is a **triple-transport founding stack** (river -> canal -> rail), a richer version of the common "water then rail" two-stage pattern seen elsewhere in this tier — useful if the generator wants a "canal town" variant with a disused/repurposed canal channel near downtown.

## Downtown / Main Street layout
- Downtown fronts the Maumee River with red-brick historic storefronts, restaurants, and shops with "ample walking space."
- Civic/institutional landmarks include the old post office and **Saint Augustine Cathedral** (a Catholic cathedral — unusually grand for a town this size, reflecting the German Catholic immigrant settlement wave) and **the Armory**, an arts/events center built to resemble a medieval fort — an example of a small town having one architecturally unusual civic landmark distinct from the generic storefront stock (same pattern as McCook's Wright house or Wapakoneta's space museum).
