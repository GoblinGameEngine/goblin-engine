# Bryan, Ohio

Population: 8,729 (2020 census) — county seat of Williams County, northwest Ohio.

## Founding / water relationship
Platted 1840 by John A. Bryan (former Ohio state auditor, who donated land for the county seat), incorporated as a village 1841, reincorporated as a city 1941. Known as the **"Fountain City"** for a network of underground artesian springs that supplied fresh water and food preservation for early settlers — a groundwater-based founding rationale rather than a surface river/mill site, a third variant (alongside river-mill and rail-junction founding) worth supporting in the generator: towns sited for **reliable well/spring water** rather than a visible stream.
Source: [City of Bryan history](https://www.cityofbryan.com/history/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan,_Ohio)

## Downtown / Main Street layout — concrete street widths (confirmed)
Bryan's historic platting specifies exact street widths, a rare piece of hard numeric data for this tier:
- **Main Street and High Street: 100 feet wide** (curb-to-curb + right-of-way, the commercial spine streets)
- **All other streets: 66 feet wide** (the standard "one Gunter's chain" width used across huge swaths of 19th-c. American town plats — 66 ft = 4 rods, a very common historic platting unit)
This 100 ft vs. 66 ft split (wide commercial spine, narrower standard grid) is a strong, reusable numeric rule for the generator: **make the primary Main Street ~1.5x the width of ordinary residential/grid streets.**
Source: [History of Bryan Ohio](https://ohiogenealogy.org/williams/history-of-bryan-ohio.htm)

## Civic anchor
Williams County Courthouse built 1888, located on the **city square** — the classic Ohio courthouse-square pattern (courthouse lawn at the center, commercial storefronts facing it on all sides), same archetype as Fairfield, IA.
Source: [History of Bryan Ohio](https://ohiogenealogy.org/williams/history-of-bryan-ohio.htm)

## Downtown character
Main Street/downtown lined with historic storefronts, including long-standing bank branches — 1-2 story masonry construction typical of the tier, zero-setback.
Source: [City of Bryan](https://www.cityofbryan.com/)
