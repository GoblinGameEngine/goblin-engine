# McCook, Nebraska

Population: 7,446 (2020 census) — county seat of Red Willow County, southwest Nebraska High Plains.

## Founding / water relationship
Platted 1882 specifically as a **railroad town**: a planned agreement between the Burlington & Missouri River Railroad and the Lincoln Land Company created McCook as a new division point roughly halfway between Denver and Omaha. This is the pure "railroad company town" founding pattern — distinct from the river-mill or courthouse-center patterns seen in the Ohio/Iowa examples in this tier: the town exists because the railroad and a land company chose the spot, not because of pre-existing water power. McCook does sit on the **Republican River**, but the river is a secondary geographic feature relative to the rail line as the actual founding driver.
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCook,_Nebraska), [City of McCook history](https://www.cityofmccook.com/153/History-of-McCook)

## Downtown / Main Street layout
- The main commercial spine is **Norris Avenue** (north-south), not "Main Street" — a naming variant worth supporting in a generator (some towns name the spine after a local notable, here Senator George Norris, rather than "Main St").
- Norris Ave. hosts civic/cultural anchors at specific numbered addresses (Museum of the High Plains at 421 Norris Ave.; the Sutton House, a Frank Lloyd Wright design, at 602 Norris Ave.) — the presence of house numbers in the 400-600 range over what is described as compact downtown blocks implies roughly **100 numbers per block**, consistent with standard American block-numbering (usually 1 block = 100 address numbers, regardless of actual block length, which typically runs 300-400 ft in railroad towns of this era).
- Being a railroad division town, downtown grew parallel and adjacent to the rail corridor/depot (McCook station), a layout distinct from river towns where downtown often turns to face the water.
Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCook,_Nebraska), [Visit McCook](https://visitmccook.com/about-us/), [McCook station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCook_station)

## Traffic / zoning categorization
- County seat + rail division point + agricultural trade center for the surrounding High Plains wheat/cattle region — three overlapping functions in one small downtown, typical of Plains railroad towns (contrast with Ohio/Iowa towns where courthouse function alone often anchors downtown).
- Rail corridor + adjacent grain-handling and industrial uses form a distinct zone from the Norris Ave. retail core, per the standard Plains railroad-town layout (depot/elevator row on one side of downtown, retail spine one to two blocks over).
Source: [Britannica — McCook](https://www.britannica.com/place/McCook)

## Distinctive building types
- **Sutton House** (602 Norris Ave.) — a Frank Lloyd Wright Usonian-era design, a rare "architect-designed landmark house" outlier within an otherwise vernacular small-town housing stock; useful as a generator "one unique landmark building" slot.
- **Museum of the High Plains** (421 Norris Ave.) — repurposed civic building housing fossil collections and local-history exhibits, the same "one regional museum" pattern seen in Wapakoneta.
- Grain elevators and rail depot buildings cluster along the tracks at the edge of the retail core — see `_tier_summary.md` for generic elevator footprint figures (small-town elevators typically ~25-90 ft footprint, 70-125 ft tall).
Source: [Visit McCook](https://visitmccook.com/about-us/)
