# Avon Lake, Ohio (Lorain County)

Population: 25,206 (2020 census; up 11.6% from 22,581 in 2010); 26,342 est. 2024 (+45.2% since 2000). Land area 11.13 sq mi, no significant inland water — fronts directly on Lake Erie. On Lake Erie's southern shore, ~17 miles west of Cleveland.

## Core layout — Growing exurban/bedroom-community archetype
Avon Lake has **no traditional courthouse-square or rail-corridor downtown**; it grew as a 20th-century Cleveland exurb built directly along the Lake Erie shoreline rather than around a historic platted town core. First settled early 19th century (Peter Miller House, 1830, survives) but stayed rural/sparse — 904 residents in 1920 — before exploding to 9,403 by 1960 (city status granted that year) and growing steadily since to 25,206 (2020). This is the clearest **"lake-adjacent bedroom-suburb growth"** case in the research set: population growth driven by Cleveland-metro commuting and lakefront residential desirability rather than an internal town-founding economic engine. Commercial development is corridor/strip-style along the main east-west arterials (consistent with a car-oriented postwar exurb) rather than a single Main Street.

## Residential pattern
Suburban post-1950s subdivision pattern typical of a Cleveland exurb; no historic platted grid core. Average household size 2.53 (2010 census; down from 2.70 in 2000 — a declining-household-size trend typical of a maturing suburb). Owner-occupied housing at **79.8%** (2020) / 83.1% (2024 est.) — a strongly owner-dominated, single-family-home suburb, notably higher-ownership than the declining rust-belt towns in this same new tier (Sandusky, Ashtabula).

## Water
**Lakefront-sited, but only recently lake-oriented in its built form.** Avon Lake sits directly on Lake Erie with no separate inland water body. For most of the 20th century the waterfront's dominant use was industrial: the **Avon Lake Power Plant**, a coal-fired plant built 1925–26 (one of the largest in the world when completed; 652 MW nameplate capacity at peak) occupied a long stretch of shoreline and drew lake water for cooling. It was decommissioned April 1, 2022, with final structures removed by implosion in December 2024; the city has announced plans to convert the site into a **public lakefront park** — a concrete, dated "industrial lakefront → post-industrial park" conversion, distinct from (but the same underlying pattern as) Sandusky's rail-yard-to-marina conversion in this same tier.

## Distinctive buildings
- Peter Miller House (1830) — pre-industrial-era survivor.
- Former Avon Lake Power Plant site (decommissioned 2022, imploded Dec. 2024) — slated to become a public lakefront park.
- Inland industrial employers: Lubrizol, Ford Motor Company, PolyOne Corporation — a manufacturing base coexisting with the bedroom-suburb residential majority.

## Demographics (summary — full table in `research/demographics/community_profiles/avon_lake_ohio.md`)
Growing (+45.2% since 2000); median household income $124,334 (2024 est., far above Ohio's $72,212 — highest of the six new lakeshore/gap-tier settlements); median age 44.4–45.0; 79.8% homeownership; poverty rate 3.2%; 49.3% bachelor's+ — the most affluent settlement in the entire expanded research set.
