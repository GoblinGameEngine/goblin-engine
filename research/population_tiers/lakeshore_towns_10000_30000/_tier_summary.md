# Tier Summary: Lakeshore Towns, Population 10,000-30,000

This is a **new tier subfolder**, added to fill a gap between the existing villages (500-5,000), small towns (6,000-10,000), and small cities (30,000-50,000) tiers: no prior tier covered the roughly 10,000-30,000 population band, and none of the 36 generic-Midwest settlements researched previously were sited directly on a Great Lakes shoreline. It holds 3 towns, all directly on the south shore of Lake Erie in Ohio, chosen to fill archetype gaps not represented in the generic-Midwest set: a growing exurban/bedroom suburb, a larger-scale lake-resort/tourism-and-retiree city, and a moderately-declined rust-belt harbor-industrial city.

| Town | State | 2020 Pop. | 2024 est. | Trend | Archetype |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Avon Lake | OH | 25,206 | 26,342 | growing (+45.2% since 2000) | Growing exurban/bedroom community |
| Sandusky | OH | 25,095 | 24,174 | declining (-13.2% since 2000) | Lake-resort/tourism-and-retiree, larger scale |
| Ashtabula | OH | 17,975 | 17,775 | declining (-15.2% since 2000) | Declining rust-belt/harbor-industrial, moderate |

## Cross-town patterns

**Downtown organization — three distinct presets, none identical to the generic-Midwest courthouse-square/rail-spine/park-square trio:**
- **No traditional downtown at all** (Avon Lake): a postwar Cleveland exurb built as corridor/strip commercial development along arterials, with no historic platted town core — the lakeshore equivalent of a purely suburban growth pattern.
- **Diagonal/Masonic-pattern grid opening directly onto the water** (Sandusky): the Kilbourne Plat's diagonal avenues cut across a standard grid, and the historic commercial core (Shoreline Dr./Washington Row/Hancock/Decatur) sits directly against Sandusky Bay.
- **Bifurcated upland-core + separate harbor-district** (Ashtabula): a genuinely split downtown, with civic/inland commercial functions separated by distance from a distinct historic harbor-front commercial district (Bridge St./Ashtabula Harbour Commercial District) down at the river mouth. This is a new preset worth adding to the generator alongside the courthouse-square/rail-spine/park-square options already established in the small-towns tier.

**Water-and-property-value pattern:** all three towns show the same underlying "industrial-to-amenity waterfront conversion" seen in the small-cities tier (Manitowoc), but at different stages/triggers — Avon Lake's is a 2022-2024 power-plant-to-park conversion, Sandusky's is a completed rail-yard-to-marina conversion, and Ashtabula's harbor remains a working-into-declining industrial harbor (coal pier closed 2016) that has not yet been redeveloped for amenity use. The generator could model these three as sequential stages of the same lakefront-industrial-decline process.

**Economic divergence at similar/larger population scale:** Avon Lake (growing, high-income, 79.8-83.1% homeownership, 3.2% poverty) and Sandusky (declining, mid-income, 25.6% poverty) sit at almost identical 2020 populations (~25,100-25,200) despite radically different trajectories and built-form implications — useful evidence that population size alone should not drive the generator's affluence/decline parameters; founding-and-water-relationship type (bedroom-suburb lakefront vs. sheltered-bay tourism port) matters more.

**Tourism vs. resident-population divergence:** Sandusky's Cedar Point-driven tourism economy (accommodation & food services a top-3 employer sector) coexists with a shrinking resident population — a useful generator lesson that a town's seasonal/tourist economic intensity and its year-round population trend can move in opposite directions.

## Assumptions and gaps
Lighter-touch research pass consistent with the rest of this project: Wikipedia, City-Data.com, and DataUSA were the primary sources; no GIS/satellite imagery was inspected. Several fields (homeownership % for Sandusky, bachelor's+ % for Sandusky and Ashtabula, current average household size for Ashtabula) were not cleanly isolated and are flagged as gaps in the per-town files and in `research/demographics/community_profiles/`.
