# Sandusky, Ohio (Erie County seat)

Population: 25,095 (2020 census); 24,174 est. 2024 (-13.2% since 2000). Land area 9.64 sq mi, water area 12.19 sq mi (the city's legal boundary includes a large share of Sandusky Bay). On Lake Erie's southern shore/Sandusky Bay, midway between Toledo (45 mi) and Cleveland (50 mi).

## Core layout — Lake-resort/tourism-and-retiree archetype, larger scale
Founded 1818 on the southeast shore of Sandusky Bay, incorporated 1824. The original town plat is a documented, distinctive pattern: surveyor **Hector Kilbourne** laid out a modified grid with avenues cutting diagonally across it, reportedly echoing Masonic symbolism — a genuinely unusual, sourced downtown-plan geometry, distinct from the standard courthouse-square or rail-spine grids seen elsewhere in this research set. The **Downtown Sandusky Commercial Historic District** (NRHP-listed 2017) is roughly bounded by Shoreline Dr., Washington Row, and Hancock and Decatur Sts. — i.e., the historic commercial core runs right up against the bay shoreline (Shoreline Drive). The historic waterfront industrial/rail zone has been substantially **redeveloped as marina dockage** (Battery Park Marina occupies a former railroad site) as 20th-century shipping activity declined — an "industrial waterfront → marina/tourism waterfront" conversion, the same underlying pattern as Avon Lake's power-plant-to-park conversion in this same tier, but arriving via a different original industry (rail/shipping vs. electricity generation).

## Residential pattern
Standard platted grid off the Kilbourne diagonal-avenue plan; 13,351 housing units (2020), average household size 2.28 (2010 census) — the smallest average household size of the three new lakeshore towns, consistent with an older, more rental-heavy population.

## Water
**Directly water-founded, bay-and-lake-facing.** Sandusky sits on Sandusky Bay, a large drowned-river-mouth embayment of Lake Erie, giving it a sheltered harbor distinct from the open-lake-exposed towns in this tier (Avon Lake, Ashtabula). This sheltered-bay siting underlies both its historic shipping-port role and its present role as the **ferry gateway to the Lake Erie islands** (Kelleys Island, Put-in-Bay/South Bass Island, Middle Bass Island, North Bass Island) — directly linking this tier's Sandusky entry to the micro-resort-tier Put-in-Bay entry.

## Distinctive buildings
- **Cedar Point** amusement park ("America's Roller Coast," second-oldest theme park in the US, 18 roller coasters) — occupies a peninsula at the harbor mouth and is the dominant land-use/economic anchor; operator Six Flags is the top employer (6,590 jobs), an extraordinary single-employer concentration for a city this size.
- Adjacent large-scale seasonal-resort lodging cluster: Cedar Point Shores, Castaway Bay, Great Wolf Lodge, Kalahari Resorts.
- Historic house/maritime museums: Cooke-Dorn House, Follett House Museum, Maritime Museum of Sandusky, Merry-Go-Round Museum.
- Ferry terminal(s) at Jackson Street Pier serving the Lake Erie islands, plus a US Customs entry point downtown.
- Battery Park Marina (former rail/industrial waterfront, now marina).

## Demographics (summary — full table in `research/demographics/community_profiles/sandusky_ohio.md`)
Declining (-13.2% since 2000, despite the tourism economy — resident population and tourism revenue diverge here); median household income $51,053 (2024 est.); median age 36.5–39.5; homeownership not cleanly isolated; poverty rate 25.6% (high despite tourism, consistent with a seasonal/service-wage job base); accommodation & food services is a top-3 employment sector (13.2%), the clearest tourism-economy signature among the three lakeshore-tier towns.
