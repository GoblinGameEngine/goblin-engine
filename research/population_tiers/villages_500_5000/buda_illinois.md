# Buda, Illinois (Bureau County)

Population: 482 (2020 census); down from a peak of 990 in 1890. Land area 1.02 sq mi, 0 sq mi water. Computed density ≈ 470/sq mi. Coordinates 41.32889°N, 89.67917°W, elevation 745 ft. Concord Township.

## Core layout
Founded 1854 explicitly to serve the Chicago, Burlington & Quincy Railroad; incorporated as a village in 1869. This is a classic **railroad-siding town**: the original commercial core is a short strip fronting the rail line, historically 1-2 blocks of Main Street. At under 500 current residents, the active commercial core today is reduced to a handful of buildings (church, village hall, post office, a bar/tavern, maybe one shop) — likely fewer than 10 occupied storefronts. No formal downtown square; layout is a simple crossroads/strip pattern typical of towns platted directly by a railroad company rather than around a courthouse.

## Residential pattern
Population has been declining for over a century (990 → 482), which in built form usually means the platted grid is *larger than the occupied footprint* — i.e., there are more empty or demolished lots within the original plat than the population would suggest. Residential area is a small grid of 1-3 streets parallel/perpendicular to the rail line, with farmland reached within a couple of blocks of the platted edge (land area only 1.02 sq mi total, so core-to-farmland distance is short, roughly 300-500 m from the geometric center).

## Traffic/zoning
Minimal. Non-residential uses cluster right along the old rail-facing Main Street: church(es), post office, a tavern/bar (very common survivor business in tiny Illinois villages), and likely a volunteer fire department. Bureau Valley CUSD 340 (formed via consolidation from 1960) means the school itself is NOT local — students bus to a consolidated district school outside Buda, a very common pattern at this population size (see synthesis file).

## Parking
Informal on-street parking along Main Street; no evidence of formal lots at this scale.

## Water
No mapped water area within city limits (0.00 sq mi water, per Census). Buda is a **railroad town, not a water-sited town** — it displaced the earlier settlement of French Grove (which had been the local post office) once the railroad by-passed it. This is an important counter-example: not every small Midwestern village sits on a creek; many exist purely because a 19th-century rail line crossed there.

## Topography/drainage
No specific data found; flat glaciated till-plain terrain typical of Bureau County. Assume standard roadside drainage ditches (see synthesis).

## Distinctive buildings
- Buda Engine Co. — an early-1900s industrial works founded by George Chalender in 1910 (later relocated to Harvey, IL). Notable as an example of small manufacturing that briefly existed in a village this size before consolidating to a larger city — a useful "boom-then-decline" story beat for a generator, not a structure to model literally today.
