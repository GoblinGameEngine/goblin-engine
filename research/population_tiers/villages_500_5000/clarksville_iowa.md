# Clarksville, Iowa (Butler County)

Population: 1,264 (2020 census); peaked at 1,424 around 1980, then declined 12.2% from 2010's 1,439. Land area 1.31 sq mi (1.35 sq mi total incl. water), water 0.039 sq mi. Official density 965.9/sq mi. Coordinates 42°46′47″N 92°40′05″W, elevation 932 ft.

## Core layout
Clarksville is a genuine **mill-site founding** on the Shell Rock River: George Washington Adair dammed the river and built a sawmill in 1853, then a flouring (flour) mill in 1867 at the corner of N. Cherry St. and W. Water St., on the northeast bank of the river — i.e., the town's origin point is a specific, named mill site directly on the water, predating formal incorporation (1874) by two decades. Today, Schmadeke Feed Mill still operates on Main St., showing continuity of ag-processing use near the historic mill site. This is one of the clearest sourced "founded on water for power" examples in this sample (contrast with the rail-only towns: Buda, Wyanet, Dysart, Toulon, Plainview).

## Water
**Directly water-founded.** The Shell Rock River both powered the original sawmill/flour mill (1853/1867) and continues to run along the northeast edge of the historic core. This is a strong example for the synthesis "founding relationship to water" pattern: dam + mill siting, not just proximity.

## Topography/drainage
River-adjacent core implies the town respects a floodplain edge near N. Cherry/W. Water Streets; no specific flood-avoidance data found but the street name "Water St." itself marks the historic river-frontage edge of the plat.
