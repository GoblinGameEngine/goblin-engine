# Hoopeston, Illinois (Vermilion County) — upper edge of tier

Population: 4,915 (2020 census), down from 5,351 in 2010 — sits right at the top of the 500-5,000 village/small-town tier, useful as the upper bound reference. Land area 3.69 sq mi, 0 sq mi water. Computed density ≈ 1,332/sq mi.

## Core layout
Hoopeston is a **double-railroad crossing town**: laid out in 1871 at the intersection of the Lafayette, Bloomington & Western Railroad and the Chicago, Danville & Vincennes Railroad (the latter still operates today, jointly run by CSX/Union Pacific), named for Thomas Hoopes, who donated land for the crossing. The two crossing rail lines **"separated the town into four sections"** — a directly sourced structural fact: a village/small-town founded at a rail crossing can organically end up with a quadrant layout defined by the two rail corridors rather than a single Main Street axis. Incorporated as a village in 1874 at ~1,000 population, later incorporated as a city in 1877 — i.e., Hoopeston crossed from "village" to "city" governance status at roughly the 1,000-population mark, useful as a rule-of-thumb threshold if the generator distinguishes village vs. city governance flavor.

## Traffic/zoning
Historic economy built on ag-processing directly tied to downtown/near-downtown industrial sites: sweet-corn canning and machinery manufacturing. This supports the "Sweetcorn Capital of the World" identity and the **Hoopeston National Sweetcorn Festival**, continuously held since 1938 — the oldest continuously-run sweet corn festival, and a strong festival/Main-Street-closure beat for a generator (an annual event that shuts down and reprograms the commercial core).
