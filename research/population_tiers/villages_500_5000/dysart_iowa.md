# Dysart, Iowa (Tama County)

Population: 1,281 (2020 census); peaked at 1,379 in 2010 (a 7.1% decline 2010→2020). Land area 1.25 sq mi, 0 sq mi water. Computed density ≈ 1,025/sq mi. Coordinates 42.17°N, 92.31°W, elevation 974 ft.

## Core layout
Pure **railroad town**: founded 1872 following construction of the Burlington, Cedar Rapids & Northern Railway, platted the same year, incorporated 1881, named for founder/farmer Joseph Dysart. Grew quickly to 600 people by 1879 (7 years after founding) — a fast initial railroad-boom growth curve, then a long plateau/slow-decline phase (peak not until 2010, well over a century later, then decline). This "fast boom, long plateau, gentle modern decline" curve is a useful population-history template distinct from Elma's "boom-then-bust-back-to-baseline" curve.

Notable: served by the **Union Community School District**, formed in 1993 by merging two prior districts, which maintains "learning centers" in *both* Dysart and neighboring La Porte City — i.e., a two-town consolidated district that still keeps a physical school presence in each town rather than concentrating in one. This is a softer consolidation pattern than Elma's (full closure), useful as a middle-ground option for the generator: villages at ~1,200-1,300 population may retain a partial/satellite school building even after formal district consolidation, whereas villages under ~600 (Elma) are more likely to lose the building entirely.
