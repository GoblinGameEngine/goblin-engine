# Roanoke, Illinois (Woodford County)

Population: 1,960 (2020 census); an estimated 1,878 as of July 2025. Land area 0.94 sq mi land / 0.97 sq mi total (0.035 sq mi water). Computed density ≈ 2,085/sq mi — **the highest density in this 12-village sample**, likely a legacy of its former coal-mining-town phase. Founded 1872, incorporated as a village 1874, named after Roanoke, Virginia.

## Core layout
Rare, precisely sourced original-plat data: **the original plat consisted of 15 blocks**, bounded by Main, Front, Ann, and Pleasant Streets. This is a directly usable concrete figure for a procedural generator — a village-tier original town plat on the order of ~15 blocks at incorporation. **Broad Street** (not Main St.) serves as the actual downtown commercial spine — a reminder that the "Main Street" naming convention isn't universal; a generator should allow the primary commercial street to be independently named from the platted street grid's nominal "Main Street." The historic rail station still stands, though the rail line itself was dismantled in the mid-1980s — another example (with Wyoming/Toulon) of a now-decommissioned rail corridor within an otherwise-active village.

## Residential pattern
Higher density than typical for this tier (2,085/sq mi vs. sample range of ~410-1,530 elsewhere), consistent with Roanoke's history as a **company/mining town**: the second coal shaft in Woodford County was sunk in Roanoke in 1881 and at peak employed roughly 300 workers before closing in 1940. Company/mining-origin villages in this population tier can be expected to have tighter historic platting (smaller lots, denser worker housing) than pure farm-service villages of similar current population.

## Traffic/zoning
No formal zoning data found. A grain elevator system (farmer-owned co-op) serves Roanoke as one of four locations (with Benson, Cooper, Evans) totaling 24 million bushels capacity across all four sites — confirms an active elevator presence but doesn't isolate Roanoke's individual share.

## Parking
No specific data found; assume informal on-street along Broad Street.

## Water
0.035 sq mi of mapped water within city limits — modest but nonzero, more than most other villages in this sample; no specific named water body identified in this research pass, but the nonzero figure combined with "Front Street" as one of the four original-plat boundary streets suggests the original plat may have fronted a creek or drainage feature (an assumption, flagged as such).

## Distinctive buildings
- Historic rail depot/station — still standing, though the rail line was dismantled mid-1980s.
- Roanoke-Benson High School, 208 W. High St. — a two-town consolidated district name (Roanoke-Benson), another concrete example of the small-town school-consolidation naming convention (paired-town district names like "Union," "Roanoke-Benson," "Howard-Winneshiek" recur across this sample).
- Site of a 1906 mine disaster (four men fell 400 ft down the main shaft) — a possible historical-marker/memorial beat rather than a structure.
