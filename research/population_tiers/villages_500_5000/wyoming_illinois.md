# Wyoming, Illinois (Stark County)

Population: 1,300 (2020 census) — the **largest community in rural Stark County** (larger even than the county seat, Toulon, pop. 1,193). Land area 0.85 sq mi. Density ≈ 1,522-1,529/sq mi (highest-density non-coal-town example in this sample). Founded May 3, 1836 by General Samuel Thomas (War of 1812 veteran); named after Pennsylvania's Wyoming Valley.

## Core layout
Historic downtown with a defined identity program: murals depicting the 1836 founding and early-20th-century scenes, a vintage street clock, and the First Congregational Church with a distinctive pointed tower anchoring the streetscape. The former CB&Q railroad depot now serves as headquarters for Rock Island Trail State Park (the same rail-trail that terminates at Toulon) — another adaptive-reuse depot example. A **grain elevator complex on East Williams Street is explicitly noted as the town's tallest structure** — directly confirms the "grain elevator as skyline landmark" pattern for the synthesis file, and gives a named street (E. Williams St) for elevator placement relative to the platted grid, i.e., elevator sits on a side street near the rail corridor rather than directly on Main Street.

## Residential pattern
Land area only 0.85 sq mi — among the smallest in this sample — yet holds 1,300 people, giving the highest "organic" density of the sample (excluding the ex-coal-town Roanoke). Predominantly flat surrounding farmland; expect farmland within roughly 300-400 m of any built edge given the small total footprint.

## Traffic/zoning
No formal zoning data found. Non-residential anchors: First Congregational Church (with tower, presumably near Main St.), grain elevator complex (E. Williams St, off the core), former depot (adaptively reused, off-Main).

## Parking

## Water
Sited **roughly half a mile north of the Spoon River**, with two small unnamed creeks in the town's southern portion draining toward the Spoon River. This is a clear "near water but core set back from the floodplain" pattern: the platted town core is not directly on the river, but tributary creeks pass close enough on the south side to matter for drainage/flood planning — a useful middle case between Clarksville (built directly on/at the river) and Toulon/Dysart/Buda/Plainview (no water feature at all).

## Topography/drainage
Predominantly flat, farmland-fringed landscape; the two small unnamed creeks noted above are the local drainage network feeding the Spoon River — a nice small-scale "creek feeds larger regional river" example matching the requested drainage-pattern research point.
