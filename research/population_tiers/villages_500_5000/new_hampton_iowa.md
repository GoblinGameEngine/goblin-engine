# New Hampton, Iowa (Chickasaw County seat)

Population: 3,494 (2020 census); peaked at 3,940 in 1980. Land area 3.36 sq mi, water only 0.0039 sq mi (essentially none). Density 1,042.3/sq mi (official). Coordinates 43.05639°N, 92.31528°W, elevation 1,158 ft.

## Core layout
County-seat town, founded c. 1855, incorporated April 26, 1873. The **Chickasaw County Courthouse, built 1929**, is the "center for county services" and the anchor of downtown — a later (1929, not mid-1800s) courthouse than Toulon's 1856-57 building, showing that courthouse construction dates vary widely even among similarly-sized county seats and are not always original to the founding era. Downtown Main Street supports enough contiguous, walkable storefront density to host two recurring public events that depend on a continuous commercial streetscape: the **Heartland Days parade down Main Street** (every June) and a **"Live Windows & Light Parade"** holiday event featuring interactive **storefront window displays** — i.e., multiple adjoining ground-floor shopfronts with display windows, confirming a zero-lot-line, contiguous-storefront downtown block (not scattered/gapped buildings) at this population size.

## Residential pattern
At 3.36 sq mi for 3,494 people, New Hampton is the second-largest by land area in this sample (after Hoopeston) — consistent with being a county seat with a proportionally larger administrative/institutional footprint (courthouse complex, county offices, schools) alongside standard residential grid. ## Traffic/zoning
County-seat administrative functions (courthouse, county offices) plus dual school systems: the public **New Hampton Community School District** and **St. Joseph's Catholic School** (parochial) — i.e., at the ~3,500-population tier, a town can support *two* separate school systems (public + parochial) rather than losing its school to consolidation, a sharp contrast to the sub-1,000-population villages in this sample (Elma) that lose their only school.
