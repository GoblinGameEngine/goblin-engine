# Elma, Iowa (Howard County)

Population: 505 (2020 census); peaked at 976 in 1900, and briefly reached an estimated 1,500 in the early 1900s when railroad maintenance shops were located there. Land area 1.23 sq mi, 0 sq mi water. Computed density ≈ 410/sq mi. Coordinates 43.24861°N, 92.43861°W, elevation 1,178 ft.

## Core layout
Elma is a **railroad town** (built up in the late 1880s by construction of the railroad through the area; post office opened 1886; incorporated July 11, 1891) that briefly grew much larger around a rail maintenance-shop payroll, then shrank back toward its natural small-village size once the shops left — a useful "boom infrastructure, bust back to baseline" pattern for a generator (built-out grid sized for ~1,500 now holding ~500). Expect an oversized street grid relative to current building occupancy. Core is a short Main Street/crossroads commercial strip, not a courthouse square (Howard County seat is Cresco, not Elma).

## Residential pattern
Large lots, minimal formal grid beyond a few streets, consistent with the pattern of a town whose peak-era grid outlived its peak population. Farmland reached quickly — total land area is only 1.23 sq mi, so the built edge sits within a few hundred meters of the platted boundary in any direction.

## Traffic/zoning

Notably, Elma Elementary **school closed in 2013** — the town is served by the Howard-Winneshiek Community School District, meaning local schooling has been fully consolidated away. This is a strong, sourced example for the synthesis pattern "villages under ~1,000 people frequently lose their local school to district consolidation, leaving only a church/post office/tavern as non-residential anchors."

## Water
First settlement in the area (1854) was recorded along "the little Wapsipinicon creek" — a tributary of the Wapsipinicon River — predating the railroad-driven town platting by three decades. So Elma has a genuine early water-based founding (a creekside settlement) that was later superseded/absorbed by the railroad town platted around 1886-1891. Current Census water area is 0 sq mi, meaning the built core itself doesn't front the creek closely — consistent with the common pattern of the *rail-era* town core sitting back from the water that the *original* settlement was sited on.
