# Manitowoc, Wisconsin (pop. ~31,895, 2020 census)

Manitowoc County seat, on the Lake Michigan shoreline where the Manitowoc River enters the
lake. A shipbuilding/fishing port town, now revitalizing its river/lake frontage for
housing and recreation.

## Downtown / commercial core layout
- Eighth Street Historic District = Manitowoc's old downtown core, roughly bounded by
  Buffalo St., Eighth and Seventh Sts., Hancock St., and Tenth/Ninth/Quay Sts. — a compact
  multi-block historic commercial spine along Eighth Street, with many ground-floor
  storefronts remodeled over time but upper stories (2nd-3rd floor historic facades)
  largely intact — the classic small-Midwest-downtown pattern of "modernized storefront
  below, preserved historic upper floor above."
- Downtown is adjacent to, but historically separated from, the Manitowoc River waterfront
  by industrial/shipyard land use; recent redevelopment (new riverfront condos,
  completion of a riverwalk trail) is converting former industrial river frontage to
  residential/recreational use — same industrial-to-amenity waterfront transition pattern
  as Mankato and the CNU "Rust Belt riverfront" literature more broadly.

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- Classic Great Lakes port-city layout: downtown sits between the river mouth (historic
  shipbuilding/industrial harbor) and the open lake shoreline. The river corridor through
  town is the focus of current property-value-driving redevelopment (new $100,000+
  condominiums directly on the river), while the open lake shoreline itself is a separate
  amenity zone.

## Topography / drainage
- Flat Lake Michigan coastal plain; the Manitowoc River provides the town's primary
  internal drainage/urban waterway, distinct from the lake shoreline itself — a two-part
  water-edge pattern (river through town + open lake at the edge) shared with Fond du Lac
  and other lake-adjacent river-mouth towns in this list.
