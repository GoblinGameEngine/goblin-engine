# Jefferson City, Missouri (pop. 43,228, 2020 census)

Missouri state capital and Cole County seat, on a bluff on the south bank of the Missouri
River. A useful "civic core" example: state government dominates downtown land use in a
way ordinary county-seat towns don't quite match, but the surrounding commercial/
residential fabric is otherwise typical small-Midwest-city.

## Downtown / commercial core layout
- Missouri State Capitol Historic District: 122 contributing buildings across the central
  business district, roughly bounded by Adams, McCarthy, and Mulberry Streets and the
  Missouri River — i.e., a compact several-block CBD pinned against the river on one side
  (same "river-edge compresses the grid" pattern as Mankato).
- High Street is the primary commercial spine, a compact grid linking government buildings,
  museums, and historic neighborhoods — capital cities of this size tend to organize the
  whole downtown grid around one ceremonial/commercial axis leading to the civic building.
- Capitol itself sits on a bluff directly overlooking the river — high ground reserved for
  the most prestigious civic building, a repeating pattern (state capitols, county
  courthouses, and churches in this size tier are usually sited on the highest local
  ground available near downtown, both for prominence and flood safety).
- Architecture styles present: Classical Revival, Late Victorian, Queen Anne, Mission
  Revival, Modern Movement — district developed ~1850-1950, i.e. typical century-long
  downtown build-out for county-seat/capital towns.

## Residential zoning (numeric, from city staff analysis of proposed code amendments)
- Current (pre-amendment) code: **front setback 25 ft** in all residential and mixed-use
  districts; proposed amendment reduces it to **20 ft**.
  - **Side setback (single-family/two-unit): current 10 ft, proposed reduced to 6 ft.**
  - The city's own stated rationale for reducing both: existing setbacks were producing
    unbuildable or awkward lots on the older, narrower platted lots near downtown,
    discouraging infill — i.e. **older pre-WWII lot widths are frequently narrower than
    what later citywide zoning code assumed**, a mismatch worth modeling if the generator
    wants "gentle non-conformity" in older neighborhoods near downtown.

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- The Missouri River borders downtown/the Capitol district directly; historic commercial
  river frontage includes the Jefferson Landing State Historic Site (Christopher Maus
  House, Lohman Building, Union Hotel) — described as the state's oldest remaining
  Missouri River commercial district, i.e. legacy river-trade warehousing preserved as a
  heritage/museum district rather than redeveloped, sitting directly below the bluff-top
  civic core.

## Topography / drainage
- Classic river-bluff siting: flat floodplain immediately along the river (historic
  commercial/warehouse use, most flood-exposed), rising bluff behind it carrying the
  Capitol and the primary high-value civic/commercial district, with residential
  neighborhoods spreading across the upland terrain away from the river.
