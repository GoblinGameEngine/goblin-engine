# Elyria, Ohio (Lorain County — county seat)

Population: 52,656 (2020 census; down 3.4% from 54,533 in 2010; 53,117 est. 2023). Peaked at 57,538 in 1980, so roughly a 4,900-resident (-8.5%) decline over four decades. Land area 20.55 sq mi (or 19.9 sq mi per City-Data), water area 0.26 sq mi. Elevation 709 ft. Coordinates 41.37°N, 82.11°W. Located 23 miles southwest of Cleveland.

## Core layout — Moderate/large declining rust-belt archetype
Elyria is a genuine **mill-site founding**, directly parallel to the Clarksville, Iowa pattern but at a much larger scale: founded March 1817 by **Heman Ely**, who built a log house, dam, gristmill, and sawmill at **the forks of the Black River** (the East and West Branches converge in what is now downtown Elyria). The town's name blends the founder's surname with "Illyria," the classical name for the western Balkans. Elyria is the **Lorain County seat**, and downtown organizes around the county government core (Old County Courthouse, Lorain County Administration Building) sited near the historic forks — a hybrid mill-founding/courthouse-square pattern. By 1900 the town had grown to roughly 8,000 residents; it then industrialized heavily through the 20th century before the 1980 peak and subsequent rust-belt decline.

## Traffic/zoning
The **1967 opening of Midway Mall** is explicitly documented as having "changed the local economy by attracting local businesses from the town center or causing so much competition they went out of business" — a textbook mid-century retail-flight-from-downtown pattern, directly comparable to the generic small-city suburbanization story but here explicitly sourced and dated.

## Water
**Directly water-founded (mill-site).** Heman Ely's 1817 dam/gristmill/sawmill sat at the forks of the Black River; the river still runs through downtown and through **Cascade Park**, the city's largest park, which includes East Falls — a waterfall formed in a glacially-carved ravine. This is one of the strongest "founded on water for power, water remains a downtown amenity today" examples in the full research set, alongside Clarksville, IA.

## Topography/drainage
Glacially-carved ravine terrain along the Black River (Cascade Park's falls); otherwise flat-to-rolling Lake Erie coastal-plain till, typical of the region.
