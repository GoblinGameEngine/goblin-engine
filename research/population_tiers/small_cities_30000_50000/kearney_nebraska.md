# Kearney, Nebraska (pop. 33,790, 2020 census)

Buffalo County seat, south-central Nebraska, on the north bank of the Platte River.
Elevation 2,146 ft; total area 15.15 sq mi (14.88 land / 0.28 water). A flat-plains
example — no bluffs or hills to constrain the grid.

## Downtown / commercial core layout
- Traditional grid downtown, unconstrained by terrain (unlike the river-bluff towns in
  this set), so it grew as a regular square grid rather than an elongated river-edge grid.
- City's Unified Land Development Ordinance (ULDO, City Code Ch. 11-59) includes a
  **Traditional Neighborhood Design (TND) overlay district** with a build-to line of
  **5-20 ft from the front property line** (rather than a simple minimum setback) — a
  regulatory tool aimed at reproducing traditional Main-Street-style building placement in
  new development, useful evidence that "build-to zone" (not just minimum setback) is the
  correct model for historic-feeling downtowns.
- TND lots may shrink to 80% of the standard district's minimum width, with the saved area
  redirected to public space (squares, greenways) — a formalized lot-size/public-space
  trade-off a generator could mimic.

## Residential zoning
- Standard (non-TND) residential setbacks follow the underlying zoning district's normal
  minimums; TND allows narrower lots as above.

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- City lies along the north bank of the Platte River, a wide, shallow, braided prairie
  river; unlike the bluff towns, the Platte does not push downtown against a cliff — the
  river here reads more as a landscape/recreational edge (channel + sandbar braiding,
  wide floodplain) than a hard downtown boundary.

## Topography / drainage
- Flat central-Nebraska plain; minimal natural relief. Drainage is dominated by the wide,
  braided Platte channel and its floodplain rather than urban creeks. This is the
  "flat plains" topography archetype for the tier, contrasted with the bluff-town
  archetype (Mankato, Jefferson City, Cape Girardeau).
