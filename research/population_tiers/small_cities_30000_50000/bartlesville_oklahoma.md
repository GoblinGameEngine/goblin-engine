# Bartlesville, Oklahoma (pop. 37,290, 2020 census)

Washington County seat, northeast Oklahoma, 47 mi north of Tulsa. Non-Midwestern example
included for regional variety per the research brief — an oil-industry boom town
(Phillips Petroleum headquarters) on the Caney River, at the eastern edge of the rolling
Osage Hills.

## Downtown / commercial core layout
- Downtown Bartlesville listed as a National Register Historic District in 1991; notable
  for unusually high-value architecture for a city this size, including the **Price
  Tower** — the only realized skyscraper designed by Frank Lloyd Wright, a 19-story
  copper-and-glass tower built in 1956 amid otherwise low-rise early-20th-century
  commercial buildings. This is a useful outlier data point: a mid-century oil-boom small
  city can produce one dramatically taller "prestige" building in an otherwise
  2-4-story downtown, funded by a single dominant local corporation (Phillips
  Petroleum) rather than organic downtown growth — a plausible generator variant for
  "company towns" within this tier.
- Downtown's early growth was anchored by two features side by side: the Caney River and
  the Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railroad depot — both river and rail served as the
  original transport anchors, a common dual-anchor pattern for county-seat towns founded
  in the railroad era (as opposed to purely river-founded towns platted before railroads
  arrived).

## Residential / general zoning (numeric, from city zoning code)
- **Front and exterior side yard setback: 20 ft** from the property line abutting a
  street, reducible to **10 ft** where the lot fronts an access easement rather than a
  full street — matches the tier-wide front-setback norm closely (20-30 ft standard,
  Bartlesville at the low end).

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- The Caney River runs through town but was historically a transport/logistics resource
  tied to the oil boom rather than a scenic downtown amenity — the corporate downtown
  (dominated by Phillips 66's headquarters campus) took priority over riverfront
  amenity development for most of the 20th century, a variation on the
  industrial-legacy-waterfront pattern where the "industry" is oil-corporate rather than
  milling/shipping.

## Topography / drainage
- Sits at the eastern edge of the Osage Hills, a rolling tallgrass-prairie hill region
  (southern extension of Kansas's Flint Hills) — genuine rolling-hill topography, in
  contrast to the dead-flat plains towns (Kearney, Bowling Green) and the bluff-and-river
  towns (Jefferson City, Cape Girardeau) elsewhere in this tier. Elevation 702 ft.
