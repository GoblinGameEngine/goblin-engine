# Michigan City, Indiana (pop. 31,479, 2020 census)

LaPorte County, on the Lake Michigan shoreline in far northern Indiana (adjacent to the
Indiana Dunes). A useful Great-Lakes-shoreline example with concrete lot-width numbers
from a current (2020s) zoning-reform debate.

## Downtown / commercial core layout / residential zoning (numeric, from LaPorte
County/city Joint Zoning Ordinance and 2020s staff reform proposal)
- Older gridded neighborhoods (zoned R-1D/R-1E single-family) typically have **40-50 ft
  wide lots** — narrower than a generic modern suburban lot, consistent with pre-1940s
  Midwest platting norms.
- **Front and corner setbacks: 20 ft** under current code — city staff note this can
  render a 40-45 ft-wide lot effectively unbuildable without a variance once side
  setbacks are also applied, i.e. **older narrow lots are frequently out of compliance
  with later citywide setback rules** (same mismatch identified in Jefferson City, MO —
  a recurring, generalizable finding for this tier: "generate older near-downtown blocks
  with narrower lots than the nominal zoning minimum, to be historically consistent").
  - Reform proposal: allow staff to apply an "average-block setback" and reduced minimum
    footprint so modest infill homes can be built by-right on these narrow lots.
- **Side yard setback: 7 ft**, reducible to **0 ft** where the adjoining lot is zoned
  B1/B2 (commercial) and the shared wall is fire-rated — an explicit zero-lot-line
  allowance at the residential/commercial edge.
- A shoreline/waterfront setback (measured from the Lake Michigan shoreline) also applies
  but specific footage wasn't found in search results; expect it to be substantially
  larger than the standard lot-line setback given Indiana's Lake Michigan shoreline/dune
  protection framework.

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- The city sits directly on Lake Michigan, immediately adjacent to the Indiana Dunes —
  high-amenity lakefront land, but also constrained by dune topography and shoreline
  regulation (form-based "North End" district exists specifically to manage this
  interface). Downtown itself is set back from the immediate shoreline, with the lakefront
  zone treated as a distinct regulatory area (harbor/marina, dunes, and beach uses) rather
  than continuous downtown commercial frontage.

## Topography / drainage
- Lake Michigan shoreline with active dune topography — a topographic archetype not
  present in the river-town examples in this tier: sandy, low-relief but with distinct
  dune ridges parallel to the shore, rather than a single incised river valley.
