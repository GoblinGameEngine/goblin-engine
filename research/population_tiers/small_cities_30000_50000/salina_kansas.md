# Salina, Kansas (pop. 46,731, 2020 census)

Saline County seat, central Kansas, in the Smoky Hills region of the Great Plains, about
6 miles from the confluence of the Saline and Smoky Hill Rivers. Good example of a
"two rivers at the edge of town, not through downtown" layout.

## Downtown / commercial core layout
- Has a Downtown Design Review Board and Heritage Commission overseeing the historic core;
  the code provides for designating individual landmark properties or grouped historic
  districts.

## Residential zoning (numeric, from city zoning code)
- **Front yard setback: 25-30 ft** depending on district (two figures found: 30 ft for
  one single-family category, 25 ft for another).
- **Side yard setback: 7.5 ft** (consistent across the districts found).
- **Minimum lot area: 6,000 sq ft** for standard single-family lots (same minimum applies
  in a university-area district).
- These numbers sit near the middle of the ranges seen elsewhere in this tier: front
  setback 20-30 ft is the norm, side setback commonly 6-10 ft (Salina's 7.5 ft splits the
  difference), minimum lot size commonly 6,000-8,000 sq ft for a standard in-town
  single-family lot.

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- The Smoky Hill River runs north then northeast through the eastern part of the city; the
  Saline River flows southeast immediately north of the city; an abandoned oxbow (old
  channel of the Smoky Hill) winds through the northeast part of town. Downtown itself
  sits west of the main river, i.e. **the rivers bound the edge/east side of the built-up
  area rather than running through the commercial core** — a different pattern from the
  "river-hugging downtown" towns (Mankato, Cape Girardeau).

## Topography / drainage
- Salina sits in the Smoky Hills sub-region of the Great Plains: a "subtle mountainous or
  hilly plateau" transitioning into dissected till plains/tall-grass prairie — gentle
  relief rather than flat-as-a-table plains (Kearney) or bluff-and-river relief (Cape
  Girardeau/Jefferson City). The presence of an abandoned meander/oxbow channel northeast
  of downtown is useful evidence for a generator: **rivers near a town often carry visible
  relict channels/oxbows in adjacent low ground**, good candidates for parkland or
  floodplain green space rather than buildable lots.
