# Cape Girardeau, Missouri (pop. 39,540, 2020 census)

Mississippi River port city in southeast Missouri. One of the clearest "industrial
river-trade downtown behind a flood levee" examples in this tier.

## Downtown / commercial core layout
- Historic downtown built by steamboat-trade merchants: Italianate and Greek Revival brick
  commercial buildings, still substantially intact today (unlike many peers that lost
  their downtown to urban renewal).
- Downtown is physically separated from the river by an engineered **floodwall/levee
  system**: 8,460 ft of levee overall, with a 4,000 ft concrete floodwall section 6-16 ft
  tall, completed 1964, designed to hold back a 54 ft river stage. Five closure gates and
  two pump stations (Mill St., Merriwether St.) let Main Street cross the wall in normal
  conditions.
- The floodwall itself has become a public amenity/canvas: the Mississippi River Tales
  Mural covers ~18,000 sq ft across 24 panels on the downtown floodwall, spanning the
  length of the downtown shopping district — i.e. a flood-control structure was
  reprogrammed as streetscape/tourism infrastructure once its industrial-defense role
  became less central to downtown identity.
- Regulatory pattern: because downtown sits behind a flood-control structure, the city
  actively manages/monitors river stage relative to downtown grade (pump stations manned
  continuously once the river nears 32 ft flood stage), meaning the historic commercial
  core is understood to be below flood elevation without the wall — a strong "downtown
  built directly on the floodplain, engineering keeps it dry" pattern.

## Residential zoning
- Districts include R-3 (High Density Single Family), R-4 (Medium Density Multi-family),
  R-5 (High Density Multi-family).
- Side yard setbacks vary by district: roughly **15 ft in some districts, 20 ft in
  others** for single-family — noticeably more generous than the Jefferson City/Salina
  norm of 6-10 ft, suggesting real variation exists city-to-city even within this
  population tier; a generator could randomize side setback in a 6-20 ft band and stay
  plausible.

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- Downtown/CBD sits directly on the river, walled off by engineered flood infrastructure
  rather than pushed away from the water — contrasts with cities that relocated commerce
  inland after repeated flooding. The legacy brick warehouse/storefront core survived
  largely intact, now serving tourism/hospitality rather than active river freight.

## Topography / drainage
- The city sits where the river cuts against a line of bluffs/hills (the "Cape" in the
  name refers to a rocky headland) — unlike the dead-flat prairie towns in this list,
  Cape Girardeau has real local relief near the river, with downtown occupying the low
  flat land right at the water's edge below the bluffs, and residential growth spreading
  onto the higher ground away from the river.
