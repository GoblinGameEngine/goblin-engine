# Fond du Lac, Wisconsin (pop. ~43,021, 2020 census)

Fond du Lac County seat, at the southern tip ("bottom") of Lake Winnebago — the largest
lake entirely within Wisconsin (137,700 acres, ~30 mi x 10 mi, avg. depth 15.5 ft, max
21 ft). The Fond du Lac River's east and west branches join in downtown, less than a mile
before emptying into the lake. A clean "town at the mouth of a river, at the foot of a
lake" archetype.

## Downtown / commercial core layout
- Streets spread out from the lake shoreline into surrounding farmland — i.e. downtown
  sits directly at the water's edge with the grid radiating/extending inland from it,
  rather than the lake being peripheral to the built-up area.
- Downtown straddles the point where the Fond du Lac River's two branches converge, just
  before the lake — meaning downtown likely has (or historically had) two river crossings
  close together near its core, a distinctive layout feature a generator could reproduce
  for "confluence downtowns."

## Residential zoning (numeric, from city ordinance)
- **Front yard setback: minimum 30 ft** from the public right-of-way/private roadway in
  residential districts — matches the higher end of the tier-wide front-setback range.
  - Waterfront (lake/river-adjacent) structures: **minimum 5 ft from rear or side lot
    line** in the shoreland-adjacent case found — notably permissive compared to inland
    setbacks, though county shoreland zoning (Ch. 44) separately regulates the shoreline
    buffer itself (setback from the water's edge, distinct from lot-line setbacks).

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- Because downtown sits directly at the confluence/lake mouth, the historic economic
  rationale (river power, lake shipping) placed commerce and industry directly on the
  water; contemporary shoreland zoning (county Ch. 44, established 1971) now regulates
  building setbacks from the navigable water's edge separately from the city's general lot
  setbacks, reflecting the later-20th-century shift toward protecting/limiting shoreline
  development even in built-up areas.

## Topography / drainage
- Flat lake-plain terrain typical of the Fox River/Winnebago basin; the Fond du Lac
  River's east and west branches drain farmland to the west and converge in the city
  before reaching the lake — a converging dual-tributary drainage pattern feeding a large
  lake, distinct from the single-river-through-town pattern seen in most of this tier's
  other examples.
