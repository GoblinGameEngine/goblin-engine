# Bowling Green, Ohio (pop. 30,808, 2020 census)

Wood County seat, northwest Ohio, home to Bowling Green State University. Notable as the
only major settlement located in the literal heart (rather than the edge) of the former
Great Black Swamp region — the flattest, most artificially-drained example in this tier.

## Downtown / commercial core layout
- Zoning Code Chapter 150 includes a Central Business (CB) district with its own building
  standards distinct from general commercial districts (referenced "Table 20" governs
  building/lot standards by district, per the code index — full numeric table not
  extracted from search, but its existence confirms the same downtown-core-vs-general-
  commercial two-tier zoning pattern seen across this dataset).
- Bowling Green Historic District exists as a distinct National-Register-listed area
  separate from the CBD zoning, i.e. a residential/mixed heritage district rather than the
  commercial core itself.

## Water proximity / property value pattern
- Bowling Green is NOT built directly on a significant river the way most of this tier's
  other examples are — it sits on flat farmland with only minor tributary streams/ditches
  feeding the Portage River system, itself one of the historic Great Black Swamp
  drainage rivers. This makes Bowling Green a useful **"landlocked/flat farm town" control
  case**: no waterfront commercial premium, no flood-prone downtown-on-the-river pattern —
  downtown's siting logic here is purely rail/road/county-government-driven rather than
  water-transport-driven.

## Topography / drainage
- Defining feature: Bowling Green sits in the literal historic heart of the Great Black
  Swamp, a ~1,500 sq mi poorly-drained glacial lake-plain that was legislatively mandated
  for drainage starting in 1850 (drained into the Portage and Maumee rivers via a dense
  network of engineered ditches and tributary streams). The result is dead-flat terrain
  and an artificial, engineered ditch/tributary drainage network rather than natural creek
  patterns — the most extreme "flat, engineered-drainage" example in this tier, useful as
  a generator baseline for non-water-adjacent farm-belt towns.
